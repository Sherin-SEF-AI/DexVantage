Camera, depth and teleop planning · robot-learning data collection

See the grasp before you build the rig.

DexVantage plans the cameras, depth sensors and teleoperation of robot data-collection rigs for ACT, Diffusion Policy and VLA models such as OpenVLA, π0 and GR00T. Place the wrist, head, overhead and third-person cameras on the robot and see, before anything is bought or drilled, whether the fingertips, the object and the contact point are visible at the moment of grasp; whether depth is valid where the policy needs it; whether the teleoperator reaches what the follower must; whether the cables, the USB host and the clocks keep up. Then check the recorded datasets against the plan, and export the same cameras to MuJoCo, Isaac Lab, LeRobot and ROS 2.

From the robot model to an audited dataset

One tool from the first camera to the last episode.

What a policy can learn is decided when the cameras are bolted on: a wrist camera that loses the far fingertip at contact, an overhead camera inside the depth sensor's minimum range, a USB controller that drops frames at 30 fps. DexVantage answers those questions on the computer, then checks the recordings against the plan. The guide walks through all of it on the samples.

Import the robot

URDF, MJCF, USD or a MuJoCo Menagerie model with its own licence shown. Joint sliders, a pose scrubber over trajectories, IK drag and the gripper and fingertip taggers.

Place the cameras

Cameras, depth cameras and tactile sensors from a datasheet library on any link, a tripod or the cage; each lens model posed with the robot, its image in the Camera view.

Run the studies

Coverage, depth validity, grasp self-occlusion, VLA tokens, multi-finger occlusion, teleop reach, lighting, motion blur, sync, bandwidth, cables and brackets, checked against your requirements and against MuJoCo renders.

Calibrate and audit

Plan the hand-eye calibration, solve it, check the mount from the robot's silhouette, audit LeRobot, RLDS and ROS 2 recordings against the plan, and export the same cameras to simulation and training.

Features

Know what the policy will see, before anything is drilled.

The screenshots on this page were taken from the application on its bundled samples. Every figure named here is measured; the validation section says against what.

The hero study

Which grasps lose a fingertip, and why

A grasp set (sampled over the task volume, imported, or taken from a trajectory or a recorded dataset) is solved by batched IK. For every reachable grasp, DexVantage renders cube maps from each camera against the posed robot and asks whether each fingertip's contact patch, and the object's grasp region, is visible at the moment of contact and just before it.

  • Contact Visibility Score: the share of reachable grasps that meet your requirement, with a bootstrap interval and Wilson's interval.
  • Per camera: fingertips seen, outside the image, behind the robot's own finger, hidden by the scene.
  • What to move: one camera at a time, the move or turn that raises the score, with Apply, Preview and Explain.
  • 2 000 grasps on the UR5e sample in 114.2 s on an RTX 5080 through EGL; on ALOHA 2 182.1 s, over this project's 180 s default (performance, 2026-11-08).
The model, its validation and its limits
Coverage and depth

Where depth is valid, and where it is not

The task volume is sampled on sparse voxels. For each depth sensor DexVantage says, voxel by voxel, the first condition that fails: outside a field of view, closer than the minimum range at the chosen resolution, beyond the maximum range, occluded for one imager (finger shadows, stereo parallax), outside the projector cone, or a material warning.

  • Minimum range and field of view from the maker's tables; Intel's D400 tables are reproduced with 0 figures off.
  • Depth noise σ_z from the disparity error, checked against a Monte Carlo of triangulated matches.
  • Coverage and resolution: cameras that see each voxel, px/mm, viewing angle, defocus, redundancy.

VLA inputs

How many ViT tokens land on the object and the fingertips

A policy's crop and resize rule and its patch grid come from its model card or paper. DexVantage maps each camera's image through that rule and counts the tokens each object and fingertip covers at each pose, and warns when a crop cuts the task away. The patch grid is drawn in the Camera view.

  • Token counts per object and fingertip, the fewest over the grasp set, and what the crop leaves out.
  • Plücker ray maps per input pixel in the robot base frame, exported as .npy or .safetensors.
  • Inference times and input sizes are cited, never constants in the code.
VLA token resolution
Dexterous hands

Which finger hides which contact patch from which camera

For multi-finger hands, DexVantage sweeps grasp postures and builds the finger × camera matrix: the share of each fingertip's contact patch a camera does not see, and what hides most of it (another finger, the palm, the arm, or the image edge). It compares palm micro, dorsal wrist and head cameras and says what to move.

  • Visual-to-tactile handoff: the sensory blind gap, the distance before contact over which a fingertip's contact is neither seen by a camera nor touched by a tactile pad.
  • Palm and dorsal micro-camera mounts as printable sleeves.
Multi-finger occlusion
Hardware engineering

Bandwidth, power, cables and brackets, checked on the model

Every stream's data rate on the wire, each USB controller's and GMSL link's load against its capacity, the disk budget and bus power. Cables are routed along the links and checked for slack, bend radius and snags over the joint range. Brackets come out as watertight STL ready to print.

  • Host topology: hosts, controllers, hubs and capture cards from the library, every figure with its source.
  • Cable flex across joints, with a harness sheet.
  • Brackets: ISO 9409-1 flange adapters, T-slot clamps, pan-tilt mounts, palm and dorsal sleeves, with a clearance check against the robot.
Bandwidth, hosts and power
Teleop, light and time

What the operator can reach, what the light does, and when the frames were taken

Leader arms, GELLO, VR and space-mouse devices are mapped onto the follower: where the task volume can be teleoperated, where the follower meets a singularity, a joint limit or a self-collision, and the bimanual handover. Lights and materials give glare and IR wash-out per camera; motion blur, rolling shutter, time sync and action-chunk latency follow the robot's own motion.

  • Dead zones named with their pose; Apply moves the base.
  • Lighting: GGX materials, area lights, a glare map in the Camera view.
  • Timing: smear in pixels, rolling-shutter skew, camera-to-joint offsets, the air-grasp of a late action chunk.
Teleop overlap and dead zones
Calibration

A hand-eye calibration you can plan, and check without a board

ChArUco and AprilGrid detection, AX = XB and AX = ZB solvers with a joint bundle adjustment, and a pose planner that predicts each axis's 1σ before you move the robot. A coach suggests the next pose. Later, the robot's own silhouette checks whether a camera has slipped on its mount.

  • Predicted σ per axis at the planned poses, against your requirement.
  • Targetless silhouette check from recorded episodes.
  • Captures from recorded datasets as well as live.
Hand-eye calibration
Simulation

MuJoCo renders against every prediction

The project becomes a MuJoCo scene; every camera renders through its own lens model; the rendered visibility and depth are compared with the studies' predictions, point by point, and each disagreement is explained (a segmentation edge, a rule, the occlusion test). Synthetic LeRobot v2.1 and v3.0 episodes with known faults test the auditor.

  • On the UR5e sample the render agrees with the grasp study on 92.8 % of the contact points in view, and with the depth study on 99.8 % of the voxels.
  • Save the MuJoCo scene to run it yourself.
Ground truth, measured
Data

Recorded datasets, audited against the plan

LeRobot (v2.1 and v3.0, local or streamed from the Hub), RLDS / Open X-Embodiment and ROS 2 bags (MCAP and SQLite) are read with their cameras and joint states. The auditor checks every episode for dropped and duplicated frames, jitter, USB resets, camera-to-joint offsets, clock drift, exposure steps and motion blur, and says which episodes to drop or record again.

  • Plan against recording: does the recorded rig match the planned one, and has a camera slipped?
  • FiftyOne tags for the episodes and frames that fail.
The dataset auditor
Requirements and design

Signed off against requirements, robust to the mount

Requirements name figures the studies compute; the readiness scorecard runs them as a CI gate (exit 0, 1 or 2). Monte Carlo tolerances say how far a mount may be off; designed experiments and Sobol indices say what drives each figure; an NSGA-II optimiser and a design agent propose better rigs on a Pareto front.

  • Variants side by side, diff and merge of projects, a Git-friendly folder form.
  • Perturbation export for augmentation and Isaac randomisation.
The optimiser and design agent
Research tools

From a rig to a paper: runs, statistics, campaigns and the sim-to-real gap

An experiment tracker keeps every run with its settings, figures and provenance (CSV, Parquet, MLflow and W&B exports). Statistics and power analysis compare policies' success rates; the campaign planner schedules operators and sessions in Williams squares with storage and a calendar; the sim-to-real gap ranks what explains the difference between recorded and rendered frames; the publication kit writes methods, tables, figures and BibTeX with every number traced.

  • Cluster sweeps on a process pool or Slurm, gathered to the same result as a serial run.
  • Multi-cell comparison of several robot cells side by side.
Statistics and power analysis
Export and automation

The same cameras in simulation, training and the robot

Exports to MJCF, URDF and tf, OpenCV and Kalibr, Isaac Lab and USD, LeRobot and policy configs, masks, Plücker maps, brackets, perturbations, the canonical view warp, the Rerun viewer and an HTML or PDF report. Each export carries a provenance block and is read back by an independent reader in the validation suite.

  • Batch mode: every study has a flag, JSON out, exit codes for CI.
  • Python API (import dexvantage as dv) and a plugin SDK.
  • Assistant and MCP: an Assistant dock (Claude or Groq, with your own key) and an MCP server for MCP clients; proposals run only once you approve them.

Samples

Six robot cells to start from.

Each opens with dexvantage --sample <id> or File ▸ Open sample. The robots are MuJoCo Menagerie models bundled after their licences were read; cameras sit where a project publishes them and are labelled sample placements otherwise. Each has a tour in the guide.

ALOHA 2 · aloha

Two ViperX 300 arms (BSD-3-Clause), the published cam_high, cam_low and wrist cameras, tabletop and handover volumes, ACT and two leader arms.

SO-100 · so100

The LeRobot SO-ARM100 (Apache-2.0) with a wrist and a third-person camera, top-down pinches, SmolVLA and its leader arm.

UR5e + Robotiq 2F-85 · ur5e

A wrist RealSense D405 and an overhead D435, 200 tabletop grasps, π0 and a scripted pick trajectory.

UR5e + Shadow Hand · dexhand

The right Shadow Hand on the flange with a palm micro camera, a dorsal wrist camera and a head camera, for the multi-finger study.

Bimanual Panda · panda-bimanual

Two Franka Pandas (Apache-2.0) in one cell with wrist D405s and a front D435. There is no official bimanual Franka model: this is two instances of the single-arm one.

Unitree G1 upper body · g1

Arms and three-finger hands (BSD-3-Clause, meshes simplified to within 0.5 mm), the head D435 where Unitree's URDF puts it, GR00T N1.5.

Known limits

What it does not do yet.

Each model's document has its own known limits. The main ones:

Not run against the real programs

Isaac Lab and Isaac Sim, LeRobot's own loader and trainer, ACT, Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA, Kalibr and FiftyOne were not installed while building: the exports follow the cited formats and are read back by independent readers.

Hardware figures

Most makers state no lens distortion, readout time, sensitivity or processing latency, so those figures are bounds until you enter measured values. A figure the maker does not state shows as "not stated".

Grasps and hands

The fingers stay at their home opening, obstacles are not checked along the approach, and dexterous-hand postures are sample sweeps, not measured synergies.

Ground truth and calibration

MuJoCo renders agree with the predictions on 92.8 % (UR5e) and 84.2 % (SO-100) of the contact points in view; distorted lenses render slowly. End to end from rendered images, the hand-eye error is about 2–2.5 × the predicted σ: treat σ as a lower bound.

Speed off Linux

The GPU cube maps need Linux EGL; elsewhere the occlusion studies use the NumPy rasteriser, 15–270 × slower per cube map on the samples. The QPainter 3D fallback takes 160–586 ms a frame.

Release and platforms

Version 0.1.0rc1 is the first release candidate; nothing has been tagged or released. Built and tested on Linux x86_64 with Python 3.14 only: the Windows, macOS and arm64 bundles are built by CI, which has not run yet.

Install DexVantage.

DexVantage is free software under the GPL-3.0-or-later. The bundles below carry their own Python and need nothing else; they will be available from the release page once released, with SHA-256 checksums in SHA256SUMS.txt. Until then, install from source with Python 3.10 or later. The bundles are not code-signed, so the first start asks you to confirm.
