Requirements, metrics and the readiness scorecard

Modules: dexvantage/core/metric_defs.py (the named figures of every study), dexvantage/core/requirements.py (requirements, the scorecard, the variants compare), dexvantage/core/studies/requirements.py (studies requirements, batch --scorecard OUT.json, and variants_compare, batch --variants-compare OUT.json), dexvantage/core/studies/rig_inventory.py (study rig, batch --rig-inventory OUT.json), dexvantage/core/reproduce.py (--reproduce), dexvantage/core/run_plan.py (--run-plan), dexvantage/batch_gate.py (the batch options). Page: dexvantage/ui/pages/requirements.py (Analysis ▸ Requirements and variants; Review workspace). Checks: dexvantage/validation/checks_requirements.py (model requirements). Tests: tests/test_metric_defs.py, tests/test_requirements.py, tests/test_requirements_batch.py, tests/test_requirements_page.py. Spec §7.11, §11 and §13 ("Requirements and Variants compare").

Ported from Vantage core/requirements.py: the signed margin, the verdict over the items, the scorecard document and the CI gate. Stripped: Vantage's automotive metric registry (sectors, object classes, lidar, GNSS and power figures), the must/should priorities, the brief parser and the design-review PDF.

Metrics

A metric is one number a study's result already holds, with its unit and the direction that is better (core/metrics.py, the shared contract). core/metric_defs.py registers one for every study's headline figure (tag headline) and the obvious secondary figures. Nothing is computed there beyond unit changes (fractions to %, metres to mm, seconds to ms) and sums or extremes over rows the result lists.

A figure the result does not state reads None with status not stated and, where the study says why, its sentence (reason). A figure the study states only as a bound carries bound:

  • at_least: the time-sync skew and action-label error when some clock or exposure figure is not stated (the study's "≥"), the controllers over budget and the total payload when a stream has no stated rate, the cable pulls and snags when a cable's length or bend radius is not stated, the blind gap when a finger is blind from the start of the scan;
  • at_most: the motion smear and rolling-shutter skew over the maximum exposure or readout the stream allows.

The contract change (additive): an extractor may return {'value', 'bound', 'reason', 'note'} instead of a number; metrics.read(metric, doc) turns any extractor's output into a row, and a result shaped otherwise than the extractor expects reads not stated instead of raising.

Requirements

A requirement is a metric, a comparison (>=, <=, <, >, ==) and a threshold in the metric's unit, with the study that measures it (core.project.Requirement: uid, name, metric, op, threshold, unit, study, is_default, note; the project file's requirements section, unchanged). A requirements file (YAML or JSON) is

format: dexvantage.requirements
version: 1
requirements:
- {name: Contact visibility, metric: grasp.contact_visibility, comparison: '>=', threshold: 95, unit: '%',
   study: grasp_visibility, default: true}

comparison also reads op and words (at least, less than, ≥ …). A unit or study that is not the metric's is an error, not a conversion: a threshold in the wrong unit would be silently wrong.

Defaults (labelled defaults, is_default true, note "a documented starting point, not a standard"; docs/examples/requirements.yaml; --check defaults): contact visibility ≥ 95 %; camera to joint-state skew < 2 ms; controllers over budget ≤ 0; cable pulls and snags ≤ 0; sensory blind gap before contact ≤ 0 mm. Editing a default's threshold or comparison on the page makes it the user's own figure (no longer labelled a default).

The scorecard

For each requirement, with value v, threshold t and the metric's figure as the study states it:

margin = v − t      for ≥ and >          pass when margin ≥ −ε (≥) or margin > ε (>)
margin = t − v      for ≤ and <          pass when margin ≥ −ε (≤) or margin > ε (<)
margin = −|v − t|   for ==               pass when margin ≥ −ε,       ε = 1e-9 · max(1, |t|)

Positive margins are good. With a bound the comparison on v decides only in the bound's direction: at_least passes ≥/> and fails ≤/< (and == when v > t); at_most the reverse; anything else is not evaluated, with the sentence "The study states only that the figure is at least v, which does not settle less than t: give the figures it lacks." A study that refuses gives "not evaluated" with its own sentence; a study that crashes gives an error.

Verdict: pass ("ready") when every item passes; fail ("not ready") when one fails; else incomplete ("not evaluated"); empty ("no requirements"). Each study runs at most once per scorecard (a shared result cache of 24 results, keyed by the project's content, the study's settings, the library entries, the active variant and the dataset fingerprint).

The CI gate dexvantage PROJECT --check requirements.yaml [OUT.json] prints the table, writes the scorecard JSON with its provenance (default scorecard.json) and exits 0 when every requirement passes, 1 when one fails or is not evaluated (a gate cannot certify what it did not measure) and 2 on an error (a file that cannot be read, invalid YAML, an unknown metric, a threshold that is not a number, a wrong unit, no project, a study that crashed).

Variants compare

Every variant of the project against every requirement (the scorecard per variant, each study once per variant): a cell per variant and requirement (value, bound, status, margin, reason), the best variant per requirement (by the metric's better direction; for == the smallest distance to the threshold; ties are all named; a cell without a value is never best), and a ranking by requirements passed, then fewest failures. The page's Apply makes the top-ranked variant active when it passes more (one undo step); Preview draws its sensors next to the active ones; New variant from this one copies the active variant (its sensors keep their uids, so host links, cables and policy cameras apply to the copy) and makes it active (one undo step).

Reproduce and plans

--reproduce RESULT.json re-runs what wrote a result (the study in its provenance; a scorecard with the requirements recorded in it; a run-plan summary step by step with each step's settings) on the project given, else the sample its command names (--sample NAME), else the project file it names next to the result or in the current folder, and compares every number and text (core/provenance.py compare), exactly or within --tolerance. Computation-time blocks that are not under a seconds key (timings, throughput, timing, timing_s) may differ and are counted as such. It prints the verdict, the reasons and each mismatch (path, recorded, reproduced) and with --reproduce-out REPORT.json writes the full report. Exit codes as --check: 0 reproduces, 1 does not, 2 cannot be tried.

--run-plan plan.json [OUT_DIR] runs a list of steps in order (study with settings and out; check with a requirements file, defaults or inline items; export with formats and options; reproduce with tolerance), each on a snapshot with that step's settings only, every result stamped with the plan and the step in its provenance; one summary (plan-summary.json, schema dexvantage.run_plan/1) lists each step's status, output, result SHA-256, the headline metrics of a study and the verdict of a check. Exit 2 when a step could not run, else 1 when a check failed or a reproduction did not match, else 0; a step that cannot run is recorded with its sentence and the plan carries on.

Known limits

  • The scorecard evaluates the active variant with each study's stored settings; the studies' own Known limits apply unchanged (contact visibility is the grasp study's "every fingertip at least 50 % visible in at least one camera"; the bandwidth margin is the specification's capacity bound minus the computed load, and so on).
  • A bound decides only in its direction; the multi-finger blind gap is scanned at the study's refine step (2 mm by default), so a gap shorter than a step reads 0.
  • There are no priorities: every requirement is decisive. Thresholds compare with the study's rounded figure.
  • The variants compare runs every study each variant needs (the grasp study takes its time per variant).
  • rig.cost, rig.mass_g and rig.power_w count the enabled sensors only (no hosts, cables, lights, brackets); the cost is not stated until a library entry states a price.
  • --reproduce uses the project's settings as they are now (a change shows as "the project differs"); dataset studies need --dataset as when they ran; GPU renders may differ in their last bits between drivers.
