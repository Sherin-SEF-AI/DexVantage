The hardware library: devices from datasheets

Module: dexvantage/core/library/__init__.py. Schema: dexvantage/resources/library.schema.json (JSON Schema draft 2020-12). Data: dexvantage/resources/library/<kind>/<id>.json. Tests: tests/test_library.py. The lens models the library builds are in dexvantage/core/optics (see Optics: lens models, pixel density and defocus).

What an entry is

One JSON file per device, in the folder of its kind (depth, camera, tactile, host, policy, cable, light, robot, teleop), named after its id. Required: id, kind, maker, name, sources (at least one, each with url and the date read) and note. The rest, each a figure or null:

Block What it holds
top level family, interface, power_w, mass_g, size_mm ([x, y, z] in the device body frame: x the maker's depth or thickness, y the width, z the height)
imagers[] name, role (ir_left, ir_right, rgb, mono, tof), pose + pose_stated, shutter, hfov_deg, vfov_deg, dfov_deg, pixel_um, resolution (native, the one the FOV is for), lens_model, f_number, focus; optional fov_modes (FOVs stated for other stream shapes, such as a depth FOV for 16:9 and 4:3 streams); optional, only where the source states them: focal_length_mm, sensor_size_mm ([w, h] active area), sensor_diagonal_mm, optical_format (the nominal type as written, e.g. 1/2.6", never checked as a size) and figures_conflict (the maker's own figures for the imager disagree: which, and which one the entry uses)
projector hfov_deg, vfov_deg, dfov_deg, pose, pose_stated
depth technology, reference_imager, stereo_pair, baseline_m, min_z (the table as stated: width, height, metres), max_range_m, rms_error (text as stated, metric: rms, median_abs, std_dev or unspecified, pct, at_m), subpixel_px, depth_fov; the Intel entries add range_text (the range line as written) and depth_start_mm (the depth zero plane behind the front glass)
streams[] imager (depth, rgb or an imager's name), width, height, fps, format, bits_per_pixel
tactile pad_mm, camera_resolution, fps, sensing, taxels; optional area_mm2 (a curved pad whose source states only an area)
host type, ports, lanes, stated_mbps, practical_mbps (only when a source states it), gmsl_links; optional ingest[] (each way cameras reach the host as stated: bus usb3/usb2/pcie/csi/gmsl/ethernet/other, count, per_port_mbps, aggregate_mbps, practical_mbps per port, practical_aggregate_mbps, what the source's words; nothing derived)
cable type, diameter_mm, min_bend_static_mm, min_bend_dynamic_mm, flex_cycles, lengths_m, connectors, active; optional stated_mbps, drag_chain_rated, robotics_rated (true / false as the maker says, null when not stated)
light type, luminous_flux_lm, luminance_cd_m2, cct_k, cct_k_range, emitting_size_m, ring_inner_m, beam_deg, ir_irradiance_w_m2, ir_at_m, what; optional cri, illuminance_lux at illuminance_at_m (on-axis, as stated), wavelength_nm (IR illuminators)
policy each field {value, source, quote} (docs/models/policies.md)
teleop device_kind (leader_arm, gello, vr, spacemouse, glove, hand_tracker), mapping (joint, cartesian), for arms leads and same_joints; each figure {value, source, quote}: dof, tracking_hz, latency_ms, workspace (a study volume, or null), range_text, reach_mm, accuracy_mm, position_resolution_mm, joint_resolution_deg, feedback (Teleop overlap and follower dexterity)

Frames. A pose is the imager's (or projector's) optical frame (z forward, x right, y down) in the device body frame, which is REP-103 (x forward along the optical axis, y left, z up) with its origin at the optical centre of the reference (depth) imager. A sensor's mount (core/project.py Mount) places that body frame. For a stereo camera the right imager is one baseline along −y.

Where the figures come from

Every number is copied from a cited source: the maker's datasheet or product page, with its URL, title, the date it was read and which fields it covers. The source is read when the entry is written; nothing is filled from memory. When a page cannot be fetched the figures stay null and the note says so. The six Intel RealSense entries (D405, D415, D435, D435i, D455, D457) come from:

  • RealSense Product Family D400 Series Datasheet, Revision 023, March 2026, document 337029-017 (the former Intel RealSense D400 datasheet; the product line is now RealSense, Inc.'s, under licence from Intel), read 2026-11-06: baselines and FOVs (Tables 3-11, 3-49), imager, projector and colour-sensor properties (Tables 3-12 to 3-20), sizes and masses (3-51 to 3-54), stream modes (4-2 to 4-4), min-Z (4-12), depth quality (4-15), depth start point (4-17), depth origin (4.8.1), power (7-7 to 7-10).
  • RealSense Product Family D457 Datasheet, Revision 006, August 2025, document 337029-011, read 2026-11-06.
  • The RealSense store product pages (store.realsenseai.com/buy-realsense-depth-camera-<model>.html), read 2026-11-06: the RGB shutter of the D435/D435i (the datasheet gives none for the RGB camera outside the module) and the connectors; elsewhere a cross-check.

Where two sources disagree the entry uses the datasheet and the note gives both (examples: the D405 depth FOV is 84° × 58° in the datasheet and 87° × 58° on the store page; the D405 connector is "Micro-B" in the datasheet and "USB-C" on the store page; the D415 projector FOV is 67/41/75° in the SKU table and 64/41/72° in the projector table).

Hosts. A host's ingest is modelled only from what its source states: ports and stated_mbps are the USB 3.2 ports whose generation is stated and the USB-IF per-port rate of that generation (the Thor developer kit's USB-C ports say only "3.1", so they are listed in ingest without a rate; the Thor modules say "3x USB 3.2" with no generation, so their stated_mbps is null), lanes the MIPI CSI-2 lanes, and ingest lists every way in with the source's words. Two sources state a sustained figure (practical_mbps): Basler's card, "Up to 380 MBps per port reliably supported" and "Up to 1.52 GBps total" (3 040 and 12 160 Mbit/s, decimal megabytes × 8), and Leopard's grabber, "PCIe X8 Gen3 (64 Gbit/s, ~51 Gbit/s netto)". No GMSL2 link rate is entered for a deserializer board: none of the boards' pages states one. Desktop GPUs (RTX 4090/5080/5090) are not host entries: their stated figures (cores, memory, PCIe to the CPU) are not camera-ingest figures, and the USB controllers of the machine they sit in are the motherboard's; a desktop host is a USB 3.2 reference entry (or a capture card) with your machine's measured figure entered as a custom device. The GPUs appear where they matter, as the hardware of a policy's stated inference time.

Policies. As Policies: what a robot-learning policy takes from each camera: every field {value, source, quote}, the inference time only where the paper or code states it, with the GPU named (π0.5, the SmolVLA fine-tunes, the Push-T diffusion policy and LeRobot's ACT state none). Variants are separate entries because their figures differ: the OpenVLA-OFT LIBERO recipe (2 images, chunk 8, 112 ms on an A100) and the ALOHA OFT+ recipe (3 images, chunk 25, 321 ms, 25 Hz); SmolVLA's LIBERO (2 cameras) and Meta-World (1 camera) checkpoints; Diffusion Policy's simulated Push-T (96 → 84 px, one camera) beside its real-world setting; LeRobot's ACT defaults (whole chunk executed, no ensembling) beside the paper's ACT.

Lights. Makers of video lights state an on-axis illuminance at a distance ("5500lux at 0.5m", "6,300 lux @ 1m"), a CRI and a beam angle, rarely a luminous flux; the illuminance is entered as stated (illuminance_lux, illuminance_at_m) and not turned into a flux or an intensity, so the lighting study, which needs a flux or a luminance, says those lights' output is not stated until one is entered. The AXIS T90D20 states its wavelength (850 nm), beam angles and an illumination range for cameras (144 m with the 10° lens), not an irradiance.

Cables. Basler states plainly which of its cables suit drag chains and robots (drag_chain_rated, robotics_rated); the Micro-B/A P cable is "fixed installation only" (no moving radius, no flex cycles), which the cables study then flags on a moving joint. The Stereolabs datasheet's "Bending radius (loaded) 66 mm" is entered as the moving radius, an interpretation the note says.

Teleop devices (Teleop overlap and follower dexterity): no maker states a tracking volume as a box or cylinder, so every entry's workspace is null and says why; latencies are what each maker calls latency (MANUS "Signal latency ≤ 7.5ms", Apple's "12‑millisecond photon‑to‑photon latency" of the passthrough); display refresh rates are not entered as tracking rates.

Gaps policy

  • A figure the source does not state is null, shown as "—" with the tooltip "not stated by the maker" (library.NOT_STATED). No figure is derived from another to fill a gap in the file (pixel size from focal length and FOV, say).
  • An assumption the model needs is labelled where it is made: an RGB camera whose offset the maker does not state is placed at the depth origin with pose_stated: false, and the note says "ASSUMPTION"; the Intel projectors are placed at the baseline midpoint the same way. The UI and the studies read pose_stated and say so.
  • Intel publishes no lens distortion coefficients ("Distortion <= 1.5%"), so the built-in imagers are pinhole models fitted to the stated FOV (lens_model: "pinhole", an assumption the note states). A unit's calibration replaces it.

From a sensor to cameras

library.sensor_imagers(sensor, mode=None) returns [Imager], each a 4-tuple (name, role, pose_in_body, camera) (frames.Pose, optics.CameraModel) with the attributes label (what the model is and where its figures come from), is_default, pose_stated, calibrated and mode.

  1. Which stream. mode=(w, h) applies to every imager that streams that size; the others, and mode=None, use the imager's largest listed stream (then its native resolution). mode={'RGB': (1280, 720)} picks per imager.
  2. Which FOV. A FOV the maker states for that stream shape (fov_modes, e.g. Intel's depth FOV for HD 16:9 and VGA 4:3) comes first; then the imager's own FOV when the stream has the native aspect ratio (scaled). Otherwise the FOV is derived from the native one by a centred crop at the same focal length scaled to fit (an assumption the label says). The camera is CameraModel.from_fov(w, h, hfov, vfov, model): both stated angles are matched, so the pixels need not be square.
  3. Calibration. sensor.calibration overrides per imager: {'imagers': {'IR left': {'K': 3 × 3, 'D': [...], 'width': w, 'height': h, 'model': 'brown' | 'kb' | 'pinhole' | ..., 'pose': {'position', 'quaternion'}}}}; a flat {'K', 'D', 'width', 'height', 'model'} applies to the first imager. A calibration at another size of the same shape is scaled (CameraModel.scaled).
  4. No device. A sensor without a model_id (or with one the library does not have) gets one imager, a pinhole 1280 × 720 px, 90° × 60°, labelled "Default camera (no device chosen) … a default, not a device figure".

library.projector(sensor) gives the projector's FOV and pose (frames.Pose), library.depth_spec(sensor) a copy of the depth block, library.min_z(sensor, w, h) the stated minimum Z for a depth stream (None when not stated).

Custom devices

Sensors ▸ New custom device… (the spec's "Library ▸ New custom device…": the window has no Library menu; the Library panel's right-click menu has it too) writes $XDG_DATA_HOME/DexVantage/library/<kind>/<id>.json (~/.local/share when XDG_DATA_HOME is not set) through library.add_custom(dict), after the same schema check; a custom device cannot take a built-in id. remove_custom(id) deletes it. A file that does not parse or match the schema, or reuses an id, is skipped with a plain message (library.problems(), also logged); the app never stops on one.

Prices

core/prices.py; schema price (library.schema.json). An entry's price block is the price of one unit as the maker's own online store or official price list states it on the date read:

"price": {"amount": 375, "currency": "USD", "unit": "each", "kind": "maker_store",
          "source": "https://store.realsenseai.com/buy-realsense-depth-camera-d435.html", "read": "2026-11-07",
          "region": "the store's default price list (USD)", "what": "store page price (schema.org offer 375 USD)"}
  • kind is maker_store (a list price from the maker's own store), maker_price_list (the maker's official price list page) or reseller (only where the maker sells through that store alone; what says why).
  • min_quantity marks a volume price (NVIDIA's "(1KU+)" is 1000): such a figure is not what one unit costs.
  • options lists other variants and their prices (polarizer, enclosure, gripper, control-box cable). When the price depends on an option the entry does not fix (a cable sold in four lengths), amount is null and only the options carry prices: nothing is picked for the user.
  • sale_amount keeps a temporary sale price; amount is the regular price.
  • No price is estimated, and no exchange rate is applied (every price found is in US dollars). A custom entry may still use the older {"value", "currency"} form; it reads the same.

The rig cost

core/prices.total, read by the rig inventory (core/studies/rig_inventory.py) and the metric rig.cost (USD):

cost = Σ amount_i over the enabled sensors that state a price
every sensor priced            -> cost (exact)
some priced, k of n unpriced   -> cost with bound 'at_least'; the reason names and counts the k parts
none priced                    -> not stated
more than one currency         -> refused: "no exchange rate is assumed"; a non-USD total is not stated as rig.cost

A lower bound was chosen over "not stated" because it still decides one direction of a requirement: "cost ≤ 500 USD" fails on "at least 574 USD" (the dexhand sample), while "cost ≤ 1000 USD" stays not evaluated until the missing part is priced (core/requirements.decide). The optimiser uses the cost objective only when every device it can choose states a price in US dollars, rig.cost's unit (optimise/objectives.price_stated, optimise/rig._price_gaps), so it never ranks designs by a bound or by a cost that is not stated; a price without a currency counts as not stated.

On the samples (2026-11-07): ur5e 700 USD (D405 + D435), aloha 1300 (four D405), panda-bimanual 1025, g1 375, dexhand at least 574 (See3CAM_24CUG 199 + D435 375; the OVM6948 states no price), so100 not stated (sample placements with no library device).

Validation

tests/test_library.py validates every file in the folder (any kind, whoever added it) against the schema with jsonschema and with the fallback structural check, checks a set of figures against the datasheet tables (baselines, min-Z, shutters, the D405's missing projector), and checks that the D435's imagers come out one stated baseline apart along −y with optical frames per REP-103 and the stated 87° × 58° depth FOV.

Data consistency (validation/checks_library.py, model library)

Known limits

  • Prices are list prices on the date read: no tax, shipping, duty or discount, and a store can show another price in another region (the region field says which list was read; several stores redirected to a regional locale but priced in USD). NVIDIA's module prices are volume prices (1000+). They go stale: library.prices_sound fails a price read more than two years before the run, so re-read and update them.
  • The rig cost counts only the enabled sensors (hosts, cables, lights, robots and brackets are priced in the library but not added to rig.cost).
  • The figures are the makers' typical values; FOVs vary unit to unit (Intel: about ±3°, mechanical tolerance ±5%).
  • Distortion and pixel size are not published for the Intel cameras: pinhole models fitted to the stated FOV, so pixel densities near the image corners carry the unmodelled ≤ 1.5 % distortion. Calibrate a unit for its own K and D.
  • max_range_m is the upper figure of a line such as "0.2 m to over 3 m (varies with lighting conditions)": a lower bound on the usable range under good conditions, not a hard limit; range_text keeps the words.
  • Stream tables are the USB 3.1 Gen 1 (or GMSL) ones; USB 2.0 modes are not entered. Simultaneous-stream limits (Tables 4-6, 4-7) are not modelled by the library.
  • The RGB offsets of the D415, D435, D435i, D455 and D457 are not stated by the maker; their RGB imagers sit at the depth origin as a labelled assumption until a unit is calibrated.
  • The IMUs of the D435i, D455 and D457 are mentioned in the notes but not modelled.
  • Without jsonschema installed the fallback check does not test every bound of the schema.
  • The data-consistency checks catch figures that contradict each other, not figures that are consistently wrong; a decentred or anamorphic lens, or a FOV measured on a crop other than the native image, can trip library.fov_shape without a typing error.
  • Most added cameras state no distortion, so their imagers are pinhole fits to the stated FOV (wide lenses such as the OAK-1 W's 127° or the Leopard 120H with "< -66.5%" distortion are poorly described by a pinhole: calibrate). The STURDeCAM39 fisheye is a Kannala-Brandt fit to its stated angles.
  • Tactile entries with magnetic or force-array sensing (XELA, PaXini, AnySkin, ReSkin, PapillArray) list no stream: their readings are not images, and the bandwidth study needs their data rate entered as a figure.
  • Research-sensor figures (Digit 360, AnySkin, ReSkin) are what the papers report for their prototypes; units built from the open designs can differ.
  • Host entries are specification tables, not measurements: NVIDIA's CSI figures are aggregates, its USB, PCIe and Ethernet share UPHY lanes ("not every listed port is available together"), and the module data sheets (downloads from NVIDIA's developer site) were not read. Only two capture cards state a sustained rate; measure yours.
  • The Meta Quest pages are rendered by script and were read through a text fetch of the page; the Basler card's specification is Basler's document hosted by a distributor (Basler's own pages refused automated reads).
  • Policy timings are each source's single measurement on the named GPU, batch 1; they do not carry over to other hardware, and the README's "slightly slower" or "3-4 times faster" are not entered.
  • Lights stated only by an illuminance give the lighting study no flux; the illuminance is not converted because the beam profile behind it is not stated.
  • Custom teleop devices cannot be made in the custom-device dialog (it covers the sensor and host kinds); write the JSON by hand into the user library, and the Library panel has no Teleop group (devices are added on the Teleop page).
