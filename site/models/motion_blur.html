Motion blur and rolling shutter

Module: dexvantage/core/studies/motion_blur.py (study id motion_blur, batch flag --motion-blur OUT.json). Page: dexvantage/ui/pages/motion_blur.py (Analysis ▸ Motion blur and rolling shutter, Placement workspace). Checks: dexvantage/validation/checks_blur.py (model timing). Tests: tests/test_motion_blur.py. Spec §7.9. Written for DexVantage (Vantage's core/timing.py models a vehicle driving past static objects; only its convention that a rolling shutter's middle row is exposed around trigger + readout/2 + exposure/2 is shared, in the time sync study).

What is computed

Along each trajectory of the project (or, without one, the approaches of the first grasp set), for every camera imager of the active variant that streams (cameras and depth sensors switched on): how fast the image of what the camera sees moves, the smear that gives during one exposure, and the skew across a rolling-shutter readout.

Image-plane velocity

A camera's optical frame (z forward, x right, y down) is at o in the world with rotation R (columns = the optical axes in world coordinates). A camera on a robot link moves with the link's twist at the trajectory's joint velocity:

v_c = J_v(q) q̇,  ω_c = J_ω(q) q̇

J(q) is MuJoCo's world-frame Jacobian of the camera's optical centre held fixed in the link (RobotModel.jacobian(q, link, point), mj_jac). A camera on a tripod or the cage has v_c = ω_c = 0. A world point P moving at v_P sits at P_c = Rᵀ(P − o) in the optical frame. Since Ṙ = [ω_c]× R,

Ṗ_c = Rᵀ (v_P − v_c − ω_c × (P − o))
(u̇, v̇) = ∂π/∂P_c · Ṗ_c

where π is the imager's lens model (core/optics). For a pinhole, ∂π/∂P_c is written out (fx/z, −fx·x/z², fy/z, −fy·y/z²). For Brown-Conrady and the fisheye models it is a central difference of π in the optical frame, with a step of 10⁻⁶ of the distance.

The points:

  • the scene: a volume_grid³ grid (4³ by default) over each task volume, and object_points (40) points on each task object's surface. These do not move (v_P = 0);
  • the trajectory's robot: its TCP and up to nine points on each fingertip pad (core/grasps.py). These move at v_P = v_o + ω × (P − o_link), from one Jacobian per link.

A point counts for a camera while it projects inside the image and in front of the camera. Occlusion is not tested.

The joint velocity q̇ is the slope between the trajectory's samples. Trajectories are linear between samples (core/robot/trajectory.py), so q̇ is constant over each interval. The study samples every step_s (10 ms), at interval mid-times.

Smear, skew and capture position

smear = |(u̇, v̇)| × exposure        (pixels, first order in the exposure)
skew  = |(u̇, v̇)| × readout         (rolling shutter; 0 for a global shutter)

The skew is how far a point's image moves between the reading of the first row and the last (Liang et al. 2008). The study also gives where a rolling shutter records a moving point (rolling_capture). Rows are read at t(r) = readout · r/(H − 1). A point at (u₀, v₀) when the first row is read, moving at (u̇, v̇), is caught when its row is read:

t* = v₀ / ((H − 1)/readout − v̇),   recorded at (u₀, v₀) + (u̇, v̇)·t*

Where exposure and readout come from

  1. Your figure, if you gave one. Settings exposure_s / readout_s, as {sensor uid: {value, source}}: what the driver is set to, or a measurement.
  2. Otherwise the library entry's figure (cited). The D400 series datasheet gives the depth imagers' manual exposure range for module 430: 1 to 166 ms (Table 4-23, entered for the D435). The RGB camera's "Manual Exposure Time" is listed as 1 to 10000 with no unit, so it is not entered. No readout time is stated for any imager in the library.
  3. Otherwise a stated upper bound. The exposure is at most one frame period, 1/fps, or the maker's stated maximum when that is shorter. A rolling-shutter readout is at most one frame period, because the rows of one frame are read within one frame period at a steady frame rate. The result labels both "upper bound". Neither is a device figure.
  4. A sensor with no library device has no frame rate, so no exposure is known. Its smear needs your exposure; the page still gives its image speed and the longest exposure that keeps the limit.

Report

  • Per trajectory segment: the trajectory's intervals, merged into at most max_segments (24) equal spans. For each segment and camera imager: the worst image speed, smear and skew, the time, the point, and a status.
  • Per camera imager: its peak image speed, smear and skew, the longest exposure that keeps the limit (max_exposure = smear_limit / peak speed), and the worst moment.
  • Worst frames: the frames_kept (3) frames with the largest image speed, each at least 5 steps from the others. Each frame keeps q and the smear vector of up to vectors_kept (150) points; the Camera view draws them.
  • Status: "sharp" when the smear is within the limit (smear_limit_px, 1 px by default; a DexVantage default, not a standard); "some blur" up to twice the limit; "blurred" beyond. When no exposure is set the smear is an upper bound, and over the limit the words are "may blur" and "may be blurred" (the severity is that of the bound). "exposure not set" when no exposure is known.
  • Recommendation: for each camera over the limit, the longest exposure that keeps the limit, rounded down to two significant figures, with the predicted smear. Apply records it as your exposure (one undo step); set the same exposure on the camera. When that is shorter than the shortest exposure the maker states, the study suggests slowing the motion by the factor needed instead. When a rolling shutter's skew is over the limit, it suggests a global-shutter camera (skew 0) and lists library entries whose imagers are all global shutter.
  • Without a trajectory: up to max_grasps (8) grasps of the first set. The arm moves joint-linearly from the pre-grasp pose (pre_grasp_m, 0.10 m back along the approach) to contact at approach_speed_m_s (0.10 m/s). Both poses are solved by IK, and both figures are defaults.

The 3D view draws the TCP's path coloured by each segment's status.

Validation (model timing, measured 2026-11-07, full run)

Check Reference Measured Tolerance
blur.image_velocity_fd central differences (h = 10 µs) of the points projected at q(t ± h), forward kinematics only; UR5e sample, both cameras, 92 frames 2.8 × 10⁻⁹ of the frame's peak speed 10⁻⁵
blur.smear_analytic f·v·E/Z for a point moving parallel to the image plane, f·ω·E for a camera turning about its y axis; pinhole and Kannala-Brandt 2.4 × 10⁻¹¹ (relative) 10⁻⁶
blur.rolling_shutter_bruteforce row-by-row readout simulation along the true motion (forward kinematics at every row time), readouts 10 and 30 ms, 2 002 points 2.4 % of the point's motion (worst 0.29 px, wrist camera, 30 ms) 5 %

The rolling-shutter difference is the model's own limit. The study takes the image motion as linear over the readout. The real motion turns and changes perspective, which shows at second order: a few per cent of the displacement for a wrist camera over 30 ms.

Known limits

  • First order in the exposure: the image velocity is taken as constant over one exposure. It jumps at the trajectory's samples, where q̇ changes at once.
  • No occlusion test: a point counted may be hidden behind the gripper.
  • The exposure bound overstates: when no exposure is set, the bound is the frame period. That overstates the smear of a camera whose auto-exposure runs shorter.
  • Light is not modelled: a shorter exposure needs more light or gain (the lighting study).
  • Other motion: the robot's other joints and other robots stay at home. A carried object's points are counted as scene points, not moved with the gripper.
  • Rolling shutter: rows are read top to bottom at a constant rate.
  • Lens derivative: the distortion of non-pinhole lenses enters through a numerical derivative.
  • Vibration: the mount's vibration is not modelled.
