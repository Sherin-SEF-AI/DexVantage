Optimiser and design agent

Spec §7.11. Code: dexvantage/core/optimise/ (nsga2.py, objectives.py, search.py, rig.py, designer.py), the studies optimise and design_agent (core/studies/optimise.py), the page Analysis ▸ Optimise (ui/pages/optimise.py), the checks validation/checks_optimise.py (model id optimise).

Ported from Vantage core/optimiser.py (the current layout as the first candidate, the Pareto front of the feasible candidates else of those closest to feasible, the changes said per camera, locked cameras kept) and core/rig_designer.py (requirements as constraints, cost from stated prices only, a seeded search budgeted in evaluations, options spread along the front and each explained, the current rig as the reference, accepting adds a variant and records the decision). Vantage's single-objective evolution is replaced by NSGA-II; its automotive objectives (blind ground, pixel density, perception range), reference vehicle rigs, lidars, radars and LLM brief are not ported.

The search: NSGA-II

Deb, Pratap, Agarwal and Meyarivan (2002), in NumPy, over mixed genes:

  • Continuous genes (mount shifts in metres, turns in degrees, the design agent's placement numbers in [0, 1]): simulated binary crossover (SBX, Deb and Agrawal 1995) in its bounded form, probability pc = 0.9 per pair and 0.5 per gene, distribution index η_c = 15; polynomial mutation (Deb and Goyal 1996), bounded, probability 1/n per gene, η_m = 20. These are the values of the NSGA-II paper's experiments and are labelled defaults (settings pc, eta_c, eta_m).
  • Categorical genes (a camera kept or not, its device, its mount site): uniform crossover (each gene swapped with probability 0.5 when the pair crosses) and mutation to another choice drawn uniformly, probability 1/n.
  • Selection: binary tournament on the crowded comparison (lower front, then larger crowding distance); the next population is parents and offspring sorted into fronts, filled front by front, the last front cut by crowding distance.
  • Constraints by constrained domination (Deb 2002, §VI): feasible beats infeasible, the smaller total violation beats the larger, Pareto dominance between feasible designs.
  • Budget in evaluations (the last generation is cut to fit); seed: one numpy generator, so a run is reproducible exactly; cancel between evaluations (the designs scored so far are kept and the result says "cancelled"); progress per evaluation.

Every objective is minimised internally (a higher-is-better metric is negated). A figure a study does not state for a design is +∞ for sorting only (the design ranks below every design that states it); it is shown as "not stated" and never replaced by a number. Along an objective it does not state, a design takes no part in the crowding distance (the extremes are those of the stated values), so the crowded comparison never prefers it to a stated design.

Objectives and constraints

Objectives are metric ids of core/metric_defs.py (the vocabulary the requirements scorecard, tolerance and experiments share). The default list is the specification's:

Objective Metric Unit Better
Contact visibility grasp.contact_visibility % higher
Depth-valid coverage depth.valid % higher
Camera count rig.camera_count count lower
Cost rig.cost USD lower
Bandwidth margin bandwidth.margin Mbit/s higher
Bracket printability brackets.not_ready count lower
VLA token coverage vla.min_tokens tokens higher

An objective is left out, with its reason in the result, when the current rig's study does not state it (the optimiser; a rig with no host has no bandwidth margin), when no design states it (the design agent), or, for the cost, when any device the search can choose has no price in its library entry (no built-in entry states one, so the cost is left out on every sample; a custom entry with a price block brings it in). Nothing is guessed.

Constraints are the project's requirements (project.requirements: metric, >=/>/<=/</==, threshold) plus settings constraints. The violation of one is its shortfall divided by max(|threshold|, a unit floor: 1 % for percentages, 1 for counts, 0.1 ms, 1 mm…), so requirements in different units weigh alike; a figure not stated counts as a violation of 1 (it cannot be shown to hold), and so does a figure stated only as a bound that does not settle the test (the scorecard's "not evaluated"). A requirement whose metric is unknown is listed and not used. A rig without a camera is infeasible.

Scoring a design

Each design is a project (core.factors.apply returns a new one; the design agent adds a variant) scored by core.metrics.evaluate, one study at a time (a study that stops with an unexpected error leaves its metrics "refused" with a sentence instead of stopping the search). The studies run at screening settings (labelled defaults, settings screen; {} uses the project's own): the grasp study on 40 grasps without sensitivity, gallery or pre-contact steps and with 200 bootstrap resamples (0.5–1 s instead of about 40 s on the UR5e sample), the brackets study on 12 joint samples without meshing. confirm scores the options shown again at the project's own settings. Identical designs are scored once (a memo), and study results are kept between runs in a shared result cache (256 results).

The optimiser (Improve this rig)

Factors (core.factors paths; settings factors replaces them): for every enabled, unlocked camera, depth or tactile sensor, dx, dy, dz within ±move_m (default 0.05 m) along its parent frame and dpitch, dyaw within ±turn_deg (default 15°) about its own axes; with allow_remove (default on) enabled; with swap_models (default off) model among the library's devices of its kind. Locked sensors have no factor; a factor in the settings that names one is refused with a note, and every design built is checked to leave the locked sensors' fields unchanged (an assertion in the search, a validation check). The current rig is the first design. Accept sets the design's factors on the active variant (one undo step) and refuses when the rig changed since the search ran.

The design agent (Design new rigs)

A rig is up to max_cameras (default 3) slots; each slot's genes are: on or off, the device (settings models, default every camera and depth entry of the library), the mount site, and three numbers in [0, 1] placing it there.

  • Static site (settings static_frames, default the cage): looking at the first task volume's centre from azimuth −180° to 180°, elevation 20° to 85° and distance 0.45 to 1.2 m (defaults elevation_deg, distance_m).
  • Link site (settings links, default each robot's gripper frame): riding on the link, looking at a point 3 cm beyond the fingertips (the mean of the fingertip frames at home, in the link frame; the approach axis runs from the link origin to it) from 2 to 15 cm behind the fingertips and 4 to 12 cm off the axis, at any angle around it (defaults wrist_back_m, wrist_radial_m).

Locked sensors are in every rig unchanged; the other current sensors are left out (each design is a complete rig) unless keep_unlocked. Each new sensor whose device states its size gets the bracket type core/brackets.suggest_kind gives. Accept adds the design as a new variant, makes it active and records the decision in the settings and the design log (one undo step).

Options and explanations

The options are up to options (default 5) designs of the first front of every design scored, with the current rig taking part in the dominance comparison (a design the current rig dominates is not offered), the same figures counted once, the best design on each objective first and then by crowding distance. Each option's explanation is built from its figures only: what it changes or what the rig is, what it gains and gives up against the current rig (with the differences), on which objectives it is the only best of the set, what it trades against its nearest neighbour on the front (objectives scaled by their range over the options), which requirements it meets or misses and which figures are not stated.

The headline is the number of designs offered with a status word: better rig found (a feasible design at least as good as the current rig on every objective and better on one), trade-offs only, current rig is best, misses the requirements (none feasible), with "(cancelled)" when stopped.

IGD is the inverted generational distance (Coello Coello and Reyes Sierra 2004): the mean distance from 1 000 points of the analytic front to the nearest point found. NSGA-II ran with population 100, 25 000 evaluations (10 000 for CONSTR) and the default operators. The self-test line optimiser runs ZDT1 at 6 000 evaluations (IGD 0.0316 against 0.05, 0.2 s) and checks the seed.

Known limits

  • The front is an approximation from a seeded evolutionary search within a budget of evaluations, not a proof of optimality; with the page's default budgets (96 and 120 designs) it is a coarse one. NSGA-II's crowding distance spreads well for two or three objectives and less well beyond (many-objective problems want reference directions, NSGA-III).
  • Each design is scored by the studies themselves, so the search inherits their modelling limits; at the screening settings the grasp study's figure carries the sampling error of 40 grasps (use confirm, or screen: {}).
  • A figure a study does not state ranks a design last on that objective, and a requirement it does not state counts as missed.
  • The cost is the sum of stated device prices only (no cables, hosts, brackets, lenses sold separately, taxes); no built-in library entry states a price.
  • The optimiser moves mounts within a box and turns within a cone of the current pose; it does not move a camera to another link. The design agent's mount sites are a sphere around the task volume and a cone around the gripper's approach axis, not every surface of the cell or the robot; the camera's roll about its view axis is not searched; the stream mode is the device's largest.
  • The budget is in evaluations, not seconds: about 0.8 s per design on the UR5e sample for the optimiser and 2 s for the design agent (whose designs carry more studies' work, e.g. new brackets).
