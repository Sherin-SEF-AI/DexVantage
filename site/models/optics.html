Optics: lens models, pixel density and defocus

Module: dexvantage/core/optics/lens.py (exported by dexvantage/core/optics/__init__.py). Checks: dexvantage/validation/checks_optics.py (model optics). Tests: tests/test_optics.py.

The projection mathematics is ported from Vantage core/lens.py (the radial functions r(θ), the Kannala-Brandt polynomial, fitting Kannala-Brandt terms to a stated field of view, and Intrinsics' projection and unprojection). DexVantage works in pixels throughout, uses OpenCV's parameterisation and pixel convention, adds the Brown-Conrady model, an exact constrained fit through the stated angles, and thin-lens defocus.

Conventions

  • Points are in the camera's optical frame (REP-103 _optical_frame): z forward along the optical axis, x right, y down, metres. θ = atan2(√(X² + Y²), Z) is the angle off the axis.
  • Pixels follow OpenCV: the centre of pixel (i, j) is the point (u, v) = (i, j). The image spans u ∈ [−0.5, W − 0.5], v ∈ [−0.5, H − 0.5]; a centred principal point is cx = (W − 1)/2, cy = (H − 1)/2. Vantage put pixel centres at i + ½ (image [0, W], centre W/2); DexVantage does not. Resampling the image by s maps u to s (u + ½) − ½ (CameraModel.scaled).
  • u = fx · xd + cx, v = fy · yd + cy, with (xd, yd) the model's normalised image coordinates. No skew.

The models

CameraModel(width, height, fx, fy, cx, cy, model, dist, max_theta) is a frozen (hashable) dataclass.

model normalised image point (xd, yd) dist
pinhole (X/Z, Y/Z) empty
brown x = X/Z, y = Y/Z, r² = x² + y², c = (1 + k1 r² + k2 r⁴ + k3 r⁶)/(1 + k4 r² + k5 r⁴ + k6 r⁶); xd = x c + 2 p1 x y + p2 (r² + 2x²), yd = y c + p1 (r² + 2y²) + 2 p2 x y [Brown 1966; OpenCV calib3d] (k1, k2, p1, p2, k3) or (k1, k2, p1, p2, k3, k4, k5, k6)
kb θd = θ (1 + k1 θ² + k2 θ⁴ + k3 θ⁶ + k4 θ⁸); (xd, yd) = θd (X, Y)/√(X² + Y²) [Kannala and Brandt 2006; OpenCV fisheye] (k1, k2, k3, k4)
equidistant g(θ) = θ empty
equisolid g(θ) = 2 sin(θ/2) empty
stereographic g(θ) = 2 tan(θ/2) [Miyamoto 1964] empty

For the radial models (xd, yd) = g(θ) (X, Y)/√(X² + Y²) with g = r/f. equidistant is kb with k = 0 exactly. OpenCV's fisheye computes θ as atan(r) of (X/Z, Y/Z), which equals atan2 in front of the camera; DexVantage's θ may pass 90° (a fisheye sees behind its own plane).

The valid angle max_theta (rad) is the largest angle off the axis the model maps. A point beyond it, or behind a pinhole or brown camera, is never projected: its uv is NaN and ok is False; it is never wrapped onto the image. A pixel beyond it has no ray (NaN). Left out, it is the angle where the model stops being one-to-one:

  • pinhole: 90°.
  • brown: the first angle where, in one of 72 directions around the axis, the image radius stops rising, the Jacobian determinant of the distortion map reaches zero, or c's denominator does (4000 angles, refined by bisection). Tangential terms grow as r² against the radial r·c, so a model with p1 or p2 always folds somewhere.
  • kb: the first angle where θd(θ) stops rising, at most 180°.
  • equidistant, equisolid, stereographic: 180°.

A max_theta beyond that angle is refused with the reason (two rays would share a pixel). A smaller one models a circular fisheye image: the corners then have no rays and fov_deg counts them at the valid angle.

Projection and unprojection

project(P) returns (uv (N, 2), ok (N,)); ok means in front within the valid angle and inside the image (each edge widened by EDGE_TOL_PX = 10⁻⁹ px so a ray unprojected from the edge itself counts as inside). uv is finite but ok False when the point is valid but off the image. unproject(uv) returns unit rays (N, 3); with_ok=True also returns the validity. pixel_rays(stride) gives the rays of the pixel centres u = 0, stride, … as (H′, W′, 3). Any leading shape is accepted.

  • Brown-Conrady inverse: Newton's method on (x, y) with the analytic 2 × 2 Jacobian (OpenCV's undistortPoints iterates the fixed point instead), started at the distorted point, kept inside the one-to-one disc (an iterate outside is pulled back to its edge; twice outside means no root inside), converged when the re-distorted point is within 10⁻¹² (normalised units, about 10⁻⁹ px at f = 1000 px). A pixel that does not converge has no ray.
  • Kannala-Brandt inverse: Newton's method on θ, safeguarded by bisection inside a bracket on [0, min(max_theta, fold angle)], where θd(θ) rises, so the root is unique. Radii beyond θd(max_theta) have no ray.
  • Classical projections: closed forms (atan, θ = r, 2 asin(r/2), 2 atan(r/2)).

Fields of view

fov_deg() returns {'h', 'v', 'd'} from the model itself: the angle off the axis of the left edge (u = −0.5) plus that of the right edge (u = W − 0.5) along the principal point's row; likewise top and bottom; and corner to corner, the mean of the two diagonals. An edge beyond the valid angle counts at the valid angle.

CameraModel.from_fov(width, height, hfov_deg, vfov_deg=None, model='pinhole', *, dfov_deg=None, max_theta=None) centres the principal point and reproduces the stated angles; fit_fov(...) returns the model with a report (base, f_px, fit, stated, model_fov, max_error_deg, note).

  • pinhole, brown (zero distortion: none is stated), equidistant, equisolid, stereographic: W/2 = fx · g(h/2) in closed form; fy from the vertical angle when one is given (non-square pixels), else fy = fx. A diagonal angle cannot be matched as well and is refused.
  • kb: square pixels. With the horizontal angle only, the equidistant curve. With a vertical and/or diagonal angle, ported from Vantage: the base projection and focal length that alone fit the stated points best (best_base, least squares of r = f g(θ) relative to each half size; pinhole a candidate only below 160°), then Kannala-Brandt terms. New in DexVantage, fit_kb_exact passes the curve through the stated points (up to three equality constraints on four terms, the remaining freedom kept closest to the base curve sampled out to the image corner: a KKT system). If that curve folds back before the image corner plus 2°, Vantage's weighted fit (fit_kb_to_fov, weights 50, 20, 8) is used at the strongest weight whose curve still rises. The valid angle reaches FIT_MARGIN_DEG = 2° past the image corner. The stated angles are reproduced; the curve between them is a model, not a measurement, and the report's note says so.
  • from_fov refuses (ValueError, saying why and what to check) a fit that misses a stated angle by more than FOV_FIT_TOL_DEG = 0.5°, a default of DexVantage: such figures fit no one-to-one lens with square pixels on that image, typically because they were stated for another crop or resolution. fit_fov returns the best effort.

Conversions

from_opencv(K, D, width, height, model) takes OpenCV's K and D (aliases: fisheye → kb; plumb_bob, rational_polynomial → brown; pinhole with non-zero D is read as brown, as cv2.projectPoints reads it). Skew, thin-prism and tilt terms are refused. to_opencv() returns (K, D, model_str): pinhole (D five zeros) and brown for cv2.projectPoints, kb for cv2.fisheye; equidistant is exactly kb with zero terms; equisolid and stereographic have no OpenCV form and are returned as the least-squares Kannala-Brandt curve over the image's angles (kb_approximation() gives the terms and the largest error in pixels: 6 × 10⁻⁸ px for a 180° equisolid lens on 1920 × 1080, 0.016 px for a 180° stereographic one). to_dict / from_dict store the figures with "pixel_centres": "opencv"; scaled(w, h) resamples.

Pixel density and viewing angle (§7.1)

pixels_per_metre(P, normals=None) is the local linear pixel density at P: √|det J|, with J the 2 × 2 Jacobian of (u, v) with respect to the two lateral coordinates (metres) of a small flat patch at P facing the camera (normal along the line of sight). It is the square root of the pixels per square metre: the geometric mean of the densities along the patch's two principal directions (the densities along single directions lie between J's singular values). Pixels per millimetre are pixels_per_metre / 1000 (pixels_per_mm). With R = |P| and g = r/f:

pinhole:         det J = fx fy / (R² cos³θ)
brown:           det J = fx fy |det D(x, y)| / (R² cos³θ)          D = ∂(xd, yd)/∂(x, y) at x = X/Z, y = Y/Z
radial models:   det J = fx fy g′(θ) g(θ) / (R² sin θ)            → fx fy g′(0)² / R² on the axis

(the image area per solid angle times the solid angle per area at range R, 1/R²). On the axis a pinhole gives f/Z. For a surface with normal n, α the angle between n and the line of sight: the line of sight is in the null space of the projection's 3-D Jacobian, so the tilted patch's area scales by |cos α| and the density by √|cos α| (normals=). angle_to_axis(P) is θ; incidence_angle(P, normals) is α (0 = facing the camera).

Defocus (§7.1): thin lens

For focal length f, f-number N (aperture diameter A = f/N), focus distance s and an object at z (distances from the lens's principal plane), the Gaussian lens equation 1/f = 1/s + 1/v gives the image distances v_s = f s/(s − f) and v_z = f z/(z − f); by similar triangles the blur disc on the sensor at v_s has diameter b = A |v_z − v_s| / v_z, which simplifies to

b(z) = A f |s − z| / (z (s − f))            (s = ∞:  b = A f / z)   [Ray 2002; Hecht 2017]

blur_circle_px(f_mm, f_number, focus_m, z_m, pixel_um) returns b / pixel pitch (z may be an array; z ≤ f gives inf). depth_of_field(f_mm, f_number, focus_m, coc_mm) solves b(z) = c:

near = s A f / (A f + c (s − f)),    far = s A f / (A f − c (s − f))   (∞ when A f ≤ c (s − f))

which equals the photographic hyperfocal form (H = f²/(N c) + f; near = s (H − f)/(H + s − 2f), far = s (H − f)/(H − s)) that the check writes out separately. Any figure None (not stated by the maker) gives None; an f-number is often not stated, so the coverage study then reports defocus as not given. DEFAULT_COC_PX = 2 px is a default of DexVantage (a blur spot up to two pixels across counts as sharp), a user setting, not a standard.

The round-trip check counts the pixels that have no ray (1025 of 120 000 with the default seed: random Brown-Conrady models whose curve folds back before the image corner); they are refused, not compared.

Known limits

  • Thin lens only: no pupil magnification, diffraction, aberrations or focus breathing; defocus distances are from the principal plane, not the camera's front face.
  • No skew, no thin-prism or tilt terms, no rolling shutter (§7.9 adds time).
  • A model fitted from a stated FOV reproduces the stated angles; its distortion between and beyond them is a model. A calibration (Sensor.calibration, OpenCV K and D) replaces it when the unit has one.
  • The Brown-Conrady valid angle is found on 72 directions and 4000 angles: a fold narrower than that could slip through, and a positive Jacobian on a disc makes the map locally, not provably globally, one-to-one. A strongly distorted calibration can fold back inside its own image; those pixels get no ray rather than a wrong one.
  • pixels_per_metre is the geometric mean density; a study that needs the worst direction should use J's smaller singular value (not provided yet).
  • MuJoCo's fiducial check (below) covers MuJoCo's pinhole cameras only; MuJoCo renders no distortion, so the distorted models are checked against OpenCV alone.

Against MuJoCo-rendered fiducials

Spec §10 asks for MuJoCo's camera projection of rendered fiducials within a stated, measured pixel tolerance. Two checks (dexvantage/validation/checks_optics_mujoco.py) do it. A scripted MJCF scene holds a 9 × 7 grid of spheres spread over the image, at 0.4 to 1.5 m, each about 14 px across (150–220 px masks), drawn with 128 slices so their outline is round; MuJoCo renders a segmentation image through EGL with multi-sampling off (validation/mjrender.py). Three cameras: fovy="60" at 640 × 480 (our CameraModel.from_fov); focal in metres with a sensorsize and fx ≠ fy at 640 × 480; focalpixel with a principalpixel offset at 800 × 600.

Frames, written out: MuJoCo's camera looks along its −z axis, +x right, +y up (XML reference, body/camera). Our optical frame looks along +z, +x right, +y down. A world point X is P = R_optᵀ (X − p) in the optical frame, with R_opt = R_cam · diag(1, −1, −1), R_cam = data.cam_xmat and p = data.cam_xpos. Row 0 of a mujoco.Renderer image is the top, and pixel centres are at integer (column, row), as OpenCV counts them. A fovy camera has square pixels with fy = (H/2) / tan(fovy/2) and the principal point at ((W − 1)/2, (H − 1)/2); with a sensor size, fx = focal_x · W / sensor width (MuJoCo stores focal and principal in length units in cam_intrinsic).

The principal offset's direction is not stated in MuJoCo's reference. Rendered on MuJoCo 3.17.0, principalpixel (px, py) moves the image of the optical axis to ((W − 1)/2 − px, (H − 1)/2 − py), row 0 at the top: an x offset to the left, a y offset upwards. mjrender.camera_model uses that; the third camera then checks that the offset is a pure shift and combines correctly with the focal lengths, not the sign itself (which was read off a render). An MJCF exporter (§9.2) must write the principal point with these signs.

Check Compared Measured (default seed) Tolerance
optics.mujoco_fiducial_masks centroid of each sphere's MuJoCo mask against the centroid of the mask CameraModel predicts (pixel centres whose ray falls within the sphere's angular radius asin(r/d)) worst 0.075 px over 189 fiducials (fovy 0.046, focal 0.075, principal 0.045); median 0 (identical masks); smallest mask overlap 98.9 % 0.1 px: one boundary pixel decided the other way moves a 180 px centroid by about 0.04 px
optics.mujoco_fiducial_centres the MuJoCo centroid against the image centre CameraModel.project predicts: midway between the projections of the two tangent rays at θ ± α in the plane of the axis and the sphere centre (a sphere's image is an ellipse whose centre is not the projected sphere centre) worst 0.167 px, median 0.068 px; without the ellipse correction the offset is up to 0.254 px (the perspective bias) 0.3 px: the centroid sampling error of these masks alone, measured on CameraModel's own predicted masks against the predicted centres, is 0.167 px

So the sub-pixel bias of a fiducial centroid has two parts, both measured here: the perspective bias of a sphere's image (0.25 px at 14 px across, growing with the angle from the axis and the size) and pixel sampling (0.17 px). Neither is an error of the projection model; with both removed, MuJoCo and CameraModel agree to 0.075 px. The render takes about 0.4 s. Without EGL the checks fail with the reason, they are not skipped.
