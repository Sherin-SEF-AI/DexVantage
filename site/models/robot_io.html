Robot import and export: URDF inertials, USD import, OpenVLA encodings, Isaac principal point

1. URDF inertials

Each link that is a MuJoCo body gets <inertial> from the robot's compiled MuJoCo model (DexVantage's link frame is the MuJoCo body frame):

  • origin xyz = body_ipos (centre of mass in the link frame), rpy = 0 0 0;
  • mass = body_mass;
  • the full tensor about the centre of mass in the link axes, I = R diag(body_inertia) Rᵀ, R the rotation of body_iquat (MuJoCo's principal axes). MuJoCo's URDF compiler diagonalises it again when it reads the file.

A comment in each <inertial> says where the figures come from (inertial_sources, read from the robot's stored file with mujoco.MjSpec, parsing only):

Source When
"from the robot's file" the body has an explicit inertial (URDF <inertial>, MJCF <inertial>)
"computed by MuJoCo from the robot file's geoms" an MJCF body without one, inertiafromgeom auto or true: the geoms' mass or density as the file states, else MuJoCo's default density 1000 kg/m³ (the SO-100's Base, the 2F-85's mount and pads)
"a minimal placeholder" no mass in the robot's files: a URDF link without <inertial>, the joint-anchor links <body>__<joint>, the sensor and optical frames, links MuJoCo merged into a parent

The placeholder is MIN_MASS = 1e-6 kg and MIN_INERTIA = 1e-9 kg·m² on the diagonal: a stated minimum, not a figure of the robot. MuJoCo refuses a moving body without mass ("mass and inertia of moving bodies must be larger than mjMINVAL"), which is why the file could not be loaded before.

The file also carries MuJoCo's URDF extension element (MuJoCo modeling guide, "URDF extensions", read 2026-11-07):

<mujoco>
  <compiler angle="radian" meshdir="meshes/" strippath="true" discardvisual="false" fusestatic="false"/>
</mujoco>

With the element present the compiler attributes are stated rather than left to the URDF defaults (measured: without strippath="true" the mesh path became meshes/meshes/…). fusestatic="false" keeps every sensor and optical frame as a body, so mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path('ros2/rig.urdf') loads the file unchanged with the camera frames findable by name. urdfdom ignores elements it does not know.

2. USD robot import

core/robot/importer.import_robot(path) converts a .usd, .usda, .usdc or .usdz file to MJCF (usd_import.convert_to_folder, a temporary folder) and imports that MJCF as for any MuJoCo file. The stored files are the converted ones: robot.xml, meshes/*.stl and .dexvantage/usd_source.json (the USD layers used with their SHA-256, the units, the up axis, the counts and the conversion notes). Robot.source.note names the USD file and repeats the notes. usd-core (pip install "dexvantage[usd]", usd-core==26.8) is optional: without it the importer and the wizard say what to install.

What is read (pxr; the OpenUSD UsdPhysics schemas, read 2026-11-07). The stage is opened with payloads loaded and traversed with instance proxies (Isaac's instanceable meshes). Bodies: prims with PhysicsRigidBodyAPI under the first PhysicsArticulationRootAPI prim (every rigid body when there is none). Joints: UsdPhysicsJoint prims that link two of those bodies or one of them and the world (a body0/body1 that is not a rigid body is the world frame of that prim). pxr's composition warnings are collected (UsdUtils.CoalescingDiagnosticDelegate) and turned into one sentence naming the layers that could not be opened.

Frames. Every prim's world map is pxr's XformCache.GetLocalToWorldTransform, scaled to metres by metersPerUnit and turned to Z up for a Y-up stage by Rx(+90°). A body frame is that map's rotation and translation (polar decomposition; the scale is kept for points, localPos and primitives). UsdPhysics states a joint by its frame in each body, F0 = X_body0 · (localPos0, localRot0) and F1 = X_body1 · (localPos1, localRot1), with

X_body0 · F0 · M(q) = X_body1 · F1, M(q) = Rot(axis, q) (revolute) or Trans(axis · q) (prismatic),

axis the token X, Y or Z of the joint frame. With the joint frames in each body, J0 = X_p⁻¹ F0 and J1 = X_c⁻¹ F1, the child's MJCF body is placed at B = J0 · J1⁻¹ and its hinge or slide at pos = t(J1), axis = R(J1) e_axis. MuJoCo then moves the child as X_p · B · Trans(pos) · Rot(axis, q) · Trans(−pos) = X_p · J0 · M(q) · J1⁻¹, the UsdPhysics constraint exactly. The joints rule the kinematics; an authored body pose that disagrees with them at the authored state is named in the notes. A joint written the other way round (its body0 is the child in the tree) is turned: frames swapped, axis negated, the same motion for the same q. Revolute limits are degrees (converted to radians), prismatic limits stage units (converted to metres); a limit that is not finite or beyond ±10⁶ (the float sentinels some writers use), or lower above upper, is no limit.

Tree. Breadth-first from the body jointed to the world (else the articulation root, said to be fixed at its authored pose: DexVantage places a robot by its base pose). A joint that would close a loop, a disabled joint, one excluded from the articulation and a generic (D6) or distance joint (imported as fixed) are named in the notes. Spherical joints become ball joints (held at home, as for every ball joint in DexVantage).

Geometry. Every UsdGeom.Mesh, Cube, Sphere, Capsule and Cylinder below a body (and not below another body): with PhysicsCollisionAPI a collision geom, otherwise (visible, purpose not guide or proxy) a visual geom (contype = conaffinity = 0, group 1, which core/robot/model.py reads as visual-only). Meshes are triangulated (fans; leftHanded flipped), moved into the body frame with their full transform and written as binary STL in metres. Cube: box of half size size/2 × scale; Sphere: sphere, or ellipsoid under a non-uniform scale; Capsule and Cylinder: radius × the scale across the axis, half length height/2 × the scale along it, turned so the axis token is the geom's z.

Mass. A body whose PhysicsMassAPI states mass and diagonal inertia gets <inertial pos quat mass diaginertia>: mass × kilogramsPerUnit, centerOfMass in metres in the body frame, principalAxes, inertia × kilogramsPerUnit × metersPerUnit². The MJCF says inertiafromgeom="false", so a body without stated mass gets none (MuJoCo does not invent one from the geoms' volume); core/robot/model.py then compiles with its stated boundmass, as for a kinematics-only URDF.

The writer (core/robot/usd_writer.write_robot_usd) writes a project robot as /<robot> (ArticulationRootAPI), one Xform per MuJoCo body with RigidBodyAPI and MassAPI, its geoms under visuals/ and collisions/, and /<robot>/joints/*. With R_a a rotation taking +X to MuJoCo's axis a, anchor p and reference qpos0: F1 = Trans(p) R_a and F0 = T0 Trans(p) Rot(a, −qpos0) R_a (slide: F0 = T0 Trans(p − a·qpos0) R_a), so the USD joint position equals DexVantage's q. A body with several hinges or slides gets massless intermediate bodies. MuJoCo 3.17's own mujoco.usd.exporter.USDExporter was checked and not used: it writes render frames from mujoco.Renderer (meshes and animated transforms, no UsdPhysics bodies, joints or limits) and needs OpenGL; MuJoCo 3.17's MjSpec.from_file has no USD decoder in the pip wheel ("could not find decoder for resource").

3. OpenVLA encodings

openvla_encoding(project, action_space=None) chooses the OXE state_encoding and action_encoding from the rig, citing (read 2026-11-07):

  • OpenVLA at c8f03f4: prismatic/vla/datasets/rlds/oxe/configs.py L33-40 (StateEncoding: JOINT = "Joint Angles (7, <PAD> if fewer) + Gripper Open/Close (1)", JOINT_BIMANUAL = "2 x [ Joint Angles (6) + Gripper Open/Close (1) ]"), L44-50 (ActionEncoding: EEF_POS, JOINT_POS "Joint Delta Position", JOINT_POS_BIMANUAL, EEF_R6), materialize.py L32-33 (make_oxe_dataset_kwargs refuses anything but EEF_POS and EEF_R6: "only EEF_POS & EEF_R6 actions supported!").
  • OpenVLA-OFT at e4287e9: the same enums (configs.py L39, L48), its ALOHA entries L681-687 (JOINT_BIMANUAL, JOINT_POS_BIMANUAL, image keys primary, secondary, left_wrist, right_wrist), materialize.py L32-33 (also accepts JOINT_POS_BIMANUAL) and L43-45 (absolute_action_mask = [True] * 14: absolute joint positions).

Inputs: the target is OpenVLA-OFT when a project policy is an OpenVLA-OFT library entry, else OpenVLA; the action space is the export option action_space ('joint' or 'eef'), else the OpenVLA policy's stored overrides['action_space'], else 'joint' (a default: the LeRobot export records joint positions); per robot, the arm joints are its actuated joints minus those marked gripper (the import wizard's Joints step), and its gripper is one open/close value.

Rig Action space Encodings Fits when
one arm eef POS_EULER / EEF_POS always (both loaders accept EEF_POS)
two arms eef POS_EULER / EEF_POS (nearest) never: no bimanual end-effector encoding
two arms joint JOINT_BIMANUAL / JOINT_POS_BIMANUAL each arm 6 arm joints + a gripper, and the target is OpenVLA-OFT
one arm joint JOINT / JOINT_POS (nearest) never: neither loader accepts JOINT_POS, and it is deltas

When nothing fits, the nearest is written, the file's docstring says "NO ENCODING FITS" with the reasons, and the export notes say so. With an OFT policy on a two-arm rig the image keys are OFT's (left_wrist is the first camera on the arm named left, else the one whose base is further along +y). oxe_dataset_config.json carries the whole choice under encoding. The dataset's own arrays are not converted (one gripper value per arm, end-effector deltas).

4. Isaac: an off-centre principal point

Isaac Lab's spawner warns that "Camera aperture offsets are not supported by Omniverse. These parameters will be ignored" (v3.0.0 sim/spawners/sensors/sensors.py L162-165; v2.3.2 L108-111). At v3.0.0 the OpenCV lens model is authored as omni:lensdistortion:<model>:fx, fy, cx, cy (sensors.py L52-58, L68-108, L190-192: "the RTX/OVRTX renderer honors these attributes natively"), and Camera._initialize_intrinsics uses them (camera.py L828-857, L879-904: "these intrinsics may be non-square (fx != fy) or off-center"; without them cx = width * 0.5). So any imager whose principal point is not at ((W − 1)/2, (H − 1)/2) gets an opencvPinhole model (zero distortion for a plain pinhole) carrying its cx, cy; the aperture offsets stay in the USD for other readers. v2.3.2 has no OpenCV fields (sensors_cfg.py) and its camera.py L589-599 sets c_x = width * 0.5: its render is centred. Each off-centre camera is then named with its offset:

  • in the export notes and isaac_cameras.json (principal_point: offset_px, off_centre, what v3 and v2 do);
  • in its comment in isaaclab_cameras.py and isaaclab_v2_cameras.py;
  • in the Export dialog, under the Isaac row, before anything is exported (principal_point_lines, no forward kinematics).

Why these tolerances: the URDF writes 17 significant digits, so anything above rounding is a wrong frame (millimetres or degrees). UsdPhysics stores joint frames and limits as 32-bit floats (about 6 × 10⁻⁸ relative), which sets the USD figures; 10⁻⁶ m is far below an axis sign, anchor or frame-swap error. The third-party file was written by another program, so a convention the importer and DexVantage's writer might share by mistake would show there. The OpenCV attributes are 32-bit floats too (about 3 × 10⁻⁵ px at 640 px). The USD checks are registered only when usd-core is installed (as formats.bracket_step is only with gmsh). Isaac Lab, Isaac Sim and OpenVLA are not installed: the exports are checked against the pinned sources, not by running those programs.

Known limits

  • URDF: no collision geometry; mimic joints and MuJoCo equality constraints are not written; sensor links carry the minimal placeholder, not the device's mass; links MuJoCo merged into a parent carry the placeholder (their mass is in that parent).
  • USD import: one articulation per file; loops (four-bars, parallel grippers' second links), mimic and tendon couplings, drives, friction, damping, armature, effort and velocity limits are not imported; materials become one colour per geom; textures, cones, planes, height fields, point instancers and subdivision are not read; a body's non-uniform scale is applied to its points and primitives but not to its joint-frame rotations or its inertia; mesh-only collision shapes are used as MuJoCo meshes (convex hulls in MuJoCo's collision); time samples are read at the earliest time; the wizard's mesh step lists the stage's layers, not each mesh.
  • The bundled third-party UR5e (dexvantage/resources/usd_robots/newton_ur5e/, BSD-3-Clause, THIRD_PARTY.md) lacks its 6.3 MB GeometryLibrary.usdc (over the 5 MB file budget), so its visual meshes are left out (said in the notes); its bodies, joints, limits, masses and collision primitives are read.
  • OpenVLA: the choice is per rig, not per recorded dataset; the dataset's arrays are not converted; the policy's action_space is not offered on the Policies page yet (set it as the export option or in the project file).
  • Isaac: v2.3.2 cannot show an off-centre principal point at all (stated per camera); the OpenCV attributes' pixel convention is taken to be OpenCV's (the source does not state it), half a pixel from Isaac's pinhole width / 2.
