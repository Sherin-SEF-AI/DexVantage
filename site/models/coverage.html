Coverage and resolution in the task volume

Module: dexvantage/core/studies/coverage.py (study id coverage, batch flag --coverage OUT.json). Page: dexvantage/ui/pages/coverage.py (Analysis ▸ Coverage and resolution, Placement workspace). Checks: dexvantage/validation/checks_coverage.py (occlusion.coverage_vs_raycast, optics.coverage_density_fd). Tests: tests/test_coverage.py. Spec §7.1. Builds on Sparse voxels with adaptive refinement, Occlusion: articulated cube maps and exact ray casting, Optics: lens models, pixel density and defocus and The hardware library: devices from datasheets.

What it computes

Every task volume is voxelised as a sparse grid with adaptive refinement (voxels.SparseGrid.from_project: 5 mm by default, refined to at most 2 mm inside refinement regions and within 20 mm of the task objects; both sizes are project.analysis settings). For every voxel centre P and every camera c:

Figure Definition
in image P in the imager's optical frame (z forward, x right, y down), Rᵀ (P − t) with R, t the imager's world pose (the sensor's mount, posed on its link at q, composed with the imager's pose in the sensor body from the library), projects inside the image: CameraModel.project ok (in front, within the lens model's valid angle, u ∈ [−0.5, W − 0.5], v ∈ [−0.5, H − 0.5])
visible in image, and the cube map of distances around the imager's optical centre has nothing nearer along the line of sight (occlusion.CubeMap.visible, ε = 1 mm, face size 512 by default)
pixels per mm CameraModel.pixels_per_mm(P): √|det ∂(u, v)/∂(lateral)| for a small patch at P facing the camera, so a 1 mm feature spans that many pixels; a surface tilted by α away from the line of sight has √cos α of it. For a pinhole lens it is √(fx fy) / (R² cos³θ)^½ / 1000 at range R and θ off the axis
viewing angle the angle between the optical axis and the line of sight (CameraModel.angle_to_axis)
defocus the thin-lens blur-disc diameter in pixels, `b = (f/N) f
redundancy the number of cameras for which the voxel is visible

Which imager is the camera. A camera sensor's first imager (its entry's reference imager). A depth sensor's RGB imager when it has one, because the colour image is what a policy is trained on; otherwise its reference (depth) imager. Setting depth_imager: "depth" uses the reference imager of every depth sensor; imagers: {sensor uid: name} picks one per sensor. The result names the imager used and why (cameras[i].imager, imager_why). The stereo pair belongs to depth validity (§7.2). Tactile pads are contact sensors and are left out (the result says how many). A sensor without a library device gets the library's labelled default imager (90° × 60°, 1280 × 720 px pinhole, "no device chosen"); the page marks it "(default)".

The joint state. A study gets only the project, so q comes from the study's settings, project.analysis.studies['coverage']['q'] = {robot uid: [values in RobotModel.joint_names order]}; a robot not named there is at its home pose. The page's Run passes the 3D view's current q in those settings for that run only (on the copy of the project the study runs on: AnalysisController.run(sid, settings)), so the coverage is for the pose on screen and the project is not changed. Batch mode uses whatever the saved settings hold (home when nothing). The result records the q used (q) and where it came from (q_source: settings or home); values outside the joint limits are clipped, with a note; a vector of the wrong length is refused with a plain message.

Defocus needs four figures: the f-number, the focus distance, and the focal length in millimetres (from the pixel pitch, f = √(fx fy) · p). The library states N for many imagers, but no maker in it states the focus distance of a fixed-focus lens, and the RealSense entries state no pixel pitch, so on the samples the blur is "not computed" and each camera's record says which figure is missing. You can enter measured figures per sensor in the settings, optics: {sensor uid: {"focus_m", "f_number", "pixel_um", "focal_mm"}}; the result marks each figure's source (maker, yours, or derived). The pitch is taken as binned with the stream (pitch × native width / stream width), an assumption. A blur up to coc_px (2 px, optics.DEFAULT_COC_PX, a default) counts as sharp, and the depth of field for that circle is reported (dof_near_m, dof_far_m).

The summary

Shares are of the task volume: each voxel weighted by its volume, so refined voxels count for the space they fill, not for their number. Voxels whose centre lies inside a box, cylinder or sphere task object are left out (no camera can see inside a solid); grid.inside_objects counts them.

Key Meaning
summary.seen_ge1, seen_ge2, blind, seen_1_only shares seen by ≥ 1, ≥ 2, 0 and exactly 1 camera
summary.resolved_ge1, resolved_ge2 shares seen at ≥ min_px_per_mm (1 px/mm, a default) by ≥ 1 and ≥ 2 cameras
summary.best_ppmm_median, best_ppmm_p5 the best camera's pixels per mm over the seen volume (volume-weighted median and 5th percentile)
summary.pct_ge2, status, word, advice the headline: % seen by ≥ 2, its status and a sentence on what to change
cameras[i].seen, outside_image, hidden, only_camera, resolved per camera (seen + hidden + outside = 1)
cameras[i].ppmm_median, ppmm_p5, ppmm_p95, angle_median_deg, angle_max_deg, range_median_m over the voxels it sees
cameras[i].defocus computed, why, the figures and their sources, blur_median_px, sharp, dof_near_m, dof_far_m
volumes[k] per task volume: voxels, seen_ge1, seen_ge2, blind, per_camera

Status thresholds (user settings, with documented defaults; not standards): the share seen by two or more cameras reads good from good_pct = 90 %, warning from warn_pct = 60 %, serious from serious_pct = 30 %, and critical below. The page edits them as one undo step each and labels each "default" until it is changed.

Advice. When the volume is well covered, the sentence names the coarsest view (lowest 5th percentile px/mm). Otherwise it names the camera that sees least, and whether more of the volume lies outside its image (aim it, move it back or widen the lens) or is hidden by the scene at this pose (move it to look past the arm or the objects).

The per-voxel arrays

voxels holds compact per-voxel arrays, each numpy bytes (little-endian), zlib-compressed and base64-encoded (coverage.encode_array / decode_array; coverage.decode(doc) returns them all as numpy arrays):

Array Encoding
ijk voxel centre as integer multiples of unit_m (half the refined edge) from its volume's grid corner origins[volume] (uint16; uint32 when needed)
volume_index, refined, inside the voxel's volume, whether it is refined (edge refine_m, else voxel_m), whether it lies inside an object
redundancy cameras that see it (uint8)
best_ppmm the best visible camera's px/mm in steps of 0.01 (uint16, up to 655.35)
cameras[i].state 0 outside the image, 1 in the image but hidden, 2 visible
cameras[i].ppmm px/mm in steps of 0.01 (0 outside the image)
cameras[i].angle viewing angle in steps of 0.5° (255: outside the image)
cameras[i].blur blur in steps of 0.1 px (254: 25.4 px or more; 255: not computed or outside the image); null when not computed

The UR5e sample's 480 000 voxels with its two cameras take 1.7 MB of JSON.

Computation

The scene's occluders are built once for q (scenegeom.scene_occluders), each camera's by the mount-link rule (scenegeom.sensor_occluders with the imager's centre as origin), and each camera's cube map through the shared occlusion.CACHE (the GPU when there is one, backend setting otherwise). The voxels are then processed in chunks of 100 000 (chunk setting), with progress after every chunk, a partial result (each finished camera's seen share) after every camera, and a cancel check before every chunk; a cancelled run returns what it has with partial: true. The UR5e sample (480 000 voxels, two cameras, 193 000 occluder triangles) takes about 0.8 s with the GPU backend; the SO-100 sample (57 600 voxels) 0.1 s.

Settings

All in project.analysis.studies['coverage'], every key optional: q; voxel_mm, refine_mm (else project.analysis'); good_pct, warn_pct, serious_pct; min_px_per_mm; coc_px; cube_n; backend (auto, gl, numpy); depth_imager (rgb, depth); imagers; modes ({uid: [w, h]}, a stream mode, else the stream chosen for the sensor on the Sensors page, Sensor.stream, as the Camera view uses it, else the imager's largest listed stream); optics; sensors (only these uids; default every enabled camera and depth sensor); chunk. The result's settings.defaults lists the keys left at their default.

The scripted scene: a 0.30 × 0.30 × 0.20 m task volume on a table, a box and a cylinder standing in it, two static cameras with the default imager and one with the Intel RealSense D435 entry (its RGB imager). The two paths share only the scene's triangles.

Known limits

Visibility is judged at the voxel centre, and per cube-map texel (0.22° at a face centre at n = 512), so a voxel within about a texel of a silhouette may be judged either way. A voxel inside a robot link or a mesh object counts as unseen (only box, cylinder and sphere objects are tested for their insides). Pixels per millimetre is for a patch facing the camera, because the task's surfaces are not known. No sensor housings occlude. Overlapping task volumes count their shared space once for each. The defocus is a thin-lens model, computed only from stated or entered figures, with the pitch assumed binned with the stream. Coverage is for one joint state per run; a trajectory or a grasp set needs a run per pose (Phase 3). Lighting, exposure, motion blur and the depth sensor's own validity (min-Z, the stereo pair, the projector) are other studies (§7.2, §7.8, §7.9). The Camera view overlay of §7.1 is not drawn yet. An exported result made with the page's live q cannot be re-run by --reproduce at that q, because q is not stored in the project (the result records it).
