Lighting, glare and IR wash-out

Spec §7.8. Code: dexvantage/core/materials.py (the BRDF and the material tags), dexvantage/core/lights.py (light sources and their photometry), dexvantage/core/studies/lighting.py (the study, batch flag --lighting), dexvantage/ui/pages/lighting.py (the page). Checks: dexvantage/validation/checks_lighting.py. Tests: tests/test_lighting.py. The worked example is in Lighting and glare: a worked example on the UR5e sample.

The study answers two questions for the rig's cameras over the gripper's working poses:

  1. How much of the object and the gripper does each camera see blown out by specular highlights at its exposure?
  2. How much valid depth does an active-stereo sensor lose because ambient IR washes its projector's pattern out, or because the surface is dark, IR-absorbing or clear?

It then tries the usual fixes (a polariser, moving or dimming a light, tilting the camera, blinding a window) and reports each one's change in the predicted figure.

Lights

Lights live in project.workspace.lights. Each is a dict:

{'uid', 'name', 'kind': 'panel' | 'ring' | 'window' | 'point' | 'spot',
 'pose': {'position', 'quaternion' (x, y, z, w)},        # the face looks along its local +z
 'size_m': [w, h] | null,
 'luminance_cd_m2': {'value', 'source'} | null,
 'luminous_flux_lm': {'value', 'source'} | null,
 'ir_irradiance_w_m2': {'value', 'source', 'at_m'?} | null,
 'cct_k': {'value', 'source'} | null,
 # optional: 'library_id', 'beam_deg' {'value', 'source'}, 'inner_m', 'dim' (0..1), 'enabled', 'sun' (a window)}

Each figure carries its source: a datasheet with its URL and the date it was read, or "my meter reading". A figure nobody states is null. The study then names the light and says which figure is missing. It does not fill the gap.

Photometry (the visible band, in candela and lux):

kind model intensity / luminance
point isotropic I = Φ / 4π (or L · A when a luminance and a size are given)
spot uniform cone, full angle β I = Φ / (2π (1 − cos β/2)); no β stated → not computed
panel, window, ring Lambertian area emitter L as stated, else L = Φ / (π A); A = w h, a ring's annulus π(R² − r²)

An area light is split into n × n patches (n_area, 4 by default; a ring into 4n patches round the annulus). Each patch is a point emitter with I(θ) = L · A_patch · cos θ on its front side. The irradiance on a plane facing an emitter is E⊥ = I(θ) / r² (the inverse-square law [McCluney 2014]). On a surface with normal n it is E⊥ max(0, n · ωi).

IR (W/m², the band the depth sensors' projectors use). ir_irradiance_w_m2 is the in-band irradiance on a surface facing the light, on its axis, at at_m. A point-like light then has J = E · at_m², and an area light has radiance J / A. If an area light has no at_m, the figure is taken as the irradiance at its own face (for a window, the IR coming through the glass), with radiance E / π.

Sun through a window. A window's optional sun block gives a parallel beam: its direction, illuminance and IR irradiance. The beam reaches a point when the line from the point towards the sun passes through the window's rectangle and nothing in the scene is in the way (core/raycast).

dim scales every figure of the light. The stated figure itself stays as stated.

The emitting face is taken as the panel's front, or the ring's annulus: this is an assumption, said in each entry's note. Machine-vision IR illuminators state radiant intensity per LED. Projectors. Intel's D400 datasheet (337029-017, March 2026, Tables 3-16 and 3-17) states "Optical Power 360 mW average" and "850 nm ± 10 nm" for the D41x and the D43x/D45x projectors. These figures are in the D415, D435, D435i and D455 entries (projector.optical_power_w, wavelength_nm, optical_power_what). The D457's own datasheet does not state the figure, so its entry has none. No other maker in the library states its projector's power.

Materials: the BRDF

f_r(ωi, ωo) = f_d + f_s. The specular term is the microfacet model of Walter et al. 2007 (eq. 20):

f_s = F(ωi · h) G(ωi, ωo) D(h) / (4 |n · ωi| |n · ωo|),   h = (ωi + ωo) / |ωi + ωo|
  • D: GGX / Trowbridge-Reitz, D(h) = α² / (π cos⁴θ_h (α² + tan²θ_h)²) [Walter 2007 eq. 33; Trowbridge and Reitz 1975].
  • G: Smith's masking-shadowing as the separable product G1(ωi) G1(ωo), with G1(v) = 2 / (1 + √(1 + α² tan²θ_v)) [Walter 2007 eqs. 23 and 34; Smith 1967].
  • F: Schlick's approximation F0 + (1 − F0)(1 − c)⁵ [Schlick 1994, eq. 15].
  • f_d: the energy-coupled diffuse term of Ashikhmin and Shirley 2000 (eq. 5), (28 ρ_d / 23π)(1 − F0)(1 − (1 − |n·ωi|/2)⁵)(1 − (1 − |n·ωo|/2)⁵). It is symmetric, so the BRDF is reciprocal, and it gives way to the specular lobe at grazing angles, so the total stays within the energy bound.

Parameters per tag. F0 comes from measured complex refractive indices through ((n − 1)² + k²) / ((n + 1)² + k²), at 550 nm (visible) and 850 nm (the D4xx projector). The indices are tabulated measurements from the refractiveindex.info database, which is public domain (CC0); all were read 2026-11-07.

tag index source n at 550 / 850 nm F0 vis / IR α ρ_d IR albedo
matte PMMA, Sultanova et al. 2009 1.4926 / 1.4835 0.0391 / 0.0379 0.5 0.5 0.5
glossy PMMA as matte as matte 0.1 0.5 0.5
metallic Al, McPeak et al. 2015 0.789 + 5.852i / 2.205 + 7.188i 0.9157 / 0.8576 0.2 0 0

α, ρ_d and the IR albedo are DexVantage defaults, not measurements. Measured BRDFs (the MERL database of Matusik et al. 2003, fitted with microfacet models by Löw et al. 2012 and others) spread widely within each of these classes. No single measured sample stands for "glossy" or "matte". Set measured values per object with the study setting materials {object uid: {'alpha', 'albedo', 'ir_albedo', 'depth_reads_through'}}.

The rule for a tag list. The first of transparent, metallic, glossy, black and matte that is present gives the material ('matte' when none is). ir_absorbing then lowers the IR albedo. A transparent part is a flag, depth_reads_through. Its default is true: active stereo and ToF see through clear plastic and glass, and return the background or nothing. Set it per object.

The polariser. An ideal linear polariser passes k/2 of unpolarised diffuse light (Malus's law averaged over the angles [Hecht 2017, §8.2]). Turned to pass the weaker component of the specular reflection, it passes k min(R_s, R_p) / (R_s + R_p) of that light. R_s and R_p come from the exact Fresnel equations with the complex index [Hecht 2017, §4.6.2], at each microfacet's incidence angle. The model turns the polariser to its best for every point, so its gain is an upper bound on what one polariser achieves. A metal reflects both polarisations about equally, so a polariser removes little of a metal's highlight.

The study

Working poses. If the project has a grasp set, the study picks up to max_poses (6) grasps evenly from it. The grasp study's IK solves them, with its own settings and obstacle checks. The reachable solutions are the poses. Without a grasp set, the study uses the joint state of the settings q, else home. The page's "Pose on screen" uses the 3D view's joint state.

Sampling. Points are drawn uniformly by area, with a seed. There are 400 per task object, 1200 over the gripper (the gripper frame's link and every link below it), and 500 per surface. Each point stands for its share of its part's area. Robot links take robot_material ('matte' by default, labelled) or link_materials. Model files carry no material tags.

Visibility comes from core/occlusion. A point is seen when it projects inside the imager's image and the camera's cube map of the posed scene (512 texels, as in the grasp study) has nothing nearer. A point is lit when the cube map at the light's centre has nothing nearer. These are hard shadows. The camera's seat on its mount link is left out by scenegeom's rule. Three details make the test agree with exact ray casting:

  • A point on a closed part (an object, the gripper) whose outward normal faces away from the camera or the light is on the part's far side, so it is hidden. Surfaces are one-sided sheets, so either side of them shows.
  • The point is lifted lift_mm (1 mm, a default) along its normal, turned towards the viewer.
  • The tolerance is slope-scaled: eps + ¾ · texel · distance · tan θ, with θ the angle from the normal, capped at 80°. A surface seen obliquely changes depth across a texel. Without the slope term, such surfaces shadow themselves (shadow-map "acne" [Williams 1978]). With 256-texel maps and a flat 1 mm tolerance, the first version of this study disagreed with ray casting on 20 % of the sampled points; it also gave 22 % on the GPU and 30 % with numpy for the same scene.

The check lighting.visibility_vs_raycast measures the remaining disagreement.

Radiance. L = Σ f_r E⊥ max(0, n · ωi) over the lights' emitters and the sun, in cd/m².

Saturation, absolute. This model runs when the imager's sensitivity S (DN per lux second at the sensor, unity gain) and f-number N are stated and an exposure t is given (the library entry, or the settings sensors[uid]). It uses the camera equation E = (π / 4) T L cos⁴α / N², then DN = S g t E. A point is saturated when DN ≥ 2^bits − 1. The lens transmittance T is 1, an assumption, because no maker states it. The gain g defaults to 1, and bits to 8. No library entry states S today, so this model runs only on your figures.

Saturation, relative. This is the default, and it is labelled "relative" wherever it is shown. It models an auto-exposure that meters the mean luminance L̄ of the visible sample as 18 % grey. ISO 12232's saturation-based speed puts that grey at 18 %/√2 = 12.7 % of saturation, which leaves half a stop of headroom. A point therefore saturates when L ≥ L̄ / 0.127 = 7.86 L̄ (sat_ratio, a default taken from that definition). The glare index L / L̄ is relative, not absolute. Two consequences follow. Scaling every light by the same factor changes nothing (the test test_relative_model_is_labelled_and_scale_free). Dimming a single light changes nothing either.

The headline is the worst camera's saturated share of the visible object and gripper area, averaged over the poses, with the worst pose named. Surfaces (the table) are reported beside it. Status: good under 1 %, warning under 5 %, serious under 15 %, else critical. These thresholds are defaults (good_pct, warn_pct, serious_pct). When an active depth sensor's predicted depth loss is worse (warning from 5 %, serious from 15 %, critical from 30 %, defaults depth_good_pct, depth_warn_pct, depth_serious_pct), it sets the status and the word names both, for example "low glare; depth washed out", so a green glare word never hides a washed-out depth sensor.

Glare maps. Each camera's image is cut into 32 × 24 cells (grid). Each cell keeps the largest saturated share of the visible area in it over the poses, and the largest level (glare index, or DN / full scale). The page draws the map. The Camera view outlines saturated cells in the critical colour and cells near saturation (half the threshold) in the warning colour.

IR wash-out. This runs for active IR stereo and structured light. At each pose the study takes the points the reference imager sees inside the projector's stated cone. The pattern's irradiance there is E_p = (P / Ω) cos θ / d². P is the projector's stated optical power, spread uniformly over its rectangular field Ω = 4 arcsin(sin(a/2) sin(b/2)). The ambient IR E_a comes from the lights' and the sun's IR figures, with shadows. The pattern's contrast is C = E_p / (E_p + E_a). This is the same for any Lambertian albedo, because both are reflected alike. A point is washed out when C < contrast_min (0.5 by default: the ambient is as bright as the pattern). A point on a surface with IR albedo under ir_albedo_min (0.1, a default) is "dark". A point on a transmissive object the depth reads through is "clear". The predicted loss of valid depth is the share of the visible object and gripper area with any of the three. If the projector's power is not stated, wash-out is not computed and the result says so. The dark and clear shares are still computed.

Recommendations. Each one re-runs the same evaluation with one change:

  • a polariser on the worst camera;
  • the light that lights most saturated points moved ±move_m (0.3 m) along world x or y, or upwards;
  • that light dimmed to dim_to (50 %);
  • the camera tilted ±5° or ±10° about its optical x axis (the rotation is about the optical centre, so its cube maps are reused);
  • for wash-out, the IR source that adds most ambient IR switched off or blinded.

The suggestion is the one that lowers the headline most. If no camera saturates, it is the one that lowers the depth loss most. Apply makes it as one undo step.

Performance

The worked example is the UR5e sample with one Key Light and a metallic can, 6 working poses and two cameras. There the study takes 1.3–1.4 s on the NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5080 (EGL device 0, the discrete GPU; driver 595.99.02). This includes the IK of up to 18 grasps and the recommendations, which re-evaluate up to 13 variants. With the numpy rasteriser (no GPU) it takes 18 s, and the headline is the same, 34.9 %. The visibility agreement at other map sizes (area share against ray casting): 0.85 % at 256 texels and 0.31 % at 1024.

The study runs on the analysis controller's worker thread. Its UI-thread work was measured on the page, offscreen: showing a result 0.5 ms, the Camera view's glare-map outlines 0.5 ms, the light glyphs 0.2 ms, the lights table 0.6 ms, painting the glare map 17 ms, and Apply 8–17 ms.

Known limits

  • Light transport. Only direct light is modelled: no inter-reflections and no ambient term. Shadowed parts are darker than they would be in a room. The relative model meters only the sampled object, gripper and surfaces, not the whole image.
  • Lights. Shadows are hard and cast from each light's centre. Emitters are Lambertian or uniform cones, with no measured beam profile and no IES file. A window is a diffuse emitter plus an optional sun beam; there is no sky model.
  • Spectra. The camera's spectral response is taken as photometric (V(λ)). The IR figures are taken to be for the band the depth imagers see; no maker in the library states the imagers' filter.
  • Projector. Its field is taken as uniform over the stated FOV, but the real dot pattern is not. Its own shadow is not modelled. Intel halves the laser power above 60 °C module temperature, which is not modelled either. The 360 mW is the datasheet's projector power and the top of the laser-power control (0–360 mW in 30 mW steps, Table 4-23): the wash-out is predicted at full laser power, and a lower setting weakens the pattern.
  • Wash-out and passive stereo. Passive stereo still matches textured surfaces when the pattern is washed out, so the predicted loss applies to textureless surfaces.
  • Materials. Roughness and albedos are defaults. Every metal is aluminium, every dielectric is PMMA, and every clear part is BK7, at two wavelengths. G is the separable Smith form, slightly darker than the height-correlated one at grazing angles. Transmission is a flag, with no refraction.
  • Polariser. Its gain is an upper bound, because it is turned at its best for every point.
  • Sampling. Points are sampled, not pixels. A highlight smaller than the sample spacing can be missed; raise object_points and gripper_points to catch it.
  • Visibility. It is decided per cube-map texel, with a slope tolerance. A point within about a texel of a silhouette or a contact can be judged either way, and a point just behind an oblique edge can leak through (0.66 % of the area in the check).
  • Robot materials. Model files carry no material tags, so a robot's link materials are a setting.
