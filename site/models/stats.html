Statistics and power analysis

Research tools part 2. Code: dexvantage/core/stats.py (the maths: NumPy and SciPy, no project, no Qt), dexvantage/core/statcompare.py (the comparison sources), dexvantage/core/studies/stats.py (the study stats, batch flag --stats-saved OUT.json; a spec file with --stats SPEC.json, dexvantage/batch_stats.py), dexvantage/ui/pages/statistics.py (Analysis ▸ Statistics), dv.stats (dexvantage/api.py), the assistant tool get_statistics (dexvantage/assistant/tools.py). Checks: dexvantage/validation/checks_stats.py (model stats). Metrics: stats.p_adjusted (headline), stats.n_needed, stats.planned_power.

The question it answers: is a difference between two rigs, two sets of runs or two policies' success rates larger than chance, how large is it, and how many evaluation episodes, tolerance draws or demonstrations does it take to tell two conditions apart? Every result names the test that ran and why, and spells out its assumptions.

Out of scope: sequential testing and early stopping. Every test here is a fixed-sample test. Looking at the results as episodes come in and stopping as soon as a test turns significant inflates the false-positive rate well above alpha; group-sequential designs (O'Brien-Fleming, alpha spending) are not offered. Decide the number of episodes first (the power calculator), then test once.

Pairing by seed: with the same seed, monte_carlo draws the same uniform (or normal) numbers for the same sensors in the same order, so draw i of A and draw i of B are the same relative mount errors (common random numbers). The comparison is then of the designs, not of the luck of the draws, and a paired test is much sharper. When the variants' sensors differ (another count or a calibrated sensor on one side), draw i is paired by index only; the paired test stays valid (the pairs are independent of each other) but gains less.

Tests and why

Success rates

Fisher's exact test (default): conditional on the total successes m, the count of condition 1 is hypergeometric, P(X = x) = C(n₁, x) C(n₂, m − x) / C(n₁ + n₂, m). Two-sided p: the probability of the tables no more likely than the observed (relative tolerance 10⁻⁷, as R and SciPy); one-sided, as every test here, about condition 2 against condition 1 ('greater': p₂ > p₁, the z test's direction; SciPy's fisher_exact names that direction for the table with condition 2 in the first row). Why: exact at any number of episodes, which is what robot-policy evaluations have (10 to 50 episodes per condition); conservative (its false-positive rate is at most alpha). The pooled two-proportion z test is the option: z = (p̂₂ − p̂₁) / √(p̄ q̄ (1/n₁ + 1/n₂)); a note warns when an expected count is under 5.

Intervals of each rate: Wilson (1927) score interval and Clopper-Pearson (1934): lower B(α/2; k, n − k + 1), upper B(1 − α/2; k + 1, n − k) (beta quantiles; never below the nominal coverage). Effect sizes: the risk difference p₂ − p₁ with Newcombe's hybrid score interval (1998, method 10, from the two Wilson intervals (lᵢ, uᵢ)): [d − √((p₂ − l₂)² + (u₁ − p₁)²), d + √((u₂ − p₂)² + (p₁ − l₁)²)]; the odds ratio (k₂(n₁ − k₁)) / (k₁(n₂ − k₂)) with Woolf's interval exp(ln OR ± z √Σ 1/cell), with ½ added to every cell when one is zero (Haldane-Anscombe, said). When the Newcombe interval excludes zero but Fisher's test does not reject, a note says the evidence is borderline (the two are different methods and Fisher's is conservative).

Figures over draws, runs or episodes

The primary test is a permutation test of the mean difference:

  • paired: T = mean(dᵢ), dᵢ = bᵢ − aᵢ; under H₀ each dᵢ is as likely positive as negative, so the null distribution is T over sign flips of the dᵢ;
  • unpaired: T = mean(b) − mean(a); under H₀ every value is as likely to have come from either side, so the null distribution is T over relabellings of the pooled values.

It is exact (every rearrangement: 2ⁿ sign flips, C(n₁ + n₂, n₁) relabellings) when there are at most n_perm (default 9 999) of them, else n_perm random rearrangements with p = (b + 1) / (B + 1) (Phipson and Smyth 2010). Two-sided p is twice the smaller tail, values within 100 ε of the observed counting as ties (SciPy's convention). Why it is the default: the figures over tolerance draws or runs are often skewed or pile up at a bound (a coverage share near 100 %), so a t test's normality is doubtful at the small n a study allows; the permutation test needs only exchangeability under H₀. It also reacts to a difference of spread in the unpaired case (said).

Shown for reference with every comparison: the paired t test and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (paired), or Welch's t (Welch-Satterthwaite df = (v₁ + v₂)² / (v₁² / (n₁ − 1) + v₂² / (n₂ − 1)), vᵢ = sᵢ² / nᵢ) and the Mann-Whitney U (unpaired). Their assumptions: the t tests, normal values (paired: normal differences); the rank tests, continuous values (ties are handled by average ranks and the tie corrections). Method choice, as SciPy's: Mann-Whitney exact (the U distribution by the recurrence f(i, j, u) = f(i − 1, j, u − j) + f(i, j − 1, u)) when a sample has at most 8 values and there are no ties, else normal with the tie correction and a ½ continuity correction; Wilcoxon (zero differences dropped) exact without ties or zeros up to 50 pairs (the distribution of T⁺ by subset sums), all 2ⁿ sign flips with ties or zeros up to 13 pairs, else normal with the tie correction.

Effect sizes: the mean difference with a BCa bootstrap interval (Efron 1987; bias correction z₀ = Φ⁻¹(#{θᵦ < θ̂} + #{θᵦ ≤ θ̂}) / 2B), acceleration from the jackknife, a = Σ U³ / (6 (Σ U²)^{3/2}), the two samples' jackknife values summed as SciPy does; seeded; percentile when BCa is undefined); Cohen's d (pooled σ) and Hedges' g = J d with the exact J(df) = Γ(df/2) / (√(df/2) Γ((df − 1)/2)), or, paired, d_z = mean(d) / sd(d); their intervals from the noncentral t (Steiger and Fouladi 1997: the λ with P(T ≤ t_obs | λ) = 1 − α/2 and α/2, scaled back); Cliff's delta P(B > A) − P(B < A) = 2U/(n₁n₂) − 1 (the rank-biserial correlation, Kerby 2014) with a seeded percentile bootstrap interval; the matched-pairs rank-biserial (T⁺ − T⁻)/(T⁺ + T⁻).

A comparison that is not clear never reads as "equal". Instead of the observed power (which only restates the p-value, Hoenig and Heisey 2001), it gives the smallest difference the data detect with the target power: the d with power_t(d, n) = 0.8 times the sd (paired: of the differences), or, for rates, the rates below and above the reference that the normal approximation detects at that n.

Several figures or comparisons

p-values are corrected across the figures (or the rate comparisons) of one analysis: Holm (default; step-down, p̃₍ᵢ₎ = max_{j ≤ i} min(1, (m − j + 1) p₍ⱼ₎), the family-wise error rate), Bonferroni (min(1, m p)), Benjamini-Hochberg (step-up, p̃₍ᵢ₎ = min_{j ≥ i} min(1, m p₍ⱼ₎ / j), the false discovery rate under independence or positive dependence), Benjamini-Yekutieli (BH times c(m) = Σ 1/i, any dependence) or none. A figure with no p-value (too few values) is left out of m.

Power and sample size

Two proportions, two-sided at alpha, equal n per condition:

  • normal (Fleiss, Levin and Paik 2003): n = [z_{α/2} √(2 p̄ q̄) + z_β √(p₁q₁ + p₂q₂)]² / Δ², with the continuity correction n′ = n/4 (1 + √(1 + 4/(nΔ)))² (default on). Its power at a given n inverts the correction (n = (n′ − 1/Δ)² / n′) and uses Φ((Δ√n − z_{α/2}√(2p̄q̄)) / √(p₁q₁ + p₂q₂)) plus the other tail;
  • arcsine (Cohen 1988): h = 2 asin √p₂ − 2 asin √p₁, n = 2 ((z_{α/2} + z_β) / h)²;
  • exact (the default of the episodes calculator): every outcome (x₁, x₂) of the two binomials enumerated, each tested (Fisher's exact test, the rejection region found per total m from the hypergeometric probabilities; or the z test with Yates' correction), power = Σ Bin(x₁; n, p₁) Bin(x₂; n, p₂) 1[reject] (up to 2 000 per condition; the normal approximation above, said);
  • simulation: seeded draws of both binomials, the same test applied.

The power of an exact test saw-tooths with n (the counts are discrete): the smallest n that reaches the target is reported, found by bisection and a scan of the 20 n below. For 70 % against 95 % at alpha 0.05 and power 0.80 the three methods give 39 (exact, Fisher), 43 (normal with the continuity correction) and 32 (arcsine) episodes per condition: the arcsine formula is the least conservative at rates near 1.

t tests through the noncentral t: two-sample df = 2n − 2, λ = d √(n/2); paired df = n − 1, λ = d_z √n; power = P(T > t_c | λ) + P(T < −t_c | λ). Where SciPy's noncentral t gives NaN far in a tail the normal approximation is used (the power there is 0 or 1 to many digits).

Evaluation episodes (episodes_needed(p1, p2)): the two-proportion n with the assumptions spelled out: every episode an independent trial with its condition's success probability (no drift, no learning, no two episodes failing together because they share a scene or a reset), the same number per condition, a two-sided test, and p₁, p₂ the rates to tell apart (a pilot's estimate is itself uncertain: plan for the smallest difference that matters).

Demonstrations per condition (demonstrations_needed): to tell a figure measured on each demonstration apart between two conditions (two rigs, two setups): a per-demonstration value (completion time, a quality score: the t test's n, paired when a scene is recorded under both) or pass/fail (the two-proportion n); then inflated by Kish's (1965) design effect 1 + (m − 1) ρ for m demonstrations per operator with intra-class correlation ρ (default 0: independent demonstrations), and by 1 / (1 − attrition) for the share the audit discards (default 0). This is not the number of demonstrations a policy needs to learn the task (that depends on the policy and the task and is not modelled).

Defaults (DexVantage defaults, labelled so in the UI and the results): alpha 0.05, power 0.80, confidence 0.95, 9 999 bootstrap resamples and permutations, seed 0, Holm's correction, 32 tolerance draws per variant.

The result, the headline and the suggestion

The study's result: the comparisons (each: the test and why, both sides, the difference with its interval, the effect sizes with intervals, the reference tests, p and the corrected p, the verdict), the correction, the pairing, the assumptions, the power calculation with its curve, references and the out-of-scope note. The headline is the comparison with the smallest corrected p, as its difference B − A (rates in percentage points), status clear difference (good: corrected p ≤ alpha), no clear difference (warning) or cannot test (critical: too few values); with only the power calculator, n per condition with enough planned / underpowered / needed.

The suggestion (the page's Apply, one undo step): when the headline difference is not clear, the episodes per condition (rates), draws per variant (variants) or runs (experiments, tracker) that would detect the observed difference with the target power, written into the settings. It plans from the observed difference, itself uncertain.

Tolerances and why: closed forms and exact distributions computed by other code agree to rounding (10⁻⁹); Monte Carlo comparisons are judged in their standard errors (4: a false alarm about once in 15 000 per comparison); the bootstrap's 0.25 comes from measurement (over 30 seeds each endpoint's standard deviation was 0.022 to 0.045 bootstrap standard errors in either implementation, the mean difference under 0.01, so two independent runs differ with a standard deviation under 0.06); Cohen's table rounds to the nearest unit and comes from approximations (for d = 0.2 at alpha 0.05 the exact noncentral t needs 394, as GPower also gives, the table 393), so one per group. Known limits

Fixed-sample tests only (no sequential testing or early stopping). Episodes and runs are taken as independent; the pairing is what the source states (by seed, by run, by the other factors). Power is computed for success rates and for normal data with equal variances; the permutation and rank tests' own power is not (their asymptotic relative efficiency against the t test is at least 0.864, Hodges and Lehmann 1956), so the t-based plan is an approximation for them. The operators' clustering is only Kish's design effect with equal cluster sizes. BCa intervals are poor with fewer than about 10 values. A difference that is not clear is not shown to be absent: read the smallest detectable difference. Corrections apply within one analysis, not across analyses run separately. The variants' Monte Carlo inherits the tolerance model's limits (docs/models/tolerance.md), and its default 32 draws per variant re-run every study the figures need.
