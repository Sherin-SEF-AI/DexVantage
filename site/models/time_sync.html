Clocks and streams (project.rig['sync'])

{'clocks':  [{'uid', 'name', 'kind': camera_hw | host | ptp | ntp | robot,
              'offset_s', 'jitter_s', 'drift_ppm'}],        each {'value', 'source'} or null (not stated)
 'streams': {sensor uid | 'joint_states': {'clock': clock uid ('' = host arrival), 'trigger': free | hardware | sync_cable}}}

The section is only written when it holds something, so an untouched rig keeps its old form. A stream with no entry is stamped by the host when its frame arrives and runs free; this is the default, and the result says so. Times are on the host's time base. A clock's offset is its reading minus the host's.

Stamp error of a camera frame

e = stamp − the instant the frame was exposed, taken as the middle of the middle row's exposure (Liang et al. 2008). Each term is an interval [low, high], and the budget is their sum:

Stamping Terms
Host arrival (no clock or a host clock), free or on a sync cable E/2 (exposure centre to its end) + T_r/2 (rolling shutter: the middle row to the last) or T_r (global shutter: rows read after the exposure) + USB transfer [bits/C_link, (bits + one frame of every other stream on the controller)/C_controller] + bus interval [0, 125 µs]
Device clock (camera_hw, ptp, ntp) ±E/2: which instant of the exposure the stamp marks is not stated
Hardware trigger (only where the maker states one) −E/2, and −T_r/2 for a rolling shutter: the stamp is the trigger edge. Transfer and processing no longer count
Measured latency (settings latency_s) the measured figure replaces exposure, readout, transfer and processing
Any clock but the host's its offset (a constant), ±jitter, and drift × 10⁻⁶ × episode_s (60 s by default)

Each input has a stated source:

  • Exposure E and readout T_r: from the motion blur study's imaging. That is your figure, else the library entry's, else the frame period as an upper bound. An exposure that is not fixed (auto-exposure) spans from the shortest the maker states (0 when none is stated) to that bound.
  • USB transfer: from the bandwidth study's topology. Bits are width × height × bits per pixel × (1 + overhead), with the 5 % overhead default. C_link is the lower of the device's and the controller's USB generation, at the USB 3.2 §4.4.11 effective rate or the controller's stated practical figure. When a sensor is not assigned to a controller, only the lower end is known, and the budget is flagged incomplete.
  • Bus interval: "For high speed and SuperSpeed, the bus interval is a microframe. The length of a microframe is 125 microseconds" (Microsoft, How to transfer data to USB isochronous endpoints, 2024-01-17, read 2026-11-07; USB 2.0 §5.8.4).
  • Processing inside the device: not stated by any maker in the library (a depth camera computes depth before it sends). A host-stamped budget is therefore a lower bound, said "at least" or "incomplete", until you enter a measured latency.
  • PTP and NTP figures: never assumed. Enter what your servo reports (for example ptp4l's offset or chronyc tracking) with its source. A figure not entered is "not stated".
  • Hardware trigger support: from the library entry's sync block (hardware_trigger, sync_cable, the imagers it reaches, the maker's words). For the D435, only the depth sensor takes the external trigger (D400 datasheet §3.7.4); for the D405, "D405 does not support the hardware sync signal" (§7.13). Setting a trigger on an imager the maker says takes none counts that imager as free-running, and says so. The page refuses the setting.

joint_states

joint_states has its own budget:

  • Stamped by the host on arrival (the default): the latency from the robot controller to the host is not stated.
  • Stamped by a robot, ptp or ntp clock: that clock's figures.
  • Measured: a figure in latency_s['joint_states'].

A frame's label is joint_states at the frame's stamp. With pairing interpolate (the default) the label is interpolated. With nearest it is the nearest sample, which adds ±1/(2r); r is joint_rate_hz, with its source.

Skew, pairs and the action-label error

skew = e_camera − e_joint = [e_c,low − e_j,high,  e_c,high − e_j,low]
  • Worst skew: the interval's largest magnitude.
  • Spread: the interval's width. That is what remains after a constant latency is subtracted.
  • Camera pairs: two free-running cameras matched by timestamp are also up to half the longer frame period apart. A shared sync cable, or a hardware trigger on the same clock, removes that.

The action-label error is the end effector's largest displacement during the skew:

max over t of |p(t + δ) − p(t)|,  δ at both ends of the skew interval

p is the TCP (grasps.gripper_geometry). It comes from forward kinematics (RobotModel.fk_array) of q(t) on a 1 ms grid, linear between grid points. The motions are the motion blur study's.

Status and recommendation

The status compares the worst skew with skew_limit_s (2 ms by default; the spec's figure, a DexVantage default, not a standard):

  • within the limit: "in sync";
  • up to twice the limit: "tight";
  • beyond that: "out of sync";
  • when a term is not stated: "out of sync" with the figure shown as a lower bound (≥), or "incomplete".

The recommendation takes the first of these that applies:

  1. A hardware trigger for the stream with the worst skew whose maker supports one. The predicted skew and action-label error come from running the study again with the trigger set. Apply sets it in project.rig['sync'] (one undo step).
  2. A sync cable between two free-running cameras that both support one.
  3. Advice: measure the arrival latency, or stamp the frames on a PTP-disciplined clock.

Known limits

  • Device processing: the makers do not state the processing time inside a device. A host-stamped budget is a lower bound until you enter a measured latency.
  • USB model: a USB transfer is modelled as the stream's frame at the link's capacity, queued behind at most one frame of every other device on its controller. Isochronous reservations, driver buffering and the host's interrupt moderation are not modelled.
  • Clocks: clock figures are what you enter; no servo is simulated.
  • Trigger delay: the delay from a hardware trigger to the exposure is not stated and not counted.
  • End effector: the TCP stands for the end effector, so an object held off-centre moves differently when the gripper turns.
  • Other joints: the robot's other joints stay at home.
