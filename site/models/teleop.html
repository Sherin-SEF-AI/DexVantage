Teleop overlap and follower dexterity

Spec §7.4. Module dexvantage/core/studies/teleop.py (study teleop, batch flag --teleop OUT.json, or --teleop-overlap OUT.json, the specification's name), page dexvantage/ui/pages/teleop.py (Analysis ▸ Teleop overlap, the Teleop workspace), checks dexvantage/validation/checks_teleop.py.

The question: where in the task volume can a teleoperator drive the follower robot, and where is the operator comfortable while the follower reaches badly (a dead zone: near a singularity, a joint limit or itself, or not at all)?

Teleop devices

project.tasks.teleop_devices holds one record per device (the Phase 5 shared contract; teleop.clean_device normalises it):

Field Meaning
kind leader_arm, gello, vr, spacemouse, glove or hand_tracker (the last two cartesian like VR: their volume is the wrist tracker's or the tracker's own)
follower_uid the robot of the project the device drives
leader {'robot_uid'}: a robot of the project whose kinematics the leader has (the follower's own uid: a leader of the same model); {'library_id'}: a library teleop entry that is not stated to lead this follower (it carries figures, no kinematics: the study says so and asks for the leader as a robot); null
library_id optional: the library teleop entry the device was made from (below)
mapping kind joint or cartesian, scale, offset (a pose of the device frame in the follower's base frame), joint_map {leader joint: follower joint}
volume VR tracking volume or space-mouse bounds in the device frame: a box (size = three edges) or a cylinder (size = [radius, height], axis z); null = not stated
source where each device figure comes from (a URL and the date read, or "measured")

Nothing is invented. A VR headset's tracking volume or a space mouse's bounds are not published as volumes by their makers; a new VR or space-mouse device has volume: null, and the study says the device's workspace is not stated until the user enters a measured or cited one. A leader arm or GELLO leads with a robot model of the project; for the SO-100 kit and GELLO that model is the follower's own:

  • LeRobot's SO-100 guide (https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so100, read 2026-11-07) builds the leader arm with the follower's assembly steps 1–23 (through the wrist; a handle and trigger replace the gripper) and calibrates both arms "to ensure that the leader and follower arms have the same position values when they are in the same physical position". The SO-100 sample carries this device (SO-100 leader arm (sample)).
  • GELLO (Wu et al. 2023, arXiv:2309.13037): "Given a target robot arm, we build a GELLO controller device that has the same kinematic structure as the target arm". Its joint ranges and link lengths are not stated in the abstract; the follower's model is used.

How a record is made from an entry (nothing is invented):

  • Leader arms and GELLO map joint to joint. When the entry is stated to lead the follower robot (leads: words matched against the robot's name and model file, ignoring case, spaces, "-" and "_"; GELLO's "*" leads any arm), the leader is the follower's own model. With same_joints true (SO-100, SO-101, GELLO) the source states the leader has the follower's joints; with false (Koch v1.1, ALOHA's WidowX) only the angles are mapped one to one, and the leader's comfortable box is taken from the follower's ranges, an ASSUMPTION written into the record's source.leader. A leader not stated to lead the follower gets {'library_id'}, and the study asks for the leader arm as a robot.
  • VR headsets, space mice, gloves and hand trackers are cartesian. Their volume is the entry's stated workspace; no maker states one as a box or cylinder (the Leap Motion's is a 160° cone from 10 to 110 cm, a headset's play area is not a tracking volume, a glove's wrist is tracked by another system), so the record's volume is null and the study says "not stated" until a measured or cited volume is entered on the page.
  • Every stated figure goes into the record's source with its URL, the date read and the quote; the record keeps library_id, the page shows which figures the maker does not state, and the study's device rows carry the id.

The comfortable workspace in the follower's frame

Joint mapping (leader arm, GELLO). For each joint_map entry (default: joints of the same name), q_f[j_f] = scale · q_l[j_l]; follower joints the map leaves out are held at home. The leader is comfortable inside the central comfort_fraction of each joint's range (default 0.8, a DexVantage default; not a human-factors measurement): [mid − f·(hi − lo)/2, mid + f·(hi − lo)/2], an unlimited revolute joint home ± f·π. The comfortable workspace in the follower's frame is

W = { FK_f(q) : q ∈ scale · C }       C = the leader's comfortable box

A task voxel is comfortable when the follower's damped-least-squares IK (core/robot/ik.py solve_batch, position target, orientation free) converges with the joint bounds replaced by scale · C. The bounds are deliberately not cut to the follower's own limits: a mapped range that runs past a follower stop leaves the follower at the stop while the leader is comfortable, a joint-limit dead zone.

Cartesian mapping (VR, space mouse, or an arm mapped by its tool position). A device-frame point p_d maps to the world as

p_world = B_f · O · (s · p_d)          B_f the follower's base, O the offset pose, s the scale

and back by p_d = (B_f · O)⁻¹ p_world / s. A voxel is comfortable when its centre maps back inside the device's box or cylinder, or, for an arm, when the leader's IK (inside its comfortable box) reaches p_d in the leader's base frame. The follower then solves its own IK.

The follower's measures

The task volumes are voxelised with core/voxels.py (SparseGrid, voxel_mm default 25 mm, no refinement: every voxel is an IK solve), plus search_voxels (default 2) layers round each volume for the suggestion. At each voxel centre the follower's tool point (gripper frame and TCP from core/grasps.gripper_geometry) is solved by IK (position only; 4 seeds, 4 × 4 fresh seeds for a target none solves; the solution nearest home, as a retargeting IK started from home finds it; self-collision avoided). At the solution, with J the tool point's Jacobian over the chain's joints (MuJoCo mj_jac; the position rows by default, jacobian: 'full' for all six):

w   = Π σᵢ(J)                  = √det(J Jᵀ) when J has no more rows than columns   [Yoshikawa 1985]
                               = √det(Jᵀ J) otherwise (a planar arm in its plane)
1/κ = σ_min / σ_max            0 singular, 1 isotropic
d_lim = min_j 2·min(q_j − lo_j, hi_j − q_j)/(hi_j − lo_j)
        over the chain's joints whose Jacobian column exceeds min_column_mm (1 mm/rad): a wrist roll about the
        tool axis cannot block a position
d_self = the self-collision distance (core/robot/collision.py) over link pairs the chain's joints move against
         each other (the 2F-85's own linkage, 3.6 mm apart at every arm pose, is left out), capped at 100 mm

The full Jacobian mixes metres and radians, so its κ depends on the length unit (the usual caveat of Yoshikawa's measure); the position default is unit-consistent and matches the position-only reach.

Dead zones

A voxel the leader reaches comfortably is a dead zone when (labelled defaults, the user's settings):

Cause Test Default
unreachable no follower IK solution —
self-collision d_self < self_margin_mm 10 mm
singularity 1/κ < min_inverse_condition 0.1 (κ > 10)
joint limit d_lim < min_limit_distance 0.05 (within 2.5 % of the range from a stop)

A voxel's first cause in that order names it. Connected dead-zone voxels of one cause (6-neighbours, scipy.ndimage.label) form a zone, listed with its volume, its box and the follower's joint values at its worst voxel (the smallest distance, 1/κ or d_lim; for unreachable zones the voxel nearest the zone's centre). Teleoperable = comfortable and no cause. The headline is the share of task voxels teleoperable through at least one device; status "clear" at 90 % or more, "gaps" from 50 %, "poor" below (labelled bands).

Bimanual

With two or more follower robots among the devices, for each pair: the share of the task volume both reach, the share teleoperable by both, and a handover test at the voxels of handover task volumes (else up to handover_samples, default 60, of the voxels both reach, seed 0): each arm reaches a point handover_gap_mm (100 mm) apart along the line between their bases; the handover quality is the share where both reach without a dead-zone cause and keep arm_margin_mm (20 mm) apart. The arm-to-arm margin is the smallest distance between the two arms' colliding-geometry vertices (about 4 000 per arm, scipy.spatial.cKDTree): an upper bound of the true distance by up to the vertex spacing, and a margin near zero may be an overlap.

The suggestion

A follower's dexterity map and its devices' comfortable regions are rigid with its base, so moving the base by whole voxels s·h shifts them over the task voxels: the teleoperable share after the move is read from the grid at index − s without new IK. A cartesian device's mapping offset shifts only its comfortable region. Every move within ±search_voxels per axis is scored; the one that most raises the headline (fewer dead-zone voxels, then the smaller move, break ties) is suggested with the predicted share and dead-zone volume. Apply makes it in one undo step (apply_suggestion): a non-primary robot's base moves; the primary robot's base is the world frame, so its move is applied as the opposite move of the task volumes, objects, surfaces, lights, fixed sensors and other robots (hand-eye board placements are not moved). A locked fixed sensor blocks that, with a sentence saying why.

Performance

Measured on the samples (batch, CPU, times include start-up): SO-100 sample (480 task voxels at 25 mm, 1 728 points with the search layers) 7.4 s; with a second, VR device 7.2 s; UR5e sample with no device 12.2 s, with a leader arm about 18–26 s (7 680 points, two IK passes). A bimanual pair of SO-100s with a 60-voxel handover test 15 s. Most of the time is the IK (MuJoCo Jacobians, one per seed and iteration). On the page every UI step stays under 3 ms (showing a UR5e result with every voxel a dead zone: 1.1 ms; its 46 080 overlay triangles: 2.4 ms); the study runs on the analysis controller's worker.

Known limits

Reach is position-only with a free orientation (a task that needs a given gripper orientation can be harder); the IK is local, so a voxel every seed misses reads as unreachable or outside the comfortable workspace; the comfortable fraction of the joint ranges is a default, not a human-factors model, and the comfortable workspace is a set of tool positions, not the operator's posture or speed; dexterity is first order at one pose; the suggestion searches lattice translations only (no rotations, no finer steps) and its prediction holds only to the voxel size; obstacles and other robots are not avoided by the reach (self-collision is); arm-to-arm distances are between mesh vertices (an upper bound); the SO-100's joint limits are Menagerie's approximate ones, so its comfortable box inherits that; the VR and space-mouse figures are whatever the user states, and a device without them is left out. Library devices: no maker states a tracking volume as a box or cylinder, so a library VR, glove, hand-tracker or space-mouse device reaches the study without a comfortable region until one is entered; the stated rates and latencies (a glove's sample rate and signal latency, a headset's photon-to-photon passthrough) are carried as figures with their sources and are not used by the reach model; a leader whose own joint ranges are not stated (Koch v1.1, ALOHA's WidowX) is given the follower's ranges, an assumption its record says; leads matches words of the robot's name and model file, so a renamed robot is not recognised (choose the leader by hand).
