Depth validity

Module: dexvantage/core/studies/depth_validity.py (study depth_validity, batch flag --depth-validity OUT.json). Page: dexvantage/ui/pages/depth.py (Analysis ▸ Depth validity). Checks: dexvantage/validation/checks_depth.py (model depth). Tests: tests/test_depth_validity.py. Spec §7.2 and the depth checks of §10.

It builds on the library (core/library: the device's imagers, depth block and projector, docs/models/library.md), the lens models (core/optics, docs/models/optics.md), the sparse voxels (core/voxels.py, docs/models/voxels.md) and the articulated occlusion (core/scenegeom.py, core/occlusion.py, docs/models/occlusion.md).

The five conditions

A voxel (its centre) is depth-valid for a depth sensor when all five hold. They are tested in this order and the first that fails is the voxel's reason, so the reasons and the valid share add up to 100 % of the task volumes. Shares are volume weighted (refined voxels are smaller). Voxels whose centre lies inside a solid task object (box, cylinder or sphere) are left out of the task volumes, as in the coverage study: no sensor measures a solid's inside, and counted they would all read "occluded" (voxels.inside_objects in the result counts them).

# Reason id Condition Where its figures come from
1 fov Inside the field of view of every imager the technology needs: both imagers of a stereo pair (active or passive); the pattern camera and the projector cone for structured light; the ToF imager for time of flight library.sensor_imagers at the chosen depth resolution (a pinhole fitted to the stated depth FOV unless the unit is calibrated)
2 min_z, max_range Depth z (along the reference imager's optical axis, Intel's depth convention) at least min-Z for the chosen resolution and at most the maximum range the entry's min_z table (else its stated min_range_m, labelled "not per resolution"); max_range_by_mode for the mode, else max_range_m
3 occluded Seen by every needed imager: no robot link, task object or surface between the voxel and the imager's optical centre one cube map per imager centre (occlusion.CACHE), from scenegeom.sensor_occluders at q
4 projector Inside the IR projector's cone, for active sensors that need its texture the entry's projector (horizontal and vertical FOV and pose)
5 material Not on a task object tagged transparent, ir_absorbing or specular (glossy, metallic) TaskObject.materials

Notes on each:

  1. Stereo overlap. Requiring both imagers is the overlap of their frusta. Intel's D400 datasheet (§4.4) gives its horizontal extent at distance Z as Depth FOV = HFOV/2 + atan(tan(HFOV/2) − B/Z) with the left imager as the reference; the check depth.fov_overlap_formula compares the two. The left edge of the overlap is Intel's "invalid depth band" (§4.5): near the camera, the left part of the depth image has no match in the right image. The spec says "the RGB and projector FOVs" for structured light; the camera that sees the pattern makes the depth, so the reference imager of the entry is used (no structured-light device is in the library yet).
  2. Range. A figure the maker does not state is not tested, and the result says so (for example the D405 at 640 × 480, which Table 4-12 gives as "-"). OAK's min_z_extended (extended-disparity mode) is not used.
  3. Occlusion from either imager covers finger shadows and the parallax occlusion of a stereo pair: a voxel seen by one imager and hidden from the other has no depth. The result splits the occluded volume into "hidden from one imager only" and "hidden from every imager". The mount link stays an occluder (a wrist camera sees its own gripper); triangles of it within 25 mm of an imager centre are its seat (scenegeom.OWN_RADIUS_M, a default).
  4. Projector need is the per-sensor setting projector: 'auto' (default) turns it on for active IR stereo when the device's projector and its FOV are stated, and always for structured light (where it is part of condition 1); off for passive stereo and ToF (a ToF illuminator's field is not in the library). The D4xx projector's position in the device is not stated by Intel; the library places it at a labelled assumption, and the page says so.
  5. Materials are warnings with a reason: the geometry is fine, the surface may still give no depth. IR-absorbing warns only for the active technologies (active IR stereo, structured light, ToF), whose depth comes from their own IR light. A voxel is "on" an object inside the object's shape grown by half the voxel's diagonal (a mesh: its bounding box so grown). The result gives each object's share of the volume that is otherwise depth-valid, and the depth-valid share with and without the warnings (valid_pct, valid_geometric_pct).

Expected depth error

For stereo, z = f B / d; a disparity error σ_d propagates to first order as

σ_z = z² · σ_d / (f · B)

[Hartley and Zisserman 2004, §12; Gallup et al. 2008]. f is the reference imager's horizontal focal length in pixels at the chosen depth resolution (disparity is horizontal), B the stated baseline and σ_d the matcher's subpixel disparity error: the entry's subpixel_px when the maker states it, else the setting sigma_d_px, 0.08 px by default (a default, not a device figure; no D4xx entry states one). The result reports the median and the 5th and 95th percentiles over the depth-valid voxels (volume weighted), written median [p5–p95], and σ_z / z.

Against the maker. Where the entry states an RMS figure as a percentage at a distance, the page shows both: the maker's bound (for example the D405's "RMS error ≤ 1 % at ≤ 0.5 m", Intel Table 4-15, so ≤ 5 mm at 0.5 m) and the model at that distance, with the σ_d that would make the model match the maker. They are not the same quantity: Intel's figure is a plane-fit RMS over 80 % of the field of view at HD resolution under its test conditions (a textured target in room light for the D405, a white wall with the projector for the others), an upper bound; the model is a per-point σ for an ideal matcher. On the UR5e sample at the default 0.08 px the model gives 1.56 mm for the D405 at 0.5 m (the bound is 5 mm; σ_d = 0.26 px would match it) and 9.5 mm for the D435 at 2 m (bound 40 mm; σ_d = 0.34 px). ToF has no stereo error model: the maker's figure is shown alone.

Not every maker's figure is an RMS. The entry's rms_error.metric says what it is: rms (Intel, and Orbbec's Gemini 2 and 335/336 "RMS distance ... to a fitted plane"), median_abs (Luxonis: "median ... absolute depth error"), std_dev (Orbbec Femto Bolt, in millimetres) or unspecified (Stereolabs: "Depth Accuracy" with no metric defined; also any entry without the field). The page shows the figure with its metric, and the σ_d that would match it only for rms; for the others that column reads "not an RMS".

What the result holds

Per sensor: the resolution and whether it is the default, min-Z and maximum range with where they come from, f, B, σ_d (and whether it is the default), the projector need and why, each needed imager's label (what its camera model is and which of its figures are assumptions), the depth-valid share, the breakdown by first failing reason, the share failing each condition among the voxels in view, the occlusion split, per-volume shares, σ_z, the maker's figure beside the model, the material warnings, the depth of the robots' fingertip frames against min-Z, a sample of up to 3000 invalid voxels per reason for the 3D view, and its world pose. For the rig: the share depth-valid for at least one sensor (the page's headline). A suggestion (Apply on the page): the depth mode of the same aspect ratio with a smaller stated min-Z that makes the most task volume depth-valid (estimated from the voxels that fail min-Z alone), or, when none gains volume, the largest one whose min-Z clears fingertips that sit inside min-Z; ties go to the larger resolution because σ_z grows as the focal length in pixels shrinks.

The min-Z blind zone in the 3D view

blind_zone_local(setup) is the part of the reference imager's field of view nearer than min-Z: a pyramid from the optical centre to the plane z = min-Z, its edges sampled on the image border. The Depth page registers it as an overlay source (ctx.overlay_sources), so it is drawn on every page, translucent in the sensor's colour, from the sensor's world pose at the window's joint state: a wrist camera's zone moves with the wrist. View ▸ Depth min-Z zones turns it off. No zone is drawn when min-Z is not stated for the chosen resolution.

Known limits

The voxel centre stands for the voxel. Occlusion is per cube-map texel from each imager's optical centre (points within about a texel of a silhouette may be judged either way, docs/models/occlusion.md); no sensor housing is an occluder. The projector's own shadow is not modelled, only its cone, and the D4xx projector's position is a labelled assumption. The imagers at a depth stream take the stated depth FOV, which Intel states as the overlap at one distance (0.2 m for the D405 and 2 m for the D415, D43x and D455 in the D400 datasheet, 4 m for the D457 in its own), so the modelled overlap is narrower than the one the stated figure implies: by 2.9–3.6° for the D405 and 0.7–1.9° for the others from min-Z outwards; Intel gives about ±3° unit to unit. min-Z is measured from the optical centre, not the front glass (Intel's depth start point is a few millimetres away). Materials are tags, not optics: no reflectance, multipath, sunlight or IR wash-out (§7.8). σ_z is first order in σ_d / d and leaves out calibration error, matcher bias, quantisation and the matcher's dependence on texture. Lens distortion is what the library states (a pinhole unless the unit is calibrated). The Camera view overlay of the depth-valid mask is not drawn yet.
