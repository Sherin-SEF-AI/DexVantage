Visual-to-tactile handoff and the sensory blind gap (spec §7.15)

Part 2 of dexvantage/core/studies/dex_hand_tactile.py (the multi-finger occlusion study, --dex-occlusion; the result's handoff key; tactile_handoff(project) runs it alone) · the chart on Analysis ▸ Multi-finger occlusion · check dexhand.blind_gap_bruteforce (dexvantage/validation/checks_chunk.py).

The question: as the hand closes in on the object, when do the cameras lose sight of each fingertip's contact region, and does a tactile pad take over at contact? The distance before contact over which a contact is neither seen nor touched is the sensory blind gap.

The approaches

They end at the study's contact cases: each grasp of a grasp set reached by IK, or each grasp posture fully closed (at most handoff_max_cases, default 6, evenly spaced). Along each, the gripper frame is backed off along −approach (the grasp's approach, else the gripper's approach axis, core/grasps.py gripper_geometry, turned with the frame) by a distance d, and the arm is solved by IK from the solution at the nearer distance (nearest_q0, one seed, self-collision not checked; the hand keeps its contact posture), as the grasp study poses its pre-contact steps.

At each d, part 1's kernel (visibility_at: exact ray casting by default, owner groups, the cameras' lens models) gives each fingertip's share of its contact patch that some camera sees. Along the approach the task objects occlude (handoff_objects_occlude, default on: the object is what hides the contact as the fingers close on it). A fingertip is seen from min_visible_pct (50 %, a default).

Distances: 0 to handoff_max_mm (default 50 mm, the grasp study's pre-contact default) every handoff_step_mm (10 mm), then every handoff_refine_mm (2 mm) inside each coarse interval where a fingertip's seen state changes.

The tactile pads at contact

Every tactile sensor in use on a link of a finger covers that finger's patch points within pad_gap_mm (3 mm) of its sensing plane and inside its pad, pad_mm from the library entry (GelSight Mini 18.6 × 14.3 mm, DIGIT 19 × 16 mm, both cited; not stated → coverage not known). A pad left at its link's origin (the mount pose not set, as the Library adds one) is assumed centred on the patch, facing along its mean normal, its longer side along the patch's longer extent; the result labels it. A placed pad senses along its mount's body x axis (REP-103 forward), its sides along body y and z. A fingertip is touched from min_touch_pct (50 %) of its patch on a pad.

The gap

From (distance, seen) samples of a fingertip:

gap = 0                                  when seen at contact
    = (d_last_hidden + d_first_seen) / 2  otherwise
    = d_max ("or more")                   when never seen

and the hidden total counts every stretch not seen, a change of state at the midpoint between samples. Touch begins only at contact, so a fingertip neither seen nor touched at contact is unsensed at contact whatever its gap. Flags: a gap above blind_gap_max_mm (default 10 mm) or an unsensed contact. Status: good with no flag, warning for flagged gaps up to 2 × the threshold, serious above, critical when a contact is unsensed (defaults).

Validation (measured 2026-11-07)

dexhand.blind_gap_bruteforce: the dexhand sample with only its head camera and a 4 mm plate between it and the hand, its top edge where the line of sight to the patches' centre passes 20 mm before contact; the fully closed power posture, 0 to 40 mm. The adaptive scan (13 visibility evaluations) against a scan of every distance at 2 mm (21 evaluations): 0 mm difference over the five fingertips, two transitions (the middle finger lost at 19 mm, the ring finger at 37 mm; the first finger, little finger and thumb not seen over the whole 40 mm); tolerance one refine step, 2 mm, because both read the same kernel at the same distances where they meet and can differ only where a short seen or hidden stretch falls between two coarse samples.

On the samples

The dexhand sample (no task object, no tactile sensor): its three closed postures are seen by the head camera up to contact (gap 0 mm, status good), and the notes say no tactile sensor is on the fingers. With a DIGIT on every fingertip and the plate above, the first three fingers are touched at contact (72–78 % of the patch on the pad), the little finger's patch is 48 % covered and the thumb's 11 % (its patch wraps round the distal link, wider than the 19 × 16 mm pad), so those two stay unsensed at contact: critical, and the flags name them. Time: about 6 s for three approaches (18 evaluations, exact ray casting on the CPU; no GPU is used by the default backend), on top of part 1's 4.5 s; --dex-occlusion on the sample takes about 12 s with start-up.

Known limits

The approach is a straight line in the gripper frame's position with the hand already in its contact posture (the fingers do not close as it comes in); a seen or hidden stretch shorter than the coarse step inside one interval can be missed (the check measures it on one scene); a distance IK cannot reach is left out and counted; touch is judged only at contact and by geometry (a pad's footprint on the patch), not by contact force or the sensor's sensitivity; an assumed pad placement is a guess the result labels, and only the pad's mount pose says where it really is.
