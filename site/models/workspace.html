The workspace check

dexvantage/core/workspace_check.py (no Qt), shown on the Workspace page (dexvantage/ui/pages/workspace.py, Workspace ▸ Workspace). Validation model workspace.

What it answers

The coverage, depth-validity, grasp and VLA-token studies measure inside the project's task volumes (project.workspace.volumes: axis-aligned boxes in the world frame). A task object outside every task volume is therefore not measured by them. The check asks one question: does every task object lie inside a task volume? If not, it suggests the smallest change that makes it true.

The model

For each task object the world triangles come from core/scenegeom.object_triangles (a box's 12 triangles, a cylinder's and a sphere's tessellation of 24 segments around and 12 rings pole to pole, a mesh object's own mesh). Its axis-aligned world bounding box is

lo = min over vertices v of v,    hi = max over vertices v of v.

The object is inside volume V (centre c, size s) when

lo ≥ c − s/2 − τ   and   hi ≤ c + s/2 + τ   (each axis),

with τ = INSIDE_TOL_M = 1 mm (a DexVantage default: rounding of typed positions). The headline is the number of task volumes with a status word:

Word When
ready at least one volume, and every object (with geometry) inside one
objects outside some object lies outside every volume
no task volume the project has none

The suggestion (Apply, one undo step). With objects outside, let B be the union of their boxes grown by MARGIN_M = 20 mm (a DexVantage default); the volume whose centre is nearest B's centre grows to the union of its own box and B. With no volume, a new tabletop volume is B around every object, or the default volume of core/project.Volume (500 × 600 × 300 mm centred at (0.45, 0, 0.15) m, a default to move to your table) when there are no objects. When the objects stand on or above the floor (z = 0), the margin stops at the floor.

Validation (measured, 2026-11-07)

dexvantage --validate out --models workspace:

Check Reference Tolerance and why Measured
workspace.object_extent the exact box of the true shape: a turned box's half-extents |R| h (Arvo 1990); a cylinder's r √(1 − a_i²) + (h/2)|a_i| per world axis i, with a the cylinder's axis in the world; a sphere's r; rotations drawn by SciPy, not core/frames 1.0 of the tessellation bound: a box is exact to rounding (10⁻¹² m); a tessellated cylinder or sphere lies inside the true shape, so its box may fall short by up to r (1 − cos²(π/24)) = 1.70 % of the radius and never stick out 0.861 of the bound over 300 random objects (largest shortfall 0.66 mm, a sphere; cylinders 0.49 mm; boxes 1.1 × 10⁻¹³ mm); quick run 0.861 over 30
workspace.inside_classification the exact classification of the true shapes' boxes in three random volumes 0 objects: every object is clear of each face by at least 3 mm, more than τ plus the 1.7 % shortfall of a 60 mm radius, so any disagreement is a fault 0 of 240 (132 inside, 108 outside); quick run 0 of 30

The self-test line workspace runs both quick checks.

Known limits

  • Volumes are axis-aligned boxes, so a suggested volume around a turned object is larger than the object.
  • The inside test uses the object's axis-aligned bounding box: a long thin object turned 45° can be called outside although its own surface lies inside.
  • An object whose geometry cannot be read (an empty or missing mesh) is not checked; the advice names it.
  • The cylinder's and sphere's tessellation makes their boxes up to 1.7 % of the radius small (measured above).

References

  • J. Arvo, "Transforming axis-aligned bounding boxes", in A. S. Glassner (ed.), Graphics Gems, Academic Press, 548–550, 1990 (arvo1990).
  • SciPy scipy.spatial.transform.Rotation (scipy_rotation).
