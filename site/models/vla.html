VLA token resolution and Plücker ray maps

dexvantage/core/studies/vla_tokens.py (study vla_tokens, batch --vla-tokens OUT.json), dexvantage/core/plucker.py (ray maps, the safetensors writer), the page dexvantage/ui/pages/vla.py, the checks dexvantage/validation/checks_vla.py (model vla). Spec §7.12.

A vision-language-action policy resizes or crops each camera stream to a fixed input (224 × 224 for π0 and OpenVLA, 256 × 256 for Octo, 512 × 512 for SmolVLA) and slices it into ViT patches (14 or 16 px). An object that covers one or two patches gives the policy almost nothing to condition on. The study counts, per policy and per camera the policy takes, the tokens each task object and each fingertip covers at the pre-grasp and contact poses of a grasp set, and warns when a critical object spans fewer than 2 × 2 tokens (a default) or falls outside the crop. The input resolution, crop rule, patch and token count come from the policy (core/policies.py resolve: the library entry, cited from the model card, paper or official code, or the user's measured figure; docs/models/policies.md).

The image → input mapping

Each camera is modelled at its stream (Sensor.stream, else the imager's largest listed stream) through its RGB imager (else its first; the Camera view's rule) and its lens model (optics.CameraModel). The policy's crop rule gives a CropGeometry (core/policies.py, validated by policies.crop_geometry against each rule's own code): the crop box in the sensor, src_rect = (x0, y0, x1, y1) in pixel-edge coordinates (OpenCV pixel u spans x = u to u + 1), the rectangle it fills in the input, dst_rect, and the scales sₓ, sᵧ (input pixels per sensor pixel):

x_in = dst_x0 + (u + ½ − x0) · sx,      y_in = dst_y0 + (v + ½ − y0) · sy

Every camera record of the result carries this mapping (mapping: crop_box_sensor_px in OpenCV pixel coordinates, scale_input_per_sensor_px, content_rect_input_px, the padding share and the equation written out). On the UR5e sample with π0 (resize_pad), the Overhead D435's 1920 × 1080 colour stream is used whole (u −0.5 … 1919.5) at 0.1167 input px per sensor px, filling rows 49 to 175 of the 224 × 224 input: 43.75 % of the input, and so of its tokens, is black padding.

The token grid

The input is sliced into square cells of patch_px: cols = ⌊w / p⌋, rows = ⌊h / p⌋ (a strided patch embedding drops a remainder). A policy whose entry states no patch but states tokens_per_image T gets the cell c = √(w h / T) when that is a whole number of pixels dividing both sides; it is labelled derived from tokens per image (it assumes the tokens tile the input on a square grid). The silhouette and the counts

A task object's world triangles (scenegeom.object_triangles) are projected through the lens and the mapping and rasterised at input resolution: an input pixel is covered when its centre lies inside a projected triangle (three edge functions with the triangle's winding, edges inclusive). A lens that bends straight lines (Brown-Conrady, the fisheyes) is handled by subdividing each triangle into four at its edge midpoints until every projected edge midpoint lies within raster_tol_px (default 0.01 input px) of the midpoint of its projected ends, at most max_subdiv levels (default 8). A triangle with a corner behind a pinhole camera or beyond the valid angle is split the same way; what is still invalid at the last level is dropped and named in the notes. The raster covers the whole camera image mapped into input coordinates, so the part of an object the camera sees but the crop cuts away is measured.

Per object, camera and pose: tokens touched (cells with at least one covered pixel), tokens at least half covered, covered input pixels, the extent (the silhouette's bounding box in input pixels divided by the cell: width × height in tokens), the share outside the crop (of what the camera sees of it), in view or not.

Warnings (critical objects only: critical_objects, default every task object):

  • spans fewer than 2 × 2 tokens (serious): its extent is below min_tokens_side (default 2, a default, not a standard) along either side at its worst pose. The extent, not the touched count, decides: a 3 px object straddling a cell corner touches 4 tokens but spans 0.2 × 0.2.
  • partly cropped (warning): more than crop_warn_fraction (default 1 %) of what the camera sees of it is cut away.
  • falls outside the crop (serious): the camera sees it, the input does not.
  • not in the input (critical): no camera of the policy has it in its input at any pose counted.

Fingertips: the contact pads' sample points (grasps.contact_points, 128 area-weighted points per pad by default): tokens touched are the cells holding a point inside the input; the extent is the points' bounding box.

The poses

With a grasp set (grasp_set, else the project's first), up to max_grasps (default 12) grasps evenly through the set, each at contact (the grasp's gripper pose) and pre-grasp (backed off pre_grasp_m along the approach, default 100 mm). The pads are carried rigidly from home with the gripper frame. A camera mounted on that robot moves with it: the arm is posed by IK of the grasp centre (ik.solve_batch with grasps.ik_targets' tool offset, 4 seeds, collision checks off); a pose IK does not reach is left out for that camera and counted in the notes. Without a grasp set the robots stand at home, or at the joint state passed in the run's settings (the page passes the pose on screen), and the notes say so.

Headline and suggestion

The headline is the fewest tokens a critical object touches in a camera that has it in its input (over every policy, camera and pose), with its extent; the status is the worst warning (resolved, partly cropped, too few tokens, not in the input). With no critical object counted at all (no task object, as on the samples) the headline is the fewest tokens on a fingertip and the status is a warning, no task object, never resolved. The suggestion tries each stream mode the camera's device lists (entry.streams(imager)) at the warned objects' worst poses and proposes the one under which the worst warned object's narrower extent grows most: a 4:3 stream padded into a square input wastes less of it than a 16:9 one. Apply sets Sensor.stream (one undo step).

Plücker ray maps

Ported from Vantage core/plucker.py (the per-pixel field of origin o, unit direction d through the exact lens and moment m = o × d, channel order dm or md, one file per camera plus provenance); here the grid is the policy's input, not a stride grid over the sensor, and the frame is the robot base, not ISO 8855.

Input pixel (row j, column i) has its centre at input edge coordinates (i + ½, j + ½); CropGeometry.to_source maps it to sensor edge coordinates (x, y), the sensor pixel is (u, v) = (x − ½, y − ½), CameraModel.unproject gives the optical ray, and the imager's pose at q turns it into the frame: base (default: the base of robot_uid, default the project's first robot, Robot.base), world, or camera (o = 0, so m = 0). Valid: inside the crop's content (not padding), inside the sensor image and within the lens's valid angle. Invalid pixels are zero in all six channels with valid = false. The mapping is separable, so u (w,) and v (h,) are stored as vectors.

export_plucker(project, out_dir, policy_uid, q=None, frame='base', dtype='float32', order='dm') writes, per camera the policy takes, plucker_<policy>_<camera>.npy ((h, w, 6)), .safetensors (tensors plucker, valid, u, v, origin, rotation; metadata schema, frame, order, convention) and .json (the convention, the lens, the crop with its sensor-pixel box, the share valid and padded, q, and the provenance block).

safetensors is written here (the package is optional): 8 bytes of header length N (unsigned little-endian 64-bit), N bytes of UTF-8 JSON starting with { and padded with spaces to a multiple of 8, mapping each name to {"dtype", "shape", "data_offsets": [begin, end]} relative to the byte buffer, plus __metadata__ (strings only); then the buffer, little-endian row-major, no holes [safetensors README "Format" and safetensors/src/tensor.rs, https://github.com/huggingface/safetensors, read 2026-11-06]. read_safetensors refuses holes, overlaps, trailing bytes and offsets that do not match the shape.

The reference of the first check is sensitive: with subdivision off (0 levels, seed 1) 162 pixels differ on the distorted lenses and one token count is off by one; at 0.05 px and 6 levels a few pixel centres within 0.05 px of an edge differ. OpenCV's undistortion returns x/z, y/z, which has no ray at or beyond 90° and is ill-conditioned close to it (0.018 apart at 89° on the scripted fisheye), so the ray check compares rays within 85° of the axis and reports how many it left out.

Known limits

Silhouettes are not occlusion-tested (an object hidden behind the gripper still counts its tokens: the grasp self-occlusion study treats visibility). Objects are their triangle models (cylinders and spheres tessellated as scenegeom does). Tokens are counted on the inference-time crop (training augmentation is not modelled) and on a square cell grid (other merging schemes are not modelled); interpolation filters are not modelled, so a token touched by a sliver of an object may carry little of it. Octo's 128 × 128 wrist input is listed by the Policies page but not applied per camera role. The fingertip pads are carried rigidly from home (the finger opening at home), and IK picks one arm configuration per grasp. A ray is that of its pixel's centre (no footprint averaging); ray maps are not masked by the scene; a wrist camera's map is for the pose it was exported at.
