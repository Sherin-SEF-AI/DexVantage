Multi-cell comparison

Several robot cells side by side: other labs' or benches' rigs (.dexv files or folders), samples, or variants of the open project. Code: dexvantage/core/cells.py (the cell set, the figures table, the geometric gap, the drift, the files), dexvantage/core/cells_pool.py (dataset pooling), dexvantage/core/studies/cells.py (the study cells), dexvantage/core/exporters/cells.py (the export cells), dexvantage/ui/pages/cells.py (File ▸ Compare cells…), dexvantage/batch_cells.py (--cells), dv.cells.compare in dexvantage/api.py, the assistant's get_cells.

The gap index is ported from Vantage core/rig_gap.py (the Rig Contrastive Distance) and the drift from Vantage core/fleet.py and the measurement idea of core/fleet_twin.py; the automotive parts (viewing directions around a vehicle, the ground in bird's-eye view, body occlusion, nuScenes and KITTI readers, Kalman drift forecasts) are not ported.

The cell set

The open project is cell 0 (its active variant); with variants on (the default) each of its other variants is a cell; then the cells the settings name, in order: {'path': ...} (a .dexv file or an unpacked folder), {'sample': name} or 'sample:<name>', {'variant': uid or name}. The reference (setting reference, default 0) is the cell the others are compared with, and the plan for the drift. Names are made unique with (2), (3).

1. Figures of merit

Every metric asked for (metrics: a list of ids, 'all' for every metric whose study reads no recorded dataset and runs no other study, or the default quick set DEFAULT_METRICS: coverage seen, seen twice and P5 resolution, depth validity, the sync skew, the payload, the camera and sensor counts, the rig mass and power — a default) is evaluated on every cell through core.metrics.evaluate (one result cache for the set). Per metric the table gives every cell's value (with its bound and the reason when it is not stated), the best and the worst cell among the plainly stated values (none when they are all equal), the spread, and per cell its difference Δ from the reference.

With tolerance_draws n ≥ 2 the tolerance Monte Carlo (core/tolerance.monte_carlo, seed seed) runs on every cell and a difference is called meaningful when

|Δ| > k · √(σ_ref² + σ_cell²),   k = 2 (MEANINGFUL_K, a default: about the two-sided 95 % band of a normal difference)

with σ the standard deviation of the metric over the cell's own draws (the spread the build tolerances alone give). When neither cell's figure moves under the draws (σ = 0) any non-zero difference is meaningful. A figure stated only as a bound is not judged. Without draws nothing is judged ("not assessed"). With core/stats installed, such a difference must also be significant over the draws: stats.compare_samples on the two cells' draws (the permutation test of the mean difference, 1 999 resamples here, with Hedges' g), the p-values Holm-adjusted across the whole table at α = 0.05 (core/stats' defaults); a difference beyond the spread but not significant is not meaningful (said).

2. The geometric gap

Each enabled camera or depth sensor contributes its picked imager (the RGB one of a depth camera, at the stream size), posed at the home pose, with a role:

  • wrist: mounted on a link a joint moves. Its optical frame is expressed in its robot's contact frame (core/exporters/canonical_warp.contact_frame: origin between the fingertips, z the approach axis, x the closing axis), so wrist cameras of different arms compare relative to the fingers. A robot without a contact frame falls back to the mount link (said in the camera's frame text). Which pad comes first is arbitrary, so the comparison also tries the other camera half a turn about the approach axis (F = diag(−1, −1, 1)) and keeps the closer reading (closing_flipped).
  • scene: static (world, cage, tripod, table, or a link no joint moves, such as the G1's torso). Its optical frame in the world (the primary robot's base).

Cameras are matched one to one within a role by the Hungarian method (scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment) on

cost = θ_axis / 90° + |c_a − c_b| / 0.5 m + |ΔHFOV| / 90° + 1 (different arms) + θ_rot / 1800°

Per matched pair:

Figure Definition
viewpoint angle θ_axis atan2(‖z_a × z_b‖, z_a · z_b) of the optical axes (exact near 0)
rotation θ_rot atan2 of the sine and cosine parts of R_aᵀ R_b (roll included)
distance ‖c_a − c_b‖ of the centres in the role's frame
target distance ratio wrist: distance to the fingertip centre; scene: to the task volumes' centre
HFOV difference and ratio the lens model's edge-to-edge field through the principal point (CameraModel.fov_deg)
resolution ratio pixels per degree at the centre, f_x · π / 180, cell over reference
task volume in the image share of the image whose rays pass through a task volume: rays through a grid of pixel centres at most 128 wide, unprojected through the lens, slab-tested (Kay and Kajiya 1986) against each volume's box
task volume in view share of the volumes' interior points (9 per edge) inside the image

Components, each in 0..1, means over the matched pairs:

viewpoint  = min(1, θ_axis / 90°)                 weight 0.30
position   = min(1, distance / 0.5 m)             weight 0.20   (Vantage matches on distance / 2 m around a vehicle)
resolution = min(1, |log2 ratio| / 2)             weight 0.15   (two octaves = 1)
coverage   = |Δ image share|                      weight 0.15
fov        = min(1, |ΔHFOV| / 90°)                weight 0.10
topology   = ½ (|n_a − n_b| / max(n_a, n_b) + unmatched cameras / (n_a + n_b))   weight 0.10

gap = Σ w_k c_k / Σ w_k ;   band: < 0.15 small (good), < 0.35 moderate (warning), else large (serious)

The weights, scales and bands are DexVantage's defaults. The bands (0.15, 0.35), the two-octave resolution scale and the 90° angle and FOV scales are Vantage's (core/rig_gap.py); the components and their weights are DexVantage's own (Vantage weights coverage 0.30, resolution 0.20, height and pitch 0.15, bird's-eye view 0.15, FOV 0.10 and topology 0.10 for vehicle rigs, with no viewpoint or position component; its 2 m is a matching cost only). The bands are not calibrated against any policy's success. The gap is symmetric and 0 for identical cells. A full matrix of every pair is in the result (gap.matrix).

3. Dataset pooling

Each cell's recording is a schema read from a LeRobot meta/info.json (the v2.1 / v3.0 feature schema of core/datasets/lerobot.py, from the LeRobot source at commit d490e47, read 2026-11-07): the cell's first recorded dataset (project.data.datasets, opened through core/datasets; an RLDS folder or a ROS 2 bag through its first episode) under pooling: auto (the default) or recorded, else the plan: the info.json its LeRobot export would write (core/exporters/lerobot.info_json over rig_streams and state_names), with the joint types and limits of the robot models, the policy's control rate and the streams' frame rates. The plan's codec is LeRobot's default encoder (AV1 through libsvtav1), labelled a default, not a recording.

The rules (cell against the reference's schema) and their mapping steps:

Feature Same Mapping step Cannot be mapped
camera key paired by key, then by role in order rename a target view with no counterpart
image size equal resize (aspect change said: pad or crop)
camera rate equal resample (drop or repeat frames)
codec equal reencode
extra camera drop
dataset rate (rows) equal resample rows
joint count equal another embodiment
joint names equal reorder (index list), or rename by position (an assumption)
joint units equal (rad / m) convert deg → rad (a ±2π heuristic on stored stats) revolute against prismatic
action space equal another space (joint against end effector)
gripper encoding equal type and range rescale the [closed, open] range linearly (an assumption)
control rate equal resample actions

Verdict: poolable as is, poolable after mapping, not poolable. The pooling mapping JSON (cells_pooling.json, schema dexvantage.cells_pooling/1) holds the target's schema and per cell its verdict, steps, what cannot be mapped, warnings (figures not stated) and the camera map.

4. Drift of one design built several times

The reference cell is the plan; a cell is a copy of it for each camera whose name it shares (case-insensitive). For each plan camera X_plan is its optical frame in its mount frame (core/calib/records.camera_mount: the moving link, else the world). A copy's camera is measured when the cell holds a solved hand-eye session for it (the latest by date; its X and σ from HandEyeResult.to_dict), else as designed (its own mount: a design difference, not drift). A copy on another mount frame is "not comparable".

Δ = [ (t_meas − t_plan) · 1000 mm ,  log(R_meas R_planᵀ) in degrees ]          (mount-frame axes)
share of tolerance  r = max( max|Δ_xyz| / tol_mm ,  ‖ω‖ / tol_deg )             (the plan camera's mount tolerance)
verdict: ok r ≤ 1, adjust r ≤ 3, re-mount above (Vantage core/fleet.py)

Across the measured copies of a camera (two or more): the median m and robust spread s = 1.4826 · MAD per component (Hampel 1974), floored at a quarter of the tolerance; with three or more copies, a copy whose robust z = (Δ − m) / s exceeds 3.5 in any component is an outlier; the build is systematic when the median is beyond the tolerance while more than half the copies sit within tolerance of the median. Then the suggestion adds a variant with those cameras at plan ⊕ median (X = [Exp(ω_m) R_plan | t_plan + m_xyz]); the plan is kept and stays active.

The suggestion

With the open project not the reference: mount its least-alike matched camera at the reference camera's pose in the role's frame (T_world = T_world_role · T_ref, F · T_ref when the pair is flipped), so that pair's viewpoint and position gaps are zero. With the open project the reference and a systematic drift: add the as-built variant. Apply is one undo step; a locked camera is not moved.

The coverage tolerance is the grid's: at most 128 columns of rays, so the cells along the polygon's edge carry about 1–2 % of a share (measured 1.44 % at worst). The self-test's cells line runs the first, second and last checks (about 1 s).

Known limits

The gap is geometric (viewpoint, distance, field of view, resolution, coverage of the task volume), not appearance (lighting, background, blur, colour) and not a predicted success rate; its bands are a heuristic. Wrist cameras are related at the home pose through the contact frame the grasp code measures (an assumed one for a robot without pads); occlusion of the task volume by the robot is not in the coverage share; scene cameras of two cells compare in their own world frames, so robots standing differently relative to their tables differ for that reason too. The gap compares the cells' designs (a calibration applied on the Hand-eye page writes the measured mount into the design); the drift reads the calibrations themselves, applied or not. Pooling reads the feature schema, not the data (the stats only for the units heuristic and the gripper range); joints correspond by name or position, never by kinematics; a recorded camera's role comes from its key name; LeRobot's own multi-dataset loader is not run (the package is not a dependency). The drift compares the latest calibration per copy and camera (earlier ones are listed), with small-angle rotation vectors, matching copies by camera name; trends over time, knocks and forecasts (Vantage fleet_twin.py) are not ported. The tolerance spread costs n evaluations of every study named per cell.
