Monte Carlo tolerances and the perturbation export

Spec §7.11 (Monte Carlo tolerances) and §8.5 (synthetic camera perturbation). Code: dexvantage/core/tolerance.py (the model, ported from Vantage core/tolerance.py), dexvantage/core/studies/tolerance.py (the study tolerance, batch flag --tolerance-mc OUT.json), dexvantage/core/exporters/perturbations.py (the exporter perturbations), dexvantage/ui/pages/tolerance.py (the Tolerances page). Checks: dexvantage/validation/checks_tolerance.py (model tolerance).

The batch flag is --tolerance-mc, not --tolerance: --tolerance already exists as --reproduce's comparison tolerance.

What is drawn

Each draw moves every enabled sensor of the active variant (locked ones too: a locked mount is still built with a tolerance). A draw of one sensor is six numbers, the factors of core/factors.py:

d = [dx, dy, dz]            metres along the mount parent frame's axes
    [droll, dpitch, dyaw]   degrees about the sensor body's own x, y, z axes, in that order:  R' = R · Rx · Ry · Rz

applied to a copy of the project through factors.apply(..., allow_locked=True).

Mount tolerance: a ± bound

Mount.tolerance (position_mm, rotation_deg) is a ± bound per axis, the way the Inspector and the Sensors page show it ("± 1 mm · ± 0.5°", the project's labelled defaults). Each of the three shifts lies within ± position_mm and each of the three turns within ± rotation_deg, independently. Inside the bound the distribution is a setting:

distribution draw σ of one component
uniform (default, labelled) U(−b, b) b / √3
normal N(0, (b/2)²) truncated at ± b (the bound is 2 σ) (b/2) √(1 − 2·2 φ(2) / (2 Φ(2) − 1)) = 0.4398 b

Uniform is the default because a drawing tolerance says only that the part is inside the bound: every value within it is taken as equally likely (Vantage's default too). The truncated normal is for a process known to centre.

Calibration covariance

A sensor with a hand-eye session (project.calibration, core/calib) whose result is solved (ok) and applied draws from that result's 6 × 6 covariance instead. The bundle adjustment parameterises the imager's optical frame X in the mount frame as X' = [R_X Exp(ω) | t_X + v] (ω about X's own axes, v along the parent's: core/calib/handeye.py). A draw is (ω, v) ~ N(0, C), turned exactly into the body's factors. With the imager's pose in the body T_im = [R_im | t_im] and the body's nominal rotation R_B:

δ     = R_im ω                                   (the turn about the body's axes: Exp(R_im ω) = R_im Exp(ω) R_imᵀ)
shift = v − R_B (Exp(δ) − I) t_im                (the body origin moves when X turns about the imager)
(droll, dpitch, dyaw) = the X-Y-Z decomposition of Exp(δ):  b = asin R₀₂, a = atan2(−R₁₂, R₂₂), c = atan2(−R₀₁, R₀₀)

so the moved project's X is exactly [R_X Exp(ω) | t_X + v]. The calibration replaces the mount tolerance only while the sensor is where the calibration put it: if its current X differs from the stored one by more than 1 µm or 1 µrad (the sensor was moved since), the mount tolerance is used and the source says why.

Bounds

bounds(project) gives per sensor position_m and rotation_deg as [lo, hi] per axis and the source: a mount tolerance's ± bound (× scale), or a calibration covariance's ± 2 σ of each marginal of the linearised body covariance J C Jᵀ, with J mapping (ω, v) to (shift, δ): ∂shift/∂ω = R_B [t_im]× R_im, ∂shift/∂v = I, ∂δ/∂ω = R_im.

Monte Carlo

monte_carlo(project, metric_ids, n, seed) evaluates the named metrics (core/metrics.evaluate: each study re-run on the moved project, no cache) on the project as it is (the nominal) and on n draws from one numpy.random.default_rng (seed), sensors in the variant's order. Per metric:

  • nominal, mean, σ (n − 1), P5, P50, P95, min, max over the draws that gave a value; the count that gave none;
  • the pessimistic 5 % figure: P5 for a higher-is-better metric, P95 for a lower-is-better one;
  • per project requirement on the metric: the share of draws meeting it, with its Wilson score 95 % interval (Wilson 1927), and whether the nominal meets it. Each draw is decided as the scorecard decides it (core/requirements.decide: a tolerance of 10⁻⁹ relative at the threshold; a figure the study states only as a bound, such as a time-sync skew of "at least 1 ms", meets or misses only when the bound settles it); a draw whose bound does not settle the requirement counts as not meeting it and is counted in undecided;
  • per mount, its contribution: for each component x the Spearman rank correlation ρ(x, f) and ρ(|x|, f) (a figure that falls whichever way the camera turns has ρ ≈ 0 and ρ(|x|, f) large); a component counts max(ρ², ρ_abs²), a mount the sum over its six (split into its position and rotation parts), and the shares are those sums normalised over the mounts. A mount whose largest |ρ| is below 2 / √(n − 1) (two standard errors of ρ under no association) is flagged as not distinguishable from noise.

The study's headline is the requirement met by the smallest share of draws (ties: the smallest margin of its pessimistic figure over the threshold, in σ), as that pessimistic figure, with a status word from the share: holds from 95 %, marginal from 80 %, often fails from 50 %, else fails (DexVantage defaults). Without requirements it is the figure with the widest relative spread, word no requirement.

Which figures: the study's metrics setting; else the metrics the project's requirements name; else (a default) the headline metrics of the fast geometric studies, coverage.covered, depth.valid, motion_blur.worst_px, vla.min_tokens (under a second per draw on the samples, measured 2026-11-07; grasp visibility takes about 30 s per draw on the UR5e sample and is left to a requirement or the settings).

The suggestion (the page's Apply) halves the position or rotation tolerance (whichever part contributes more) of the unlocked mount-tolerance sensor that contributes most to the headline figure, if above noise. Its effect is not predicted; the study is run again.

The perturbation export (§8.5)

perturbed_extrinsics(project, n, seed) draws as above and gives arrays (n draws, S sensors):

array meaning
d (n, S, 6) the factors above
delta_body (n, S, 4, 4) T_body' = T_body · Δ, Δ = [Rx Ry Rz | R_Bᵀ shift] (core/sim/render extrinsic_delta)
T_parent_body the moved body in its mount parent frame
T_parent_optical the primary imager's REP-103 optical frame (z forward, x right, y down) in the mount parent frame
T_world_optical the same in the world at the joint state given (home by default)

The exporter writes perturbations/perturbations.json (per draw and sensor: d, delta_body, the mount pose, an Isaac Lab CameraCfg.OffsetCfg with pos, rot_xyzw, rot_wxyz and convention: "ros", and a 3DGS camera with position and camera-to-world rotation) and perturbations/perturbations.npz (the arrays, sensor uids and names, seed, scale, distribution), both listed with SHA-256 in the export manifest and the JSON stamped with provenance.

Conventions, read from the targets' source on 2026-11-07: Isaac Lab's OffsetCfg.rot is (w, x, y, z) at tag v2.3.0 and (x, y, z, w) at tag v3.0.0 and on main (commit d4be263), source/isaaclab/isaaclab/sensors/camera/camera_cfg.py — both orders are written, named; convention "ros" is forward +Z, up −Y, the optical frame here. The 3DGS reference code (utils/camera_utils.py camera_to_JSON, commit 54c035f) writes a camera as its centre position and a camera-to-world rotation in the COLMAP/OpenCV axes, the optical frame here.

Validation (measured 2026-11-07)

The rig of the checks: three static sensors, "Mount A" ± 2 mm ± 1°, "Mount B" ± 1 mm ± 0.25°, and a RealSense D435 calibrated by a hand-eye result written in the check with a correlated 6 × 6 covariance (σ 1.5 mrad, 0.8 mm), so the RGB imager's offset in the body matters.

check reference measured (full / quick) tolerance why that tolerance
tolerance.linear_propagation GUM law of propagation, σ² = Σ (∂f/∂x)² σ²ₓ + wᵀ C w, written out (JCGM 100:2008 eq. 10, 13) 0.0060 / 0.0083 relative error of σ (n = 20 000 / 3 000; uniform 0.0030, normal 0.0060) 4 standard errors of a sample σ, 4 √(2 / 4n): 0.020 / 0.052 (declared as applied, full / quick) sampling error of σ̂ for a kurtosis up to 3; the mean is also within 4 σ/√n
tolerance.sampler_ks scipy.stats.kstest against uniform, truncnorm and norm; positions, SciPy intrinsic XYZ Euler angles and rotation vectors read back from the moved projects largest D 0.0235 / 0.032 over 30 marginals (n = 4 000 / 1 500) critical D at α = 0.001 / 30: 0.037 / 0.061 (declared as applied, full / quick) Bonferroni over the marginals; the calibration draws' sample covariance is within 4.1 % (Frobenius) of C
tolerance.seed_reproducible the same run repeated; the export's separate draw loop 0 0 the same seed must give the same numbers
tolerance.spearman scipy.stats.spearmanr, with ties 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁷ 10⁻¹² rounding only
tolerance.export_roundtrip the project moved through core.factors per draw, composed with the imager offset; SciPy reads rot_wxyz 1.5 × 10⁻⁹ m or rad 10⁻⁸ the JSON's 9 decimals

The self-test runs the last three (about 0.8 s, mostly imports).

Known limits

  • The six components of a mount tolerance are independent, and no two sensors are correlated: a shared bracket that bends moves its cameras together, which is not modelled.
  • The turns are applied in a fixed order (x, y, z about the moving body axes); a rotation-vector reading of the same bound differs only to second order in the angle.
  • The calibration covariance is the bundle adjustment's Gauss-Newton estimate (optimistic when the residuals are not Gaussian, docs/models/handeye.md); intrinsics' covariance, joint encoders and link lengths are not drawn.
  • The contribution shares treat the components as independent; for a calibration covariance (correlated components) they are indicative. Rank correlation finds monotone dependence on a component or its size, not interactions.
  • Each draw re-runs every study the metrics need, so n is bounded by the slowest study; a share of 95 % from 64 draws has a Wilson interval about ± 6 % wide.
  • The export gives optical frames for each sensor's primary imager only (others: apply delta_body); the 3DGS world pose of a link-mounted camera holds at the joint state of the export only.
