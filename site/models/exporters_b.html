Exporters, part B: Isaac Lab and Isaac Sim, LeRobot and the policy codes, reports

dexvantage/core/exporters/isaac.py ('isaac'), lerobot.py ('lerobot'), report.py ('report') with _pdf.py; the round trips in dexvantage/validation/formats_isaac_lerobot.py, registered in checks_formats.py as formats.isaac_usd, formats.isaac_config, formats.lerobot_export and formats.report. Spec §9.2 (exporters), §9.4 (bridges), §8.5 (randomisation bounds), §7.13 (the training-config recommendations). A worked example is in Isaac Lab, LeRobot and reports: a worked example on the UR5e sample.

Each runs through the export registry (core/exporters/__init__.py): File ▸ Export… and export(project, out_dir, ['isaac', 'lerobot', 'report'], options); every JSON file gets the provenance block, except those a target parser refuses extra keys in (below), and the manifest holds every file's SHA-256.

Contract change (additive). An exporter may return 'unstamped': [rel]: those JSON files are hashed in the manifest but not stamped, and the format's row lists them. LeRobot's train_config.json is read by draccus, which refuses unknown keys, and LeRobot's DatasetInfo.from_dict warns on them, so the train config and the info.json templates are unstamped.

Frames. A camera's offset is its imager's optical frame (REP-103: z forward, x right, y down) in its parent: the robot link the sensor is mounted on, or the environment for a static mount; Isaac Lab's convention "ros" (forward +Z, up −Y) is the same frame. The prim path is {ENV_REGEX_NS}/Robot/<link>/<camera> (the articulation's prim name is an option) or {ENV_REGEX_NS}/<camera>.

USD lens. Isaac Lab's own rule (utils/sensors.py convert_camera_intrinsics_to_usd: pixel size 1/W when no focal length is given): horizontalAperture = 1, focalLength = fx/W, verticalAperture = (H/W)(fx/fy). GfCamera's frustum puts the window on the image plane at (aperture/2 ± offset)/focal, so with DexVantage's pixel convention (OpenCV: pixel centres at integers, image from −0.5 to W − 0.5):

fx = W f / hAp,   cx = (W − 1)/2 − W·hOff/hAp
fy = H f / vAp,   cy = (H − 1)/2 + H·vOff/vAp        (USD's y is up, the image's v down)

so hOff = hAp((W − 1)/2 − cx)/W and vOff = vAp(cy − (H − 1)/2)/H. A USD camera looks along −Z with +Y up: its transform is the optical frame times diag(1, −1, −1). The stage is metres, Z up, defaultPrim /DexVantage; static cameras under /DexVantage/World, link-mounted ones under /DexVantage/<robot>/<link>, an Xform at the link's pose at the export's joint state (home by default) so the file shows the rig. clippingRange (0.005, 50) m are DexVantage's labelled defaults (the MuJoCo scene's). With usd-core (pip install "dexvantage[usd]", pinned usd-core==26.8) pxr writes the file; without it a text writer of the same prims and attributes does, and the notes say which.

Isaac Lab v2.3.2 has no OpenCV fields: its file carries the square, centred pinhole of fx, and the notes give the largest pixel gap from the real lens, or say that the lens sees rays a pinhole cannot (a fisheye beyond 90°).

Imagers. Every imager of every enabled camera and depth sensor is a camera: data_types ['rgb'], plus 'distance_to_image_plane' on a depth sensor's reference imager (the library entry's reference_imager). IR and ToF imagers are visible-light RGB prims with that imager's geometry (Isaac renders no near-infrared): the notes say so. update_period is 1/fps of the stream choice (core/studies/motion_blur.imaging), else 0.0 (every step), said in a comment.

Randomisation bounds come from core/tolerance.bounds (the TOLERANCE study's ± bound per axis of the mount tolerance, shifts along the mount parent's x, y, z and turns about the body's own x, y, z applied as R' = R·Rx·Ry·Rz; or ± 2σ of a calibration covariance's marginals). Each config file carries DR[name] (the sensor body in its parent, the imager in the body, the bounds) and pure-Python perturbed_offset(name, d), sample_offset(name, rng) (uniform within the bounds) and randomized_cameras(seed) (every CameraCfg with one draw, through configclass replace).

LeRobot, ACT, Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA — lerobot.py

Sources (read 2026-11-07): LeRobot at commit d490e472a5d1a207cfe0255caaaf74b6d66e4656 (the one core/datasets/lerobot.py follows): configs/train.py, configs/default.py, configs/policies.py, configs/types.py, policies/act/configuration_act.py, policies/diffusion/configuration_diffusion.py, the register_subclass names of pi0, pi0_fast, smolvla and groot, datasets/factory.py, datasets/lerobot_dataset.py, datasets/utils.py (DatasetInfo.from_dict); ACT at 742c753c0d4a5d87076c8f69e5628c79a8cc5488 (constants.py, imitate_episodes.py); Diffusion Policy at 5ba07ac6661db573af695b419a7947ecb704690f (config/task/real_pusht_image.yaml, config/train_diffusion_unet_real_image_workspace.yaml, dataset/real_pusht_image_dataset.py); OpenVLA at c8f03f48af692657d3060c19588038c7220e9af9 (prismatic/vla/datasets/rlds/oxe/configs.py).

info.json (v2.1 and v3.0) is what write_lerobot writes for a dataset recorded with the rig: observation.state and action (float32, the robots' actuated joints by name), one video feature per camera the policies take (each sensor's primary imager at its stream size, key observation.images.<sensor name> as core/sim/episodes.py names them, sorted as the writer sorts them), the default features, zero counters, and the video fields of LeRobot's default encoder (AV1 from libsvtav1, yuv420p, g 2, crf 30, preset 12 at v3.0). fps: the option, else the first policy's control rate, else the fastest camera, else 30 fps (a default).

Train config (per policy with a LeRobot type: act, diffusion, pi0, pi0_fast, smolvla, groot): TrainPipelineConfig keys dataset {repo_id, root}, policy {type, input_features (STATE and one VISUAL [3, H, W] per camera the policy takes), output_features (ACTION), device, push_to_hub false, repo_id}, output_dir, job_name; ACT: chunk_size from the policy entry, temporal_ensemble_coeff = m and n_action_steps 1 (ACTConfig: "n_action_steps must be 1 when using temporal ensembling"); Diffusion: horizon, n_action_steps, resize_shape (H, W) from the entry's pre_resize and crop_shape from its centre crop. train.sh runs lerobot-train --config_path=train_config.json.

§7.13 recommendations (Policy.recommendations, stored by the action-chunk study's Apply):

Recommendation Where it goes
temporal_ensemble_m (w_i = exp(−m·i), i = 0 the oldest) ACTConfig.temporal_ensemble_coeff (the same convention: "w₀ is the oldest action"); ACT's k in act/task_config.yaml (the original code hard-codes k = 0.01)

Without stored recommendations the frameworks' own defaults are written and labelled (ACT's k = 0.01, the entry's temporal ensembling, n_latency_steps 0).

ACT YAML (act/task_config.yaml): the TASK_CONFIGS entry {dataset_dir, num_episodes, episode_len, camera_names} with the camera names, temporal_agg_k and the delay in steps. Diffusion Policy YAML (diffusion_policy/task.yaml): shape_meta (rgb cameras [3, H, W] at the entry's pre_resize, the low-dim state, the action) and n_latency_steps. OpenVLA (openvla/oxe_dataset_config.py and .json): an OXE_DATASET_CONFIGS entry: image_obs_keys primary (the first static camera), secondary (the next), wrist (the first link-mounted camera; OpenVLA-OFT's primary, secondary, left_wrist and right_wrist for a two-arm rig with an OFT policy); depth keys none; state_obs_keys ['joint_state']; the state and action encodings chosen from the project (openvla_encoding, Robot import and export: URDF inertials, USD import, OpenVLA encodings, Isaac principal point), with the notes saying when no encoding of the pinned source fits.

Reports — report.py, _pdf.py

report/report.html (standalone: inline SVG figures from validation/report.svg_figure, light or dark), report/report.pdf and report/report.json. The results are those given (results: the app's results; or results_dir: batch result files), and the studies named in studies are run now ('all' when nothing is given: every study except those that run other studies many times, the tolerance Monte Carlo, experiments, the optimiser, the design agent, the variant comparison and the scorecard, which are reported when their results are given; on the UR5e sample that is 18 results and 4 refusals in 66 s, mostly the ground-truth renders and the teleop and grasp studies); a study that refuses is listed under "Not run" with its sentence. Each section: the STATUS word with its colour and the study's advice (read from the result's headline or summary), the study's named figures (core/metric_defs.py through core.metrics.read; "not stated" when the result does not state one), one bar chart of the first per-camera (per-policy, per-cable, …) list carrying a number with a unit suffix, the notes, and the result's own hash when it came with a provenance block. The scorecard is core/requirements.check on the same results (shared through a result cache, so no study runs twice). The provenance block is core.provenance.block of the report document.

The PDF is written by _pdf.py, pure Python (ISO 32000-1 file structure, Flate streams, the standard fonts with WinAnsiEncoding), so no optional package is needed and nothing is refused; characters outside cp1252 are spelled out (≥ as >=, σ as sigma).

Known limits

Isaac Lab, Isaac Sim, LeRobot, ACT, Diffusion Policy and OpenVLA are not installed here: the configs are checked by parsing and executing them against the field names of the pinned sources, not by those programs, and the USD is not rendered in Isaac Sim. The OpenCV lens attributes' pixel convention is taken to be OpenCV's (centres at integers), which the Isaac Lab source does not state; its pinhole K puts the centre at W/2, half a pixel from OpenCV's (W − 1)/2 if its pixels are centred at integers. The USD aperture offsets are exact for pxr's Gf.Camera, but Isaac Lab's spawner warns "Camera aperture offsets are not supported by Omniverse. These parameters will be ignored": so the off-centre principal point is written through the OpenCV lens attributes, which v3.0.0 projects through; the v2.3.2 file has none, so its render is centred, and each such camera is named with its offset in the notes, the config comment, the JSON and the Export dialog (Robot import and export: URDF inertials, USD import, OpenVLA encodings, Isaac principal point). OpenVLA's encodings are chosen from the rig (openvla_encoding): ActionEncoding.JOINT_POS is joint delta positions and OpenVLA's loader refuses it, which the notes say. A renderer that ignores the OpenCV attributes conforms non-square pixels to the render's aspect. IR and ToF imagers are RGB prims; depth outputs have no noise model. The randomisation is drawn once per config build (per-environment draws need Camera.set_world_poses at reset, not generated). The LeRobot state and action are every actuated joint of the robot files (a gripper's linkage joints included; the UR5e sample has 14); LeRobot's train config has no field for the delay offset (applied through delta timestamps or by shifting the action column); a dataset rate above a camera's repeats frames (said). The report's figure is one list per study chosen by a naming rule; results given with an export are reported as given. The PDF's line breaking uses approximate glyph widths.
