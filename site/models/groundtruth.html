MuJoCo ground truth: rendered against predicted

Modules: dexvantage/core/sim/scene.py (the scene), dexvantage/core/sim/render.py (renders through each lens), dexvantage/core/studies/groundtruth.py (study groundtruth, batch flag --groundtruth OUT.json). Page: dexvantage/ui/pages/groundtruth.py (Simulate ▸ Rendered against predicted visibility…; Simulate ▸ Build the MuJoCo scene… writes the scene folder). Checks: dexvantage/validation/checks_groundtruth.py (model groundtruth). Tests: tests/test_groundtruth.py. Self-test line groundtruth. Throughput: scripts/measure_groundtruth.py. Spec §9.1 ("this is DexVantage's CARLA").

The renders reuse dexvantage/validation/mjrender.py (the EGL worker process the Phase 2 checks use), extended with a worker that stays up (mjrender.Session), a per-job camera pose and intrinsics, and the EGL device choice.

The scene

build_scene(project, variant=None, objects=True, lights=True) returns a SceneBuild: the mujoco.MjSpec, the compiled model, camera_names and imagers (each sensor's primary imager: the RGB imager of a depth sensor, the one the grasp study uses), cameras (every imager of every sensor in use), body_map (body id or name → owner) and the geom → owner table the segmentation is read through.

Part How it is built How it matches the predictions
Robots the stored MJCF/URDF read as core/robot/model.py reads it (fusestatic off), attached with MjSpec.attach under a frame at the robot's base, names prefixed robot<k>_; keyframes and the file's world-body geoms dropped only the geoms RobotModel draws are drawn (visual-only geoms when the file has them, alpha > 0, a type it tessellates); the others, and every site and tendon, are moved to geom group 5, which MuJoCo's default visual options do not draw. Body poses equal RobotModel.fk (test: 0 m)
Task objects a static body per object with its exact primitive (box, cylinder, sphere) or a mesh of the project's triangles the predictions tessellate cylinders and spheres (24 segments, ≤ 1.1 % of the radius inside)
Surfaces a box whose top is at the surface's z (its thickness, else 1 mm: a labelled default) the predictions use a zero-thickness rectangle
Materials tags → MuJoCo materials (scene.MATERIALS, DexVantage defaults: glossy specular 0.8, metallic grey, black, IR-absorbing dark matte, transparent alpha 0.3) RGB only; segmentation and depth do not depend on them
Lights the project's lights as the lighting study defines them (core/lights.py: the face looks along the light's local +z; a light that is off is left out; point → point light, spot → spot of half its stated beam angle, panel, ring and window → spot of cutoff 90° and exponent 1, Lambert's cos θ; MuJoCo's default colour 0.7 times the dim; MuJoCo's classic renderer has no photometric units), else an overhead and a front directional light (labelled defaults), and MuJoCo's head light RGB only
Cameras one per imager, in the mount link's body (or the world), at mount · imager · diag(1, −1, −1) the imager's CameraModel at its stream size (below)

Camera intrinsics, verified in the installed MuJoCo's reference. Read in doc/XMLreference.rst of github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco at tag 3.17.0 (the installed version), section body/camera, on 2026-11-07: fovy is the vertical field of view in degrees and "the horizontal field of view is computed automatically given the window size"; sensorsize is the "Size of the camera sensor in length units. When specified, all intrinsic attributes become active and fovy is ignored"; focal / focalpixel are the focal length "in physical length units or in pixels, respectively. If both are specified, the pixel value is used"; principal / principalpixel are the "Offset of the principal point (optical axis intersection with the image plane) from the image center" (the direction is not stated); resolution "is not used for rendering since those dimensions are determined by the size of the rendering context". visual/map/znear is the near plane as a fraction of the model extent. The scene therefore sets, per camera at its W × H stream:

resolution = (W, H)
sensorsize = (W, H) · 3 µm            (a nominal pixel pitch: MuJoCo needs a length, only ratios matter)
focalpixel = (fx, fy)
principalpixel = ((W − 1)/2 − cx, (H − 1)/2 − cy)
statistic/extent and visual/map/znear so that the near plane is 5 mm (as core/occlusion.py and camrender)

MuJoCo 3.17.0 compiles this to cam_intrinsic = focalpixel · sensorsize / resolution (measured in tests/test_groundtruth.py; setting intrinsic directly compiles to zero, so the scene does not). The principal offset's sign, (W − 1)/2 − px, is the one mjrender.camera_model measured on renders in Phase 2; the checks below confirm it again through fiducials and per-pixel agreement. MuJoCo cameras are pinhole: a lens with distortion has its pinhole part (fx, fy, cx, cy) in the scene and the note says so.

save_scene(project, folder) writes scene.xml, assets/ (the robots' mesh and texture files) and scene_notes.txt (the cameras with their lens models, the defaults, the notes); the result loads with mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path and holds no path of this machine.

Renders through the lens model

Renderer(project, variant=None, device='auto') is a context manager around one mjrender.Session; .render(views) takes [{'sensor_uid', 'q', 'outputs', 'scale', 'extrinsic_delta', 'imager', 'camera_model'}] and returns per view rgb (H × W × 3 uint8), depth (metres along the optical z, 0 = no hit), range (metres along the pixel's ray), seg (owner index, −1 = nothing), seg_owner and the CameraModel the image follows. extrinsic_delta perturbs the mount (T_body_sensor · Δ) for a synthetic mount slip. .info names the renderer, GL, MuJoCo and device.

The lens decides the path (render.lens_plan):

  • pinhole: the scene camera at the asked size, no resampling.
  • wide (every pixel ray within 60° of the axis: Brown-Conrady, a mild fisheye): one pinhole base image in the same optical frame that covers every pixel's ray. For each lens pixel (u, v), r = CameraModel.unproject(u, v) and the base pixel is (fx_b r_x / r_z + cx_b, fy_b r_y / r_z + cy_b); cv2.remap samples it (nearest for segmentation and depth, bilinear for RGB). The lens's distortion is applied exactly at each pixel centre; nothing is fitted.
  • cube (beyond 60°, including fisheyes past 180°): the 90° cube faces around the optical frame that the rays need; each pixel takes the face its ray leaves through.

The base images are at least OVERSAMPLE = 2 (a default) times as dense, in pixels per radian, as the lens image at its densest, up to 4096 px a side (2048 for a cube face); a cap that bites is noted. The base's intrinsics are set per render through mjrender's camera_intrinsic (cam_sensorsize and cam_intrinsic in pixel units, which MuJoCo reads at every render: measured in the tests). Depth along a base camera's axis a becomes the lens's optical z and range with the pixel's own ray: range = d / (r · a), z = range · r_z.

The study

(a) Contact visibility. A seeded sample of the grasp set (50 grasps, a default), the home pose, and trajectory_frames (8, a default) evenly spaced frames, both ends included, of every joint trajectory of the same robot (trajectory.on_model: joints a trajectory does not name stay at the grasp study's joint state). A project with trajectories and no grasp set compares home and the trajectory frames. Each grasp is solved and evaluated by the grasp study itself (grasp_visibility.solve_grasps, snapped_pads, pad_world, Evaluator.states at the grasp's own q, no q-bucket cache), with the grasp study's settings. A contact point is predicted visible when it projects into the image and the contact location (the point lifted 2 mm along the pad's normal) is not hidden in the camera's cube map. It is rendered visible when it projects into the image, the segmentation at its nearest pixel is its own finger, and the rendered depth there is within depth_tol_mm (5 mm, a default) of the point's depth. "Its own finger" is the pad link and every link welded to it (groundtruth.finger_links): the Robotiq 2F-85's pad link is a collision box with no visual geometry; its silicone pad and follower are welded to it. Over the (camera, point) pairs in view:

confusion: TP both visible, FP predicted visible only, FN rendered visible only, TN both hidden
agreement = (TP + TN) / n,   IoU of the visible sets = TP / (TP + FP + FN)

per pose, per kind of pose (by_kind: home, grasp, trajectory), per camera and in total, and, over the grasps only, the grasp study's requirement (its own grasp_score) on the predicted and on the rendered fractions. Each disagreeing point gets a reason: edge (its pixel's 3 × 3 neighbourhood holds two owners), rule (the render's own occlusion test at the lifted point, rendered range ≥ its distance − eps, agrees with the prediction: the two definitions differ, typically a pad seen edge-on) or occlusion (the cube map and the render disagree about what is in front).

(b) Depth validity. For every depth sensor the depth study can analyse, at home, every imager the depth needs is rendered and the reference depth gets the sensor's noise model, z̃ = z + σ_z(z) n with σ_z = z² σ_d / (f B) (the study's own sigma_z, σ_d and baseline; seeded). On the study's own voxels (the headline): field of view, min-Z, range, projector and materials are the same functions on both sides; occlusion is the study's cube maps against the renders (a voxel is hidden from the reference imager when the noisy depth at its pixel is nearer than it by more than 1 mm, the study's eps, and from another imager when the rendered range is). On the rendered surfaces (reported beside it): the study's evaluate at each rendered surface point against the noisy point, with a left-right consistency check (the other imager's rendered range within 5 mm + 3 σ_z, a default).

Status: agrees from 90 %, mostly agrees from 75 %, else disagrees (defaults, not standards).

Throughput

EGL device 0 NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5080 76–99 14.7–17.5
EGL device 2 AMD Radeon (Ryzen 9 9900X integrated, radeonsi) 68 14.2
EGL device 1 — (MuJoCo cannot open a PLATFORM_DEVICE display on it: the Renderer says so in words)

RTX 5080, lens swapped for an equidistant fisheye (5 cube faces of 2048 px) 19 0.7 (16 views)

device='auto' lets MuJoCo take the first EGL device that initialises (device 0 here), or the one DEXVANTAGE_EGL names. End to end is bounded by moving full-size images between the processes (about 23 MB per 1920 × 1080 view), not by the GPU. The renders were measured while other work shared the machine; the spread is the range of two runs.

Known limits

The prediction leaves out the camera's seat on its mount link (the 25 mm rule of scenegeom) and the render does not; MuJoCo draws cylinders and spheres as smooth primitives, the predictions as 24-gons; one pixel decides each contact point, so a point within a pixel of a silhouette can go either way (counted as "edge"); the noise model is first order and Gaussian (no matcher bias, no holes from missing texture, no multipath); the projector pattern and IR materials are not rendered (the study's own rules on both sides); depth is compared at the home pose; renders are geometric (MuJoCo's shading: no exposure, noise, blur or rolling shutter); a lens rendered through cube faces moves a lot of data between processes and is slow; equality constraints (a gripper's linkage) are not solved, as in RobotModel; the scene file written by Build the MuJoCo scene… carries MuJoCo's XML writer's 6 significant digits (camera poses within 6 × 10⁻⁷ m and 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ rad, intrinsics within 2 × 10⁻³ px of the project's, measured by formats.mjcf_scene when the file is loaded back from disk); the renders use the scene in memory, which is not rounded.
