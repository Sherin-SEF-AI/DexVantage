Data-collection campaign planner

Research tools part 2. Code: dexvantage/core/campaign.py (the model), dexvantage/core/studies/campaign.py (the study campaign, batch flag --campaign-saved OUT.json), dexvantage/batch_campaign.py (--campaign SPEC.json [--campaign-out OUT.json]), dexvantage/core/exporters/campaign.py (the exporter campaign), dexvantage/ui/pages/campaign.py (Data ▸ Campaign), dv.campaign in dexvantage/api.py, the assistant's get_campaign. Checks: dexvantage/validation/checks_campaign.py (model campaign, 6 checks). Ported in part from Vantage core/simcampaign.py (a seeded design with each condition list shuffled per seed) and core/fleet.py (settings clamped to limits, dates parsed and normalised); nothing automotive is kept.

Inputs

The spec lives in project.analysis.studies['campaign'], or in a spec file for batch mode (schema dexvantage.campaign_spec/1). Every key is optional; each default below is a DexVantage default and the result lists the keys left at their default (defaults).

Key Default Meaning

lighting one preset, "As designed" (the lights as they are) [{name, lights: {light uid or name: dim 0..1}}] (0 = off)

The design

C conditions, O operators. Each operator records each condition m times in B rounds; in each round the operator records every condition once, as a block of k = m / B consecutive episodes, in the order of one row of a Williams balanced Latin square (Williams 1949):

first row  0, 1, C−1, 2, C−2, 3, …            row i = (first row + i) mod C
C odd:     those C rows and their C mirror images (2C rows)

In the C rows of an even order every condition is at every position once and follows every other condition exactly once; in the 2C rows of an odd order every ordered pair is adjacent exactly twice. The S = O × B sequences take the square's rows in turn (row s mod L, L the square's row count); the seed shuffles which condition each symbol stands for and which sequence (operator, round) gets which row. Hence:

  • every condition is recorded B times by every operator (always);
  • every condition is at every position of a round equally often when S is a multiple of C;
  • every ordered pair is adjacent equally often (first-order carry-over balance) when S is a multiple of L.

rounds: auto takes the smallest B ≤ m with S a multiple of L, else of C, else B = 1; the result's design.text says which balance holds and how many sequences full balance needs. m is rounded up to a multiple of B (the notes say how many more episodes that records).

Sessions. An operator's episodes are packed in order into sessions of at most session_max_min (whole minutes, rounded up; a session's length is its changeovers, its episodes with their resets, and the calibration check when it is per session). An episode never straddles two sessions. The packing is then repeated with the shortest limit that needs no more sessions (bisection: in-order packing never needs more sessions for a longer limit), so no session is a short remainder.

Days. Filled in passes: each pass offers one session to every operator who has one left, in an order rotated by one operator each day, while the day has a slot (sessions_per_day), the operator is under operator_sessions_per_day, and the session ends by day_start + day_hours (after the daily calibration check and a break_min break after each session). The first session of each day carries the calibration check when it is daily.

Storage

Per episode, for the condition's variant (core/studies/bandwidth.py, the bandwidth study's own figures):

GB = Σ payload_mbps (enabled sensors) × ratio × (1 + container_overhead) × episode_s / 8 / 1000

with payload_mbps = width × height × fps × bits per pixel of the stated stream modes (or your measured rate), container_overhead the bandwidth study's setting (3 %, a default), ratio 1 for raw and rosbag (raw image messages) and, for lerobot, your measured compression_ratio. LeRobot encodes video with AV1 (libsvtav1, yuv420p, g 2, crf 30, preset 12 at v3.0; core/datasets/lerobot.py ENCODER_DEFAULTS, from LeRobot's configs/video.py); no source states the size that gives (it depends on the content), so without your ratio the size is the raw size and the result says so. A sensor without a stated rate adds nothing: the storage is then "at least" (metric bound at_least).

Demonstrations from a power analysis

The per-group size to tell success rate p1 from p2 with a two-sided test at α with power 1 − β (Fleiss, Levin & Paik 2003, normal approximation):

n = [z_{1−α/2} √(2 p̄ q̄) + z_{1−β} √(p1 q1 + p2 q2)]² / (p2 − p1)²,      p̄ = (p1 + p2) / 2,  q = 1 − p

and with the continuity correction (the default; Fleiss, Tytun & Ury 1980): n_c = n / 4 · (1 + √(1 + 4 / (n |p2 − p1|)))². 0.5 against 0.7 at α 0.05 and 80 % power: n = 93 without, 103 with the correction. The figure comes from core/stats.py's n_two_proportions (its normal method, the same formula, with the power it reaches at n) when that module is there; the formula in power_n is the fallback, and the result keeps both (n, formula_n).

Budget and status

Each budget figure given is compared with the plan: storage against disk_gb, days against days, the busiest day's upload time (its GB × 8000 / upload_mbps / 3600 h) against upload_hours_per_day (24 h without one), the busiest operator's hours against operator_hours. Within: good; above 90 % of the limit (TIGHT, a default): warning, "tight"; over: critical, "over budget". No figure given: warning, "no budget set". A storage that is only "at least" turns a good status into "storage incomplete". The advice says what to change for each figure over or near its limit.

Outputs

The result (dexvantage.campaign/1): conditions (each with its levels, episode length and its source, GB per episode, recorded and expected kept episodes), design, sequences, sessions (day, date, start and end, operator, blocks, GB and the checklist), days, totals, budget, headline ("N episodes over D days, S GB" with its status word), advice, notes, assumptions, defaults, references.

The checklist of a session: the calibration check (the hand-eye plan's camera and board, check_poses of its planned poses, its requirement need_mm and need_deg, labelled defaults when unset; the last solved session); per item one line when every condition of the session shares it, else one per condition: the rig variant, the lighting preset (each light on at its dim or off), the object set, the teleop devices, each camera's exposure, gain and polariser as the motion-blur and lighting studies' settings hold them (what their Apply writes; "not set" otherwise); the disk to free; the blocks to record.

Exports: CSV (one row per block), iCalendar (RFC 5545: one VEVENT per session with UID, DTSTAMP in UTC, floating DTSTART and DTEND, SUMMARY and the checklist as DESCRIPTION; TEXT escaped, CRLF, lines folded at 75 octets without splitting a UTF-8 sequence), a printable HTML page (one day per printed page). Apply writes compact_plan (the schedule without the checklists, its digest) into analysis.studies['campaign']['plan'], one undo step; the study ignores that key.

Metrics: campaign.episodes (headline), campaign.days, campaign.storage_gb (bound at_least when incomplete), campaign.rig_hours, campaign.kept_per_condition.

Known limits

Every episode is taken to last episode_s + reset_s (real demonstrations vary, fail and are re-done; the keep rate covers discarded episodes on average, not their time); a trajectory's duration is the robot's scripted motion, which teleoperation may be slower than; operators are taken as equally fast; the fatigue limits are defaults, not ergonomics findings; position and carry-over balance are exact only for the numbers of sequences the design states, and the balance is of blocks, not single episodes; conditions are not balanced across days (operators rotate their order; a day holds what fits); days are filled greedily, which is not always the fewest days when sessions differ; a compressed format's storage is the raw size without a measured ratio; a sensor without a stated data rate adds nothing; upload is a steady rate without protocol overhead; times are local without a time zone (floating calendar times, RFC 5545 §3.3.5); holidays are not known; the power analysis is for two independent proportions, not several conditions at once, paired designs or learning curves. --reproduce of a --campaign SPEC.json result re-runs the project's stored spec, not the file's (store the spec with Open spec… or Apply to reproduce it).
