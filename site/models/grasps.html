Grasp sets

Module: dexvantage/core/grasps.py. Page: dexvantage/ui/pages/tasks.py (Tasks). Validation: dexvantage/validation/checks_grasps.py. Tests: tests/test_grasps.py. Spec §7.3 (grasp sets), §13 (Tasks page).

A grasp set is the input of the grasp self-occlusion study: a list of gripper poses at contact, each with the direction the gripper moves into the grasp. This module is the only code that writes or reads them.

The record

project.tasks.grasp_sets holds plain records (they round-trip through project.json unchanged):

{'uid', 'name', 'source': 'sampled' | 'imported' | 'trajectory' | 'dataset', 'robot_uid',
 'frame': the link the poses are for (default Robot.gripper_frame, else the model's last link),
 'poses': [{'id': str, 'pose': {'position': [x, y, z], 'quaternion': [x, y, z, w]},
            'approach': [x, y, z], 'kind': 'top_down' | 'side' | 'angled' | 'handoff' | 'imported' | 'trajectory',
            'object_uid': str or '', 'width_m': float or null, 't': float or null}],
 'settings': {how it was made: the sampler's options and which are defaults, the seed, the file, the trajectory,
              the gripper geometry used, notes}}

pose is the gripper frame in the world at contact (metres; quaternion x, y, z, w as core/frames.py); approach is a world unit vector. width_m is an upper bound of the object's width along the closing axis (its bounding box) or null; t is the trajectory time or null.

API:

Function Returns

from_trajectory(project, traj_uid, every=1, name=None, mode='every', close_mm=5, approach_mm=20) mode every: the gripper frame at every Nth sample; mode closures: one grasp where the gripper closes

contact_points(project, robot_uid, q) {fingertip: (K, 3)} world points on the contact pads
object_points(project, object_uid, n, rng_seed=0) (n, 3) points on a task object's surface, by area

Every function raises grasps.GraspError with a plain sentence for inputs it cannot use.

Contact pads

contact_patches decides which surface of each fingertip touches the object, once at the robot's home pose, holds it in the link's frame and carries it with the link at any q. The rule:

  1. Tagged pads (Robot.contact_pads, the import wizard's pad tagger): a box {'center', 'size'} in the link frame contributes the face whose outward normal points most nearly at the centroid of the pads on other links (12 × 12 points at cell centres); a pad with no pad on another link uses its whole box; a pad given as 'faces' (triangles in the link frame) contributes PAD_POINTS area-weighted points on them.
  2. Otherwise the fingertip frames' geometry: each fingertip link's collision geoms (its visual meshes if it has none). Its triangles' outward normals are taken away from each geom's own centroid (exact for the convex pads and boxes of the samples). A triangle is a contact face when it is
    • within PAD_LENGTH_M = 40 mm (a default) of the finger's distal end along the approach axis (the line from the gripper frame's origin to the fingertips' centroid),
    • facing the other fingertips' centroid within PAD_FACING_DEG = 45° (a default), its normal measured against that direction with its approach component removed, and
    • uncovered: a ray from its centre towards the other fingertips meets none of the same finger's triangles (core/raycast.py). When nothing passes, the rule drops the third test, then the second, then the first, and the patch's rule text says which. PAD_POINTS = 128 points (a default) are drawn on the kept triangles by area, from a generator seeded per fingertip, so a patch is the same every time.

On the samples:

  • UR5e + Robotiq 2F-85 (gripper_right_pad, gripper_left_pad): each pad link has two collision boxes, 22 mm wide and 18.75 mm long, stacked to 37.5 mm. The rule keeps exactly the inner face of both boxes on each pad (one plane, normals towards the other pad).
  • SO-100 (Fixed_Jaw, Moving_Jaw): the inner faces of the jaws' distal collision boxes and hull, mean normals 1.8° from opposite. Both patches span 58 mm along the approach (a triangle that reaches into the 40 mm band is kept whole) and end within 2.3 mm of each other.

The gripper geometry

The samplers place the grasp centre, not the gripper frame, on their targets. gripper_geometry measures it from the pads at home, in the gripper frame:

TCP       t = mean of the pads' centroids (each the area-weighted centroid of its contact faces)
closing   c = (n₀ − n₁)/|n₀ − n₁| for two pads whose mean inward normals n₀, n₁ oppose within 60°,
          else (C₁ − C₀)/|C₁ − C₀|; for more than two pads the principal axis of their centroids
approach  a = (t − (t·c) c) / |…|, the direction from the frame origin to the TCP across the closing axis;
          the frame's z axis when that is under 1 mm long

A robot without fingertips or pads gets t = 0, a = z, c = x and the set's notes say these are assumed. The sampler's tcp, approach_axis and closing_axis override them. Measured:

Robot Frame TCP (mm) Approach Closing Pad gap at home Pads along approach
UR5e + 2F-85 gripper_base (0.0, 0.0, 130.7) (0, 0, 1) (0, −1, 0) 85.4 mm 37.0 mm
SO-100 Fixed_Jaw (−0.2, −82.9, 0.7) (−0.003, −1.000, 0.008) (−1.000, 0.003, 0.000) 21.8 mm 58.1 mm

The 2F-85's TCP is the pads' centre, 14.3 mm short of Menagerie's pinch site (at the pads' tips) on the same axis.

A grasp's gripper pose follows from its TCP target p, approach â and closing ĉ (world unit vectors, orthogonal) by matching the bases B_l = [c, a, c × a] (gripper frame) and B_w = [ĉ, â, ĉ × â] (world):

R = B_w B_lᵀ,    position = p − R t

ik_targets gives the TCP frame G·[I | t] and the offset to pass as tcp= to core.robot.ik.solve_batch, so a position-first solve (the SO-100's five joints) puts the grasp centre, not the frame's origin, on the target. The TCP is the one the set was made with (settings['gripper']['tcp'], which records a sampler's tcp override) when it names the same frame, else the one measured now (an imported set).

The samplers

n grasps go round-robin over the kinds asked for; one numpy.random.default_rng(rng_seed) draws everything, so the same seed and settings give the same set. World z is up (REP-103); "heading" φ is the azimuth of the horizontal part of the approach.

Target points. With task objects whose bounding-box centre is inside the task volume, each grasp picks one uniformly and uses its world bounding box (centre c, extents e). Without objects: x, y uniform over the volume's footprint; the support height is the highest table surface (workspace.surfaces, a rectangle at height z) under the point within the volume's height range, else the volume's floor; the TCP is height_m (default 30 mm) above it, capped at the volume's top.

Kind Approach â Closing ĉ TCP on an object
top_down (top-down pinch) (0, 0, −1) heading uniform over 180° (volume points); across the narrower horizontal side of the object's box, turned by up to ±yaw_jitter_deg (15°) top − min(depth_m, e_z/2) above the centre; depth_m defaults to half the pads' length along the approach
side (cos φ, sin φ, 0) (−sin φ, cos φ, 0) the centre
angled (cos φ sin θ, sin φ sin θ, −cos θ), θ = angled_tilt_deg (45°) from vertical (−sin φ, cos φ, 0) the centre
handoff (bimanual handoff) (cos φ, sin φ, 0) (−sin φ, cos φ, 0) targets uniform in the handover volume, in mid-air

For side and angled grasps φ is the heading from the robot's base to the target, turned by a uniform amount within ±spread_deg (60°): the gripper moves outward, as an arm reaching from its base does. For handoff φ is the heading from this robot's base to the partner's base (the other robot nearest the handover volume; without one, through the volume's centre), within ±handoff_spread_deg (20°); the partner's uid is in the settings. The handover volume is handover_uid or the first volume of kind handover; without one, asking for handoff grasps is refused with what to add.

Every option is a DexVantage default, not a standard; settings.options records the values used and settings.defaults lists the ones left at their default. Sampling 2 000 grasps takes 25 ms on either sample (one core of an AMD Ryzen 9 9900X).

Imported files

CSV: a header row naming at least id, x, y, z, qx, qy, qz, qw (metres; the gripper frame's quaternion x, y, z, w in the world), optionally ax, ay, az (approach), kind, object (or object_uid), width_m and t; columns in any order, case ignored; commas, semicolons or tabs (a semicolon or tab file may write decimal commas); blank lines and # lines skipped. JSON: a list of objects with id and either pose: {position, quaternion}, position and quaternion, or the CSV's keys, with the same optional keys (approach as a list); or a grasp set record with poses. A row without an approach gets the gripper's approach axis turned by its orientation (the frame's z axis when no robot is given; the set's settings say which).

Refused, each with the line (CSV) or item (JSON) and the column: a cell that is not a finite number, a missing required column, a quaternion of zero length, an approach of zero length or given in part, an unknown kind, a blank id, a row with the wrong number of values, the same id twice, an empty file, more than 100 000 grasps. Normalised with a note: quaternions not of unit length. Noted: positions more than 5 m from the origin ("the file must be in metres").

From a trajectory

Two modes (the Tasks page's Where it closes and Every N samples). In both the trajectory is played on the robot's model (joints it does not name at home; values clipped to the limits, as playing it does), the gripper frame's world pose comes from forward kinematics, the kind is trajectory and t the sample's time.

Every N samples (mode='every', every=N): samples 0, N, 2N, …, the approach the gripper's approach axis there.

Where it closes (mode='closures', the page's default): the grasps the trajectory performs.

  • The opening at each sample (pad_opening): with the contact pads' centroids c_i carried with their links and c̄ their mean, opening = 2 · mean_i |c_i − c̄|, which for two pads is the distance between them (the 2F-85 on the UR5e sample: 85.4 mm open).
  • The closing strokes (closure_events): with Δ_k the change of opening from sample k to k + 1, a stroke starts at a fall Δ_k < −FLAT (0.1 mm, a default), runs while no Δ exceeds +FLAT (a pause is part of it) and ends at its last fall. It counts when the opening fell by at least close_mm (5 mm, a default) over it.
  • The grasp is the gripper frame at the stroke's onset (the arm in place, the fingers still open, as the grasp study poses them); the approach is the unit vector from the TCP at the last sample at least approach_mm (20 mm, a default) away from the onset's TCP, to the onset's TCP; without such a sample (the trajectory starts at the grasp) the gripper's approach axis, said in the set's notes.
  • The set's settings list each stroke: its onset and end times, how far the opening fell and the opening at the onset, and which of close_mm and approach_mm are defaults. A trajectory in which the gripper never closes that far is refused with its opening range; a robot with fewer than two fingertips or pads tagged is refused (use every N samples).

Validation

validation/checks_grasps.py, measured on 2026-11-06 (full run; quick run the same to the digits shown):

grasp.pads_vs_mujoco_boxes the 2F-85's pad box geoms from MuJoCo's compiled data (geom_xpos, geom_xmat, geom_size after mj_kinematics); the faces facing each other at home, carried to 40 random arm and finger joint vectors 1 × 10⁻⁶ m 3.6 × 10⁻¹¹ m; normals on the inner face 80 of 80

grasp.object_points_box a rotated box's closed-form signed distance; each face's share of points against its share of area (4σ binomial) 1 × 10⁻⁹ m 9.7 × 10⁻¹⁷ m; worst face 0.81σ
grasp.trajectory_closures the opening against the distance between the 2F-85 pads' inner-face centres built from MuJoCo's compiled box geoms, along a scripted pick (90 samples: lower, close, lift, open, move, close, open, a 0.01 rad twitch); the strokes found against the sample ranges the script closes over 1 × 10⁻⁶ m, strokes exact 6.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ m; strokes (1.25–1.75 s, 3.50–3.75 s) as scripted, the twitch (0.6 mm) not a grasp (measured 2026-11-07)

Why these tolerances: the meshes are float32 (3 × 10⁻⁸ m at half a metre) and everything else double, so 10⁻⁶ m catches a wrong face (8 mm away) or frame by four orders of magnitude; the pads are mirror images about the pinch site's axis, so 0.5° (1.3 mm over 145 mm) is rounding plus mesh export; the closed forms involve nothing but double precision.

Known limits

  • The samplers do not check reach or collisions: the grasp study's IK does and reports why a grasp is infeasible. On the SO-100 (five joints) most side grasps are unreachable by construction (5 of 30 in the worked example).
  • Object grasps use the object's axis-aligned world bounding box, not its shape: a grasp on a rotated or concave object can miss it, and width_m is an upper bound.
  • The closing axis is fixed by the kind (a side grasp always closes horizontally); there is no sampler for grasps along an object's own axes or from a grasp-quality model.
  • The contact patch and the TCP are measured at the home pose. A gripper whose home is closed and asymmetric gets a biased TCP; the 2F-85's linkage joints are independent entries of q (BUILD_STATE), so a q that moves one finger joint alone carries its pad where the real linkage cannot.
  • The inner-face rule takes outward normals away from each geom's centroid: right for convex geoms, possibly wrong for faces of a strongly concave mesh used as a fingertip's collision geometry.
  • 'dataset' sets arrive with the dataset readers (Phase 8). An imported row's approach is not checked against its orientation.
  • Grasps found in a trajectory are its closing strokes: a close without an object (a test close, a regrasp) is a grasp too, and fingers that move less than 0.1 mm between two samples are not seen to close. The grasp is posed with the fingers at their home opening, as every set is.
