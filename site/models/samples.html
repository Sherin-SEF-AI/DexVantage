The ALOHA 2, bimanual Panda and G1 samples

Modules: dexvantage/core/robot/bimanual_samples.py (the three builders), dexvantage/core/robot/samples.py (SAMPLES, BUNDLED, sample_project). Bundling: scripts/bundle_samples.py. Validation: dexvantage/validation/checks_samples.py (model samples). Tests: tests/test_samples_bimanual.py. Self-test line: samples. Batch: dexvantage --sample aloha|panda-bimanual|g1 … with any study flag.

Spec §11 names five samples (--sample aloha|so100|ur5e|panda-bimanual|g1) and §14 says what each holds. This page describes how the three bimanual and humanoid ones are built from their official model files, what was changed in those files and why, and how each change was checked.

ALOHA 2 (aloha)

Two robots, not one. aloha.xml holds both ViperX 300 arms in one file. The studies that work per gripper (the grasp study, the teleop study's follower, the hand-eye plan, the grasp sampler's handoff partner) take one gripper frame per robot, so the sample opens each arm as a robot of its own. scripts/bundle_samples.py writes aloha_left.xml and aloha_right.xml with mujoco.MjSpec: the other arm, the actuators, the gripper equality constraints, the keyframes and the world cameras deleted, the arm's base_link moved to the file's origin, and a keyframe home written with this arm's values of aloha.xml's neutral_pose. The sample places the arms with the base poses of aloha.xml (parsed from the bundled file as the sample is built): the left arm is the primary robot (the world is its base frame, REP-103) and the right arm stands at (0.938, 0, 0) m turned half a turn about z.

One state vector for recordings. Each arm keeps aloha.xml's joint names (left/waist … left/right_finger, right/waist …), so an ALOHA recording that stores both arms in one vector is read onto each arm by name (core/datasets/joints.py: a dataset column a model does not name is ignored).

Cameras. The ALOHA 2 paper (arXiv 2405.02292v1, read 2026-11-07): "We upgrade the cameras used in the ALOHA system to 4 RealSense D405 cameras" and "All four cameras record 848 x 480 rgb images" (§2.5); of the Menagerie model, "We precisely model all cameras and robot positions of the ALOHA 2 specification". The four sensors are therefore library D405s (intel-realsense-d405) with the 848 × 480 stream, posed from Menagerie's own cameras: the wrist cameras wrist_cam_left / wrist_cam_right of aloha.xml (riding on */gripper_base), and overhead_cam and worms_eye_cam of scene.xml (static). scene.xml itself is not bundled (its table and frame meshes add 4.9 MB): its two frame cameras and the table's collision box are copied with their attributes into aloha/scene_parts.xml. A MuJoCo camera looks along its −z with +y up; the sensor body frame is x forward, y left, z up, so R_body = R_cam · diag(1, −1, −1) · B, B the body-to-optical rotation of core/frames.py. The sensors carry the names of the ALOHA recordings, cam_high, cam_low, cam_left_wrist, cam_right_wrist (tonyzhaozh/aloha aloha_scripts/constants.py, read 2026-11-07).

Table. scene.xml's collision box table (1.22 × 0.74 m, its top 0.9 mm below aloha.xml's origin; the paper's "48" x 30" table"), 20.9 mm below the arms' base plane.

Teleop. Two leader arms from the library's ALOHA leader entry (aloha-widowx-250-leader, its figures with their sources): "two ViperX 6-DoF arms … along with 2 smaller WidowX arms … The WidowX contains the same kinematic structure as the ViperX … The follower joints are synchronized with the leader arms" (the paper, §1). Each leads its follower joint to joint; the WidowX's own joint ranges are not stated, so the follower's are used (the device record says so: an assumption). With two followers the teleop study's bimanual part runs: reach overlap, the handover zone's quality and the arm-to-arm margin.

Sample choices (labelled "(sample)"): a 0.50 × 0.40 × 0.20 m tabletop volume between the arms and a 0.20 × 0.20 × 0.15 m handover zone above the table's middle; a handover trajectory per arm (each TCP to 50 mm short of the handover zone's centre along the line between the bases, the right gripper closes, both go home; position-only IK from the waypoint before); grasp sets: 100 top-down and angled grasps per arm (TCP 60 mm above the table) and 60 bimanual handoffs for the left arm (its partner the right arm); the policy ACT (Zhao et al. 2023, trained on ALOHA).

Franka Panda bimanual (panda-bimanual)

There is no official bimanual Franka model (spec §14.4). The cell is two instances of Menagerie's single-arm franka_emika_panda/panda.xml with the Franka Hand: the file is imported once and the second robot is a copy of its records and files under a new uid (one compiled model serves both). The left arm is the primary robot; the right arm stands 0.8 m along −y, both facing +x (a sample layout). Each hand carries a wrist RealSense D405 0.07 m off the tool axis looking at the pinch point, and a RealSense D435 stands 0.85 m in front of the table looking back at the arms (sample placements). The teleop devices are the library's GELLO entry ("we build a GELLO controller device that has the same kinematic structure as the target arm", Wu et al. 2023), one per arm. Volumes, the handover trajectories, the grasp sets (100 top-down, side and angled grasps per arm, 60 handoffs) and Diffusion Policy are sample choices.

Mesh conversion. panda.xml uses 59 OBJ visual meshes (34.3 MB of text). They are rewritten as binary STL with the same triangles (float32), named <stem>_obj.stl because the folder already has STL collision meshes of the same stem (link1.stl beside link1.obj); a <mesh> that had no name is given its old file stem, the name MuJoCo gave it. The script compares every triangle of every geom at the home keyframe with the original: 0 m.

Unitree G1 upper body (g1)

The model. Menagerie's g1_with_hands.xml: both arms (7 joints each) with a three-finger hand each (7 joints: thumb 3, index 2, middle 2). Menagerie does not name the hand, so it is not called Dex3.

Legs and waist held. g1_upper_body.xml is g1_with_hands.xml with the free joint floating_base_joint deleted and the pelvis placed at the stand keyframe's base pose (0, 0, 0.79 m, upright), and its 12 leg joints and 3 waist joints deleted. At stand all 15 are 0, so deleting them leaves every body exactly where stand puts it (the script refuses if one is not 0). q is the 28 arm and hand joints, in one state vector; no joint is shared by the two arms, so IK on one arm leaves the other put (manipulability.chain_mask). stand puts the thumbs' second joint at ±1.05 rad, 0.0028 rad past its ±1.0472 rad limit; DexVantage's home clips it to the limit.

Mesh simplification (measured). The 49 meshes g1_with_hands.xml uses are 31.5 MB of STL (629 338 triangles, CAD exports: a 4 cm finger link has 30 434 triangles); the package budget is 12 MB per robot. They are simplified by quadric edge collapse (fast-simplification 0.2.0, optional extra bundle; aggressiveness 1 and 3 tried, the fewest triangles within tolerance kept) with a bisection on the reduction, each mesh as far as its two-sided Hausdorff distance from the original stays within 0.5 mm (a DexVantage choice: under 1.5 px of the head D435's 1920 × 1080 colour stream at 0.5 m). The distance is measured on each mesh's vertices plus 40 000 area-weighted samples of its surface against the other's exact surface (point-to-triangle distance, Ericson 5.1.5, with an exact candidate bound), both ways. Result: 629 338 triangles became 216 325 (10.82 MB of STL; the folder with its XML and LICENSE 10.9 MB); the largest distance of any mesh is 0.497 mm (the hand links, which were reduced most: a 9 518-triangle finger base to 2 376), the median 0.45 mm; one mesh (pelvis_contour_link) could not be reduced within the tolerance and is kept as it was. The simplified files are named <stem>.simplified.stl, the comment at the top of unitree_g1/g1_with_hands.xml says so, and so do the robot's source note and the project notes. Where the geometry matters to a millimetre, import the full model with Robot ▸ Import from MuJoCo Menagerie (unitree_g1).

Head camera. Unitree's own URDF g1_29dof_rev_1_0.urdf (unitreerobotics/unitree_ros, commit 7c40519, read 2026-11-07) has d435_joint: torso_link → d435_link, xyz (0.0576235, 0.01753, 0.42987) m, rpy (0, 0.8307767239493009, 0) rad (47.6° down). Its torso_link and head_link offsets equal Menagerie's (the head's (0.0039635, 0, −0.044) m), so the frame is placed on Menagerie's torso_link as written. Unitree's G1 page states only "Depth Camera+3D LiDAR", so the RealSense D435 is the model the frame's name gives, and the frame is taken as the camera's body frame (x along the optical axis); the URDF does not say which point of the camera it marks.

Palm and thumb contact. Menagerie's own collision meshes of the palm (on *_wrist_yaw_link) and the thumb's middle link (*_hand_thumb_1_link) overlap by 0.27 mm at stand (mj_geomDistance on the original files; the simplified meshes give the same). The two are hinged together through the thumb's first link, so g1_upper_body.xml excludes the pair from contact (<contact><exclude>, as MuJoCo excludes a parent and child); without it every arm pose counted as a self-collision and the hand-eye planner found no pose.

Hand frames. The palm mesh is a geom of right_wrist_yaw_link (there is no palm body), so the gripper frame is right_wrist_yaw_link and the fingertips right_hand_thumb_2_link, right_hand_index_1_link, right_hand_middle_1_link; one gripper per robot, so the left hand moves with q but is not the grasp study's gripper.

Sample choices. A chest-high table 0.40 × 0.90 m, its top 0.08 m above the wrists at stand (0.80 m; at stand the arms hang beside the hips), its near edge 0.20 m in front of the pelvis: there the head camera's view (47.6° down) and the hands' reach meet (at a 0.62 m table the camera saw the volume but the right hand reached few angled grasps, and the hands were out of view). A 0.20 × 0.50 × 0.16 m tabletop volume, a handover zone between the hands and "Right hand's reach (sample)", 0.15 × 0.20 × 0.10 m, where the right hand can hold an angled grasp (over the whole tabletop about 80 % of angled grasps are out of its reach); one trajectory moving both arms (each wrist TCP to a point over the table, the right hand closing along its sample power posture, core/dexhand.py default_synergies); 100 angled grasps for the right hand in its reach volume; the multi-finger study's joint state set to the trajectory's reach (the hands over the table, open), not stand; GR00T N1.5. The teleop device is the library's Apple Vision Pro entry, as Unitree's xr_teleoperate drives the G1 ("Wear your XR headset (e.g. Apple Vision Pro, PICO4, etc.)"); no source states its tracking volume, so it is left empty and the teleop study says so.

Validation

checks_samples.py, every reference MuJoCo compiling the bundled files directly (not DexVantage's importer, model or mounts):

Check Reference Tolerance (why) Measured
samples.aloha_arms every link of both arm robots against aloha.xml (both arms in one file), left base frame, 50 random joint vectors 10⁻⁶ m (to_xml writes about six significant digits) 4.6 × 10⁻⁷ m
samples.aloha_cameras the four sensors' world poses against MuJoCo's camera frames (mj_camlight) of aloha.xml and scene_parts.xml 10⁻⁶ m (same rounding) 5.1 × 10⁻⁷ m
samples.g1_held_legs every link of the upper body against g1_with_hands.xml at stand, 51 joint vectors 10⁻⁶ m 0 m
samples.g1_head_camera the head camera against Unitree's d435_joint composed on MuJoCo's torso_link (R = Rz Ry Rx written out) 10⁻¹² m (rounding only) 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁶ m
samples.panda_instances both Pandas against panda.xml's bodies moved by each base pose 10⁻⁹ m 0 m

The bundling script measures what cannot be checked without the originals: the arm files against aloha.xml (4.5 × 10⁻⁷), the frame cameras and table against scene.xml (0), the Panda's triangles against the OBJ files (0 m), the G1 meshes' Hausdorff distances (above) and the upper body against the full model (0). The robot catalogue check (robots.catalogue_dof) recounts the bundled aloha.xml, panda.xml and g1_with_hands.xml against its records.

Known limits

  • ALOHA 2: the grippers' two fingers are coupled by an equality constraint in aloha.xml that the arm files leave out (kinematics treats each finger joint on its own, as for the 2F-85); the WidowX leader arms are modelled with the followers' own joints and ranges; the aluminium frame and the camera mounts of scene.xml are not occluders (only the table is, as a surface).
  • Panda: the cell layout, the camera placements and the GELLO leaders are sample choices; the two arms share one compiled model, so a change to one robot's file does not apply to the other.
  • G1: the simplified meshes differ from Menagerie's by up to the measured Hausdorff distance; the legs and waist do not move and the pelvis is fixed in the world (a humanoid that steps, squats or bends at the waist is not modelled); the head camera's model is inferred from its frame's name; the stand keyframe's thumb value is clipped to its limit.
  • Every study that works per gripper sees one gripper per robot (the G1's right hand).
  • The G1's head camera rides on torso_link, which no joint moves here: the hand-eye planner and solve treat it as a static camera (core/calib/records.py camera_mount, a link no joint moves counts as the world), but the silhouette calibration still asks for a camera not on a robot link and refuses it.
  • The synthetic episodes (--synth-episodes) move one robot, so an ALOHA or Panda campaign records one arm's joints.
