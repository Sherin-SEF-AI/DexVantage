Time sync: a worked example on the UR5e sample

Is each image labelled with the joint state of the moment it was taken? The Time sync page adds up how far each camera's frame stamps are from the instant of exposure, against the robot's joint_states. It turns that skew into the action-label error: how far the end effector moves in that time along your trajectory. The model, its sources and its validation are in Time sync.

1. Open the sample and run

  1. File ▸ Open sample ▸ UR5e (the wrist D405, the overhead D435 and the 4.5 s scripted pick).
  2. Analysis ▸ Time sync opens the page (Placement workspace). Press Run (0.2 s).

No clocks or triggers are set, so every stream is taken as stamped by the host when its frame arrives, and free-running. That is the default, and the notes say so. The headline reads ≥ 54.43 ms · out of sync.

The budget of the Wrist D405 · IR left stream lists each term with its source:

Term Low (ms) High (ms) From
exposure centre to the end of the exposure 0 16.7 no exposure set: up to the frame period at 30 fps; no minimum stated
readout after the exposure (global shutter) 0 33.3 readout not stated by Intel: at most the frame period
USB transfer of the frame 4.30 4.30 1.94 MB per frame at 3600 Mbit/s (the device's own USB 3.2 Gen 1)
USB bus interval 0 0.125 125 µs microframes (Microsoft Learn; USB 2.0 §5.8.4)

Two terms are not counted. The D405 is not assigned to a host controller on the Bandwidth page, so its wait behind other devices is not bounded. The processing inside the device before it sends the frame is not stated by Intel. That is why the figure says "≥": the real skew can only be larger. joint_states has its own unstated term, the robot controller's latency to the host.

At the pick's peak TCP speed (0.17 m/s), the skew moves the end effector by up to 9.19 mm: the action-label error, shown in the Label error column. The 3D view draws the TCP's path and joins the TCP at the exposure to the TCP at the stamp at the worst moment. Show worst moment poses the robot there, and the Camera view draws the same segment.

2. The hardware trigger

The suggestion reads: Trigger Overhead D435 in hardware. It quotes Intel: "For D435, D435i, D435f and D435if, only the depth sensor can be synced by an external trigger" (§3.7.4). It predicts the D435 IR stream's worst skew falling from 54.4 ms to 16.7 ms, and its label error from 9.19 mm to 2.82 mm. What is left is half the exposure. With a fixed exposure (the Motion blur page) that becomes a constant you can subtract.

Apply sets the trigger in project.rig['sync'] (one undo step) and runs again. The D435's RGB stream stays free-running: Intel's statement covers only the depth sensor, and the budget says so. The next suggestion concerns the D405. Intel states it "does not support the hardware sync signal" (§7.13), so the advice is to measure its arrival latency, or to stamp on a PTP-disciplined clock. The page refuses a hardware trigger on the D405 with Intel's words.

3. Make the budget complete

  • Bandwidth page: add a host and plug both cameras into its USB 3 controller. The transfer term then becomes 1.90 to 7.60 ms for two 848 × 480 16-bit streams per camera on one USB 3.2 Gen 1 controller: the stream's frame alone, up to behind one frame of every other stream.
  • Motion blur page: set the exposure (for example 1 ms).
  • Measured latencies: enter each stream's arrival latency in the settings latency_s, for example from the RealSense frame metadata (the frame timestamp against the arrival time), with its source, and joint_states' too. With these, every stream's budget is complete.
  • Clocks: for a PTP or NTP clock, enter the offset, jitter and drift your servo reports, with their source. The study never assumes them.
  • Labels: choose interpolated labels (the default), or the nearest joint_states sample with its rate in Hz and its source.

The camera pair

The pairs table shows the wrist and overhead cameras. Both are free-running at 30 fps, so frames matched by timestamp can be up to 16.7 ms apart, on top of the difference in their stamp errors. A sync cable would lock them, but Intel states the D405 has none ("Sync cable possible: no").

Batch

dexvantage --sample ur5e --time-sync sync.json          # 0.7 s with start-up; CPU only

The SO-100 sample's cameras have no library device. With no frame rate, the budget has only the bus interval and says incomplete; choose the cameras' devices on the Sensors page.
