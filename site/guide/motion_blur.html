Motion blur and rolling shutter: a worked example on the UR5e sample

Will the images be sharp while the arm moves? The Motion blur page follows each camera's view along the project's trajectories. It reports how far the image smears during one exposure and how far a rolling shutter skews it. It also gives the longest exposure that keeps the smear within your limit. The model, its equations and its validation are in Motion blur and rolling shutter.

1. Open the sample and run

  1. File ▸ Open sample ▸ UR5e. The rig has a wrist D405 on the flange and an overhead D435 on the cage. The sample also carries a 4.5 s scripted pick (Scripted pick (sample), a sample motion, not a recording). From home the arm lowers over 1 s, holds for 0.5 s, closes the 2F-85 over 0.5 s, lifts for 1 s and carries the part sideways for 1.5 s.
  2. Analysis ▸ Motion blur and rolling shutter opens the page (Placement workspace). Press Run (0.3 s).

The headline reads up to 38.04 px · may be blurred: the wrist D405's IR left imager at t = 3.03 s, seeing the task volume. "Up to" is there because no exposure is set, so the study uses the longest exposure the stream allows: the frame period at 30 fps, 33.3 ms. That is an upper bound, not an Intel figure. The table:

Camera Shutter Exposure (ms) Image speed (px/s) Smear (px) Skew (px) Longest exposure (ms)
Wrist D405 · IR left global 33.3 (at most) 1 141 38.04 0 0.876
Wrist D405 · RGB global 33.3 (at most) 1 051 35.04 0 0.951
Overhead D435 · IR left global 33.3 (at most) 146 4.86 0 6.85
Overhead D435 · RGB rolling 33.3 (at most) 312 10.40 up to 10.40 3.21

The wrist camera rides on the flange, so the whole scene streams past it: its twist J(q)·q̇ turns it at the wrist's angular velocity. The overhead camera stands still and sees only the gripper move (its worst point is the left pad). The D435's RGB camera has a rolling shutter. Intel does not state its readout time, so the skew is bounded by the frame period: "up to 10.40 px".

The segments table follows the motion:

  • lowering (0–1 s): 35.0 px;
  • the hold (1.0–1.5 s): 0 px, sharp;
  • closing (1.5–2.0 s): 16.0 px, the pads moving in the wrist camera's view;
  • lifting (2–3 s): 27.0 px;
  • carrying (3.0–4.5 s): 38.0 px.

The 3D view draws the TCP's path in those segments' status colours.

2. Look at the worst frame

Choose the Wrist D405 · IR left row, pick its first worst frame and press Show frame. The robot is posed at t = 3.03 s, and the Camera view switches to that camera and imager. It draws each point's smear vector, lengthened when short; the legend says by how much.

3. Apply the suggestion

The suggestion reads: Set Wrist D405: exposure at most 0.87 ms (smear up to 38.04 px → 0.99 px); Overhead D435: exposure at most 3.2 ms (smear up to 10.40 px → 1.00 px). It adds that the D435's rolling shutter skews the image by up to 10.40 px, and that a global-shutter camera has none; the library's are the D405, D455 and D457.

Apply records these exposures as yours in one undo step, and the study runs again. Every camera is now sharp, within 1 px (the default limit, a DexVantage default, not a standard). The skew is not changed by the exposure, so the rolling-shutter advice stays.

Set the same exposure on the cameras, for example in the RealSense viewer or your driver's parameters. A shorter exposure needs more light or gain; the Lighting page treats that. Intel states the D435 depth imagers' manual exposure as 1 to 166 ms (datasheet Table 4-23), so 3.2 ms is inside its range. For the D405 the datasheet's exposure table does not name the module, so no range is checked.

4. Your own figures

  • Your exposure: pick a camera, type the exposure in ms and where it comes from (for example "driver setting, 2026-11-07"), then press Set exposure (one undo step). Clear goes back to the bound.
  • Smear limit: your tolerance in pixels (default 1 px).
  • Readout: the study takes a measured rolling-shutter readout from the settings readout_s ({sensor uid: {value, source}}).

The SO-100 sample: no trajectory, no device

The SO-100 sample has no trajectory, so the study uses 8 approaches of its grasp set: 0.10 m along the approach at 0.10 m/s, both defaults, solved by IK. Its cameras have no library device, so there is no frame rate and no exposure. The headline says exposure not set. The page still gives the image speed (up to 1 559 px/s for the wrist camera) and the longest exposure for 1 px (0.64 ms).

Batch

dexvantage --sample ur5e --motion-blur blur.json        # 0.8 s with start-up; CPU only (MuJoCo kinematics)

The result is JSON with a provenance block; --reproduce blur.json runs it again and compares.
