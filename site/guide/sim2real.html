The sim-to-real gap: a worked example

How far is the render of your cell from what the cameras recorded, and what would close the gap? This walk-through uses the UR5e sample and a recording with four known gaps on its overhead D435. It reads the ranked causes and applies them one at a time. The method, its equations, checks and limits are in docs/models/sim2real.md.

The recording here is synthetic, so every gap is known. On a real rig it is your LeRobot, RLDS or ROS 2 bag recording, made with the rig the project describes.

A. A recording with known gaps

From Python (no window):

import dexvantage as dv
rig = dv.sample('ur5e')
rig.gap_dataset('out/s2r_gaps', {'camera': 'observation.images.overhead_d435', 'brightness_dn': 20,
                                 'blur_px': 1.5, 'latency_s': 0.04,
                                 'mount': {'position_m': [0.003, 0, 0], 'rotation_deg': [0, 1.0, 0]}},
                episodes=1, frames=40, size=[320, 180], seed=4)

This renders one 40-row episode of the sample's pick through both cameras (the synthetic episode generator: its mount slip and camera–joint offset faults). It then brightens the overhead frames by 20 DN and blurs them with σ 1.5 px before the video codec writes them. It takes 1.6 s. dexvantage_truth.json beside the data lists what was injected under sim2real_gaps.

(Simulate ▸ Generate synthetic episodes… gives a recording with the mount slip and offset faults; the photometric gaps are only in the API and the checks.)

B. Run it (Data ▸ Sim-to-real gap)

  1. Open the sample: File ▸ Open sample ▸ UR5e.
  2. On the page, click Dataset… and choose out/s2r_gaps. The choice is one undo step and is stored with the project. Without a choice the page uses the audit's dataset.
  3. Click Run. The study takes 7–8 s on the RTX 5080: about 330 renders per camera, plus the silhouette calibration of the overhead camera.

The headline reads 24.4 DN, "very large gap": the overhead camera's mean absolute difference between the recorded frames and their render, before any correction. The wrist D405, which got no gap, reads 1.1 DN.

C. Read it

The Recorded / Rendered / Difference row shows a frame of the chosen camera. Choose Rendered with every correction and the difference image goes almost black. Difference over the recording lays the difference in red over the recorded frame.

The list What explains the gap gives each correction's Shapley share: what it removes, averaged over every order of applying the corrections.

# cause removes share figure fixed on
1 Exposure and colour 14.6 DN 60 % gain 1.00, 0.99, 1.00, offset +19, +19, +19 DN Sim-to-real gap
2 Mount offset 3.2 DN 13 % 6.7 mm, 1.31° Silhouette calibration
3 Blur 2.4 DN 10 % σ 1.43 px Motion blur
4 Camera latency 2.1 DN 9 % +39 ms ± 1 ms Time sync

The four shares add up to what all four corrections remove together. The 2.1 DN left is the scene's own (codec and noise): a clean recording reads 1.8 DN.

The Figures of the camera tree gives more detail:

  • histogram EMD of about 18 DN per channel, sharpness ratio 0.01 (the recording is far blurrier than the render);
  • noise σ 0.11 DN recorded against 0.42 rendered (the blur removed the noise);
  • silhouette IoU from 0.53 to 0.89 (background difference: the dataset stores no masks);
  • latency 39.3 ms against the time-sync study's prediction for the stream;
  • MMD² 0.142 (p 0.015) as planned and −0.084 with every correction (a kernel MMD on thumbnails, not FID).

The mount reads 6.7 mm against a true 3 mm, because the blur and the brightness weaken the silhouette's edges. On the same recording without them it recovers 1.08° for the true 1° and is 2.0 mm from the true 3 mm (see the model doc: a 3 mm translation is not resolved at 320 × 180).

D. Close it

Apply makes the top suggestion (or the row you click) in one undo step. Preview says what it would change, and Open page goes to the page that owns the setting. On this recording five passes of Run and Apply gave:

pass gap Apply
1 24.4 DN exposure: the render's camera response for the overhead D435 (gain and offset)
2 10.0 DN mount: the recovered mount written to the sensor
3 7.6 DN latency: −39 ms (stamp minus exposure) entered for the camera in the time-sync settings
4 4.6 DN blur: the render's blur σ for the camera
5 2.1 DN, "close to real" nothing left to suggest

From Python the same loop is:

res = rig.sim2real('out/s2r_gaps')
while res['apply']:
    rig = rig.apply_sim2real(res['apply'])
    res = rig.sim2real('out/s2r_gaps')

On a real rig the photometric suggestions say two things. First, what the simulator's camera response must be for its images to look like yours: export the result for your sim pipeline. Second, what to change on the camera instead: "lower the exposure or gain", "check the focus". When every camera shows the same gain, the cause is ranked as Lighting: the room's light differs from the project's. Add the room's lights on the Lighting page; with lights in the project, Apply scales them.

E. Batch and export

dexvantage --sample ur5e --sim2real out/s2r.json --dataset out/s2r_gaps

This writes the result with its provenance and exits 0. A missing dataset is refused in one sentence (exit 2). Export… on the page writes the same JSON. The named metrics are sim2real.image_gap_dn (the headline), sim2real.remaining_dn, sim2real.explained, sim2real.latency_ms, sim2real.mount_offset_mm and sim2real.silhouette_iou, for requirements, experiments and the API.
