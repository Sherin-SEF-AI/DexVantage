Isaac Lab, LeRobot and reports: a worked example on the UR5e sample

Export the UR5e sample's cameras to Isaac Lab, write the LeRobot dataset template and a train config that carries the action-chunk study's recommendation, and write a report. The model, the sources and the checks are in Exporters, part B: Isaac Lab and Isaac Sim, LeRobot and the policy codes, reports.

In the app: File ▸ Export… (or Export ▸ Export to MuJoCo, Isaac, ROS 2, LeRobot…), tick Isaac Lab and Isaac Sim cameras, LeRobot, ACT, Diffusion Policy and OpenVLA configs and Report (HTML and PDF), choose a folder. The dialog lists each format's files and notes. From Python:

from dexvantage.core import exporters as X, jobs
from dexvantage.core.robot.samples import sample_project
from dexvantage.core.studies import action_chunk_sync as AC

p = sample_project('ur5e')
AC.apply_recommendations(p, jobs.run_study('action_chunk_sync', p.snapshot()))   # the page's Apply
man = X.export(p, 'out/ur5e', ['isaac', 'lerobot', 'report'],
               {'report': {'studies': ['bandwidth', 'motion_blur', 'time_sync', 'action_chunk_sync']}})

1. Isaac Lab and Isaac Sim

out/ur5e/isaac/ holds cameras.usda (written by pxr when usd-core is installed: pip install "dexvantage[usd]"), isaaclab_cameras.py (Isaac Lab v3.0.0), isaaclab_v2_cameras.py (v2.3.2) and isaac_cameras.json. The sample has six imagers (the D405 on wrist_3_link and the D435 on the cage, each with IR left, IR right and RGB):

Camera Parent Size fx, fy (px) Lens in Isaac Lab Outputs
wrist_d405_ir_left wrist_3_link 1280 × 720 710.79, 649.46 OpenCV pinhole, zero terms (non-square) rgb, distance_to_image_plane
wrist_d405_rgb wrist_3_link 1280 × 720 710.79, 721.62 OpenCV pinhole, zero terms rgb
overhead_d435_rgb environment 1920 × 1080 1396.81, 1406.75 OpenCV pinhole, zero terms rgb

(The focal lengths come from the D405 and D435 entries' stated fields of view, so fx ≠ fy: Isaac Lab's plain pinhole would use fx for both, so each camera carries an OpenCV model with zero distortion that holds fx, fy, cx, cy.)

A camera config, v3.0.0:

WRIST_D405_RGB = CameraCfg(
    prim_path=ENV_NS + '/Robot/wrist_3_link/wrist_d405_rgb',
    update_period=0.03333333333333333,          # the D405's 30 fps stream
    width=1280, height=720, data_types=['rgb'],
    offset=CameraCfg.OffsetCfg(pos=(-0.0, 0.1308, 0.07), rot=(-0.8627, -0.0, 0.0, 0.5057), convention="ros"),
    spawn=sim_utils.PinholeCameraCfg(focal_length=0.5553..., horizontal_aperture=1.0, ..., distortion=...),
)

Add it to your scene config (scene.wrist = CAMERAS["wrist_d405_rgb"], the robot's prim named Robot, or pass {'isaac': {'robot_prim': 'MyArm'}}). The IR imagers are written as RGB prims with the IR imager's geometry (the notes say so: Isaac renders visible light).

Domain randomisation. The sample's mounts carry DexVantage's labelled default tolerance, ± 1 mm and ± 0.5° per axis (the notes say so); set your bracket's own on the Sensors page or the Tolerance page. Then, per training run:

from isaaclab_cameras import randomized_cameras, sample_offset, DR
cams = randomized_cameras(seed=3)               # every CameraCfg with one draw of its mount
pos, rot, d = sample_offset('wrist_d405_rgb')   # one draw: d = (dx, dy, dz) m, (droll, dpitch, dyaw) degrees

The draw moves the sensor body exactly as DexVantage's tolerance study does (formats.isaac_config compares it).

2. LeRobot, ACT, Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA

The action-chunk study recommends a delay offset of 0.1 s for the sample's π0 (5 frames at its 50 Hz control rate) and, for π0 (which executes chunks open loop), no ensemble weight. The export writes:

  • lerobot/v2.1/meta/info.json and lerobot/v3.0/meta/info.json: 14 state and action names (the UR5e's six joints and the Robotiq's eight linkage joints), observation.images.overhead_d435 (1080 × 1920 × 3) and observation.images.wrist_d405 (720 × 1280 × 3), fps 50. The notes say the 30 fps cameras repeat frames at 50 fps: record at 30 fps or pass {'lerobot': {'fps': 30}}.
  • lerobot/pi0_sample/train_config.json: policy type pi0, the two cameras as VISUAL [3, H, W] inputs, push_to_hub: false; train.sh runs lerobot-train --config_path=train_config.json (flags after it override, e.g. --steps=20000, or --policy.path=<a pretrained π0 checkpoint> to fine-tune). Set {'lerobot': {'repo_id': '<you>/<dataset>'}} for your dataset's name (default local/<project>).
  • lerobot/pi0_sample/delta_timestamps.json: {"action": [0.1, 0.12, …]}, the 50-step window starting 0.1 s after each observation, and make_dataset.py, which builds the LeRobotDataset with it (LeRobot's train config has no field for the offset).
  • act/task_config.yaml, diffusion_policy/task.yaml and openvla/oxe_dataset_config.py: the same cameras for the original codes (OpenVLA: primary overhead_d435, wrist wrist_d405).

With an ACT policy (Policies page, ACT), the train config gets temporal_ensemble_coeff = the study's m with n_action_steps: 1, and the notes say that ACT needs one image size for every camera (here 1280 × 720 and 1920 × 1080 differ).

3. The report

report/report.html opens in any browser (it follows the light or dark setting); report/report.pdf is the same on A4 pages. When the project has requirements (for example DexVantage's defaults, core/requirements.default_requirements()), the report starts with the scorecard; each study then has its STATUS word (for example on contact for the action-chunk study, no hosts for bandwidth, may be blurred for motion blur), its figures, a chart (payload per sensor, peak speed per camera) and its notes; studies that cannot run on the project are listed under Not run with their reason. The provenance block at the end gives the DexVantage version and code hash, the project's content hash and the time.

In batch, run the studies first and point the report at their files:

dexvantage ur5e.dexv --motion-blur res/blur.json --time-sync res/sync.json
python -c "from dexvantage.core import exporters as X; from dexvantage.core.project import Project; \
  X.export(Project.load('ur5e.dexv'), 'out', ['report'], {'report': {'results_dir': 'res'}})"
