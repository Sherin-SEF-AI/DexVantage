Tolerances: a worked example on the SO-100 sample

This carries the SO-100 sample's two mount tolerances (the wrist camera on the fixed jaw, the third-person camera on its tripod, both at the labelled defaults ± 1 mm, ± 0.5°) through a coverage requirement, tightens the mount that matters, and exports perturbed extrinsics for training. The model is in docs/models/tolerance.md. Figures below are from a run on 2026-11-07 at the default settings (64 draws, seed 0, uniform within the bounds).

1. A requirement to judge by

dexvantage --sample so100

The study carries the figures your requirements name. Add one for the share of the task volume seen, coverage.covered >= 94 % (the sample's nominal is 94.52 %). Without requirements the study carries the headline figures of coverage, depth validity, motion blur and VLA tokens (a default) and has no threshold to judge them by.

2. Run

Open Analysis ▸ Tolerances and press Run (about 9 s: each of the 64 draws re-runs the coverage study on the moved rig). The headline reads 94.34 %, status P5 · holds:

Task volume seen (>= 94.00 %, a default): 100 % of 64 draws meet it [94–100 %, Wilson 95 %]; P5 94.34 %, nominal 94.52 %.

The table shows the figure ± spread: mean 94.54 % ± 0.103 % (σ), [P5–P95 94.34–94.70 %]. Quick-study headlines on the other pages still show the design as it is (94.52 %); this page is where the spread shows.

The contribution chart says the wrist camera carries 77 % of the spread (largest |ρ| 0.49, against a noise level of 2 / √63 = 0.25) and the third-person camera 23 % (|ρ| 0.31). For the wrist camera, the rotation part is the larger (Σρ² 0.44 against 0.33 for position).

3. Tighten and run again

Preview draws the wrist camera's 64 perturbed frusta (to scale, out to the task volume) in its colour and, in green, the same draws at the tightened tolerance. Apply sets its rotation tolerance to ± 0.25° (one undo step). Run again: P5 94.39 %, mean 94.53 % ± 0.077 %, and now the wrist camera's position part dominates; the next suggestion is ± 0.5 mm. The per-sensor editor below the chart sets any mount's ± mm and ± ° directly, one undo step each.

A calibrated sensor (Calibrate ▸ Solve hand-eye, then Apply) draws from its calibration covariance instead of its mount tolerance; the editor's "Drawn from" column says which, and a sensor moved after its calibration goes back to its mount tolerance.

4. Export perturbed extrinsics

Export perturbations… writes 100 draws (seed 0; the n_export setting changes the count) to perturbations/perturbations.json (287 kB) and perturbations/perturbations.npz (85 kB), with an export_manifest.json holding their SHA-256 and provenance. In a script:

from dexvantage.core import exporters as X
X.export(project, 'out', ['perturbations'], options={'perturbations': {'n': 500, 'seed': 1}})

For Isaac Lab, each draw's isaac_lab entry is a CameraCfg.OffsetCfg in the camera's mount frame (the wrist camera's link Fixed_Jaw, the world for the tripod): pos, rot_xyzw (Isaac Lab v3.0 and later) or rot_wxyz (v2.x), convention="ros". Pick a draw per environment at reset to randomise the mounts. For the 3DGS renderer, the gs entry is the camera centre and camera-to-world rotation at the robot's home pose (OpenCV axes). For DexVantage's own MuJoCo renders, pass delta_body as the view's extrinsic_delta.

The same seed gives the same draws, in the export and in the study.

Batch

dexvantage project.dexv --tolerance-mc tolerance.json

writes the study's result (every draw's figures and mount changes) as JSON with provenance.
