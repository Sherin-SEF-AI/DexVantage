Grasp sets: a worked example on the UR5e and SO-100 samples

The grasp self-occlusion study asks, for many grasps, whether the cameras see the fingertips and the object at contact. Its input is a grasp set: gripper poses at contact with their approach direction. The Tasks page makes, imports, lists and deletes them. The model, the rules and their validation are in Grasp sets.

1. Sample a set on the UR5e

  1. File ▸ Open sample ▸ UR5e and Robotiq 2F-85 with a wrist D405 and an overhead D435.
  2. Open Tasks (Tasks ▸ Tasks). The sample comes with one set, "Tabletop grasps (sample)" (200 grasps: top-down, side and angled, seed 0), so the headline reads 200 grasps in 1 set. To follow these steps from an empty list, select it and press Delete set; the headline then reads 0 grasps · none yet.
  3. Under Sample a grasp set: robot "UR5e + Robotiq 2F-85", task volume "Tabletop (sample)", tick Top-down pinch, Side grasp and Angled, 90 grasps, seed 1.
  4. Press Preview: the grasps are drawn in the 3D view as dashed glyphs (a stem along the approach, a palm, two fingers ending at the grasp centre; blue top-down, orange side, green angled) and in the Camera view. Nothing is added yet.
  5. Press Add set. The headline reads 90 grasps in 1 set · ready; the table lists "Sampled: top-down pinch, side grasp, angled (90)", source sampled, 90 poses, 30 of each kind. Edit ▸ Undo removes it in one step. A set of 1 000 grasps or more is sampled on a worker thread (the status bar shows "Sampling … grasps"); the preview or the new set appears when it is done.

The status bar says why the grasps sit where they do: "No task object is in Tabletop (sample): grasp points are on its support surface, 30 mm up." The sample's volume holds no objects, so the grasp centres are 30 mm (a default) above the table, spread over the volume's footprint. The selected set's note gives the gripper geometry used: the grasp centre (TCP) 130.7 mm out along gripper_base's z axis, between the 2F-85's pads, measured from their inner faces at home.

What the UR5e can reach (IK with 8 seeds, self-collision checked, ik.solve_batch(..., tcp=…) through grasps.ik_targets): top-down 30 of 30, side 28 of 30 (2 "no convergence"), angled 29 of 30 (1 "self-collision").

2. Grasp an object

Add a box with Workspace ▸ Add object inside the task volume and sample again. The table's note now reads "Grasp points on 1 task object in Tabletop (sample)": top-down grasps close across the box's narrower side, their centre half the pads' length (18.5 mm) below its top; side and angled grasps centre on the box; each pose records the object's uid and width_m, the box's extent along the closing axis.

3. The SO-100

Open the SO-100 sample and sample the same three kinds. The gripper frame is Fixed_Jaw; the TCP sits between the jaws' inner faces, 82.9 mm along the jaw, with the jaws 21.8 mm apart at home. The arm has five joints, so IK reaches the grasp centre first and turns the jaw as far as it can: top-down 26 of 30 reached (orientation exact for 1), side 5 of 30 (14 "joint limit", 11 "out of reach"), angled 24 of 30. A side grasp needs a sixth joint, which the SO-100 does not have; keep to top-down and angled sets for it.

4. Import a file

Import file… reads CSV or JSON for the robot chosen in the form. A minimal CSV:

id,x,y,z,qx,qy,qz,qw,ax,ay,az,kind,object
a,-0.10,0.50,0.03,1,0,0,0,0,0,-1,top_down,
b,-0.20,0.45,0.05,0.7071068,0,0,0.7071068,,,,,cup

Positions are metres, the quaternion is the gripper frame's (x, y, z, w) in the world; a row without ax, ay, az gets the gripper's approach axis turned by its orientation. A mistake is named with its line and column, for example "Could not import grasps.csv: Line 3, column y: "abc" is not a number. Write positions in metres and the quaternion as four numbers (x, y, z, w)." A file in millimetres is imported with the note "1 position is more than 5 m from the origin: the file must be in metres, not millimetres."

5. From a trajectory

With a trajectory in the project (Robot ▸ Import trajectory…), pick it next to Add from trajectory.

  • Where it closes (the default) adds the grasps the trajectory performs: one each time the opening between the contact pads falls by 5 mm or more (a default), at the moment the fingers start to close, approaching the way the tool centre came in over its last 20 mm. On the UR5e sample with the scripted pick of the validation check (validation/checks_grasps.scripted_pick: lower, close, lift, open, move, close) the set holds two grasps, at 1.25 s and 3.50 s, and the message says "the gripper closes 2 times in the trajectory". Run the grasp study on it to see whether the cameras see the fingertips at those moments.
  • Every N samples adds the gripper frame at samples 0, N, 2N, …, each with its time.

Tick Swept volume to see where the arm goes along the chosen trajectory: the robot drawn see-through at 8 evenly spaced times (a default), fainter early and stronger late (core/robot/swept.py; the first time for a robot its meshes are simplified as a background job, a few hundred milliseconds).

5a. From a recorded dataset

From a dataset… (next to Import file…) reads the grasps a recording performs: each episode's joint states are read like a trajectory and one grasp is added each time the gripper closes by 5 mm or more (a default), exactly as Where it closes above. The dialog asks for the dataset (Folder… for a LeRobot v2.1 or v3.0 dataset, a ROS 2 bag folder or an RLDS folder; File… for a single .mcap or .db3 bag, or a LeRobot meta/info.json, which stands for its dataset), the episodes (all, or 0, 2-4), Where it closes or Every N rows, and the unit of the recorded joints (rad, or deg for recordings that store degrees). The set is for the robot chosen in the form above. The episodes are read on a worker thread (the status bar shows the progress); the set is added in one undo step.

Worked example on the UR5e sample (measured 2026-11-07):

  1. Simulate ▸ Generate synthetic episodes… with 2 episodes, the faults unticked, writes a LeRobot v3.0 dataset of the sample's scripted pick (2 × 136 rows at 30 fps, both cameras rendered in MuJoCo on the RTX 5080, 8.4 s).
  2. Tasks ▸ From a dataset…, Folder… that dataset, Read. The message says "Added 2 grasps from 2 episode(s) of synth: the gripper closes 2 times", and the set's poses are named e0c1 and e1c1 (episode 0, its first closing), each at 1.50 s: the row before the 2F-85's fingers start to move. Reading the two episodes took 24 ms; 200 episodes of the same pick, 27 200 rows, took 0.87 s (31 000 rows/s, on this machine's CPU).
  3. Run the grasp study on the set to see whether the cameras saw the fingertips at those moments.

Joint names are matched to the robot's by name; LeRobot's shoulder_pan.pos or main_shoulder_pan style names are also matched (the set's settings say which rule matched). A recording whose joints are not the robot's is refused in words ("it looks like another robot's recording"); one whose joints are degrees read as radians is said in the notes ("the values look like degrees"). A gripper stored as a percentage of its stroke, as some LeRobot SO-100 recordings do, is not a joint angle: convert it first.

6. Export and delete

Export… writes the selected set as JSON with the provenance block. Delete set removes it in one undo step.

In scripts

from dexvantage.core import grasps
from dexvantage.core.robot.samples import sample_project
p = sample_project('ur5e')
gs = grasps.sample(p, p.robots[0].uid, ['top_down', 'angled'], 500, rng_seed=1)
grasps.add_set(p, gs)
tips = grasps.contact_points(p, p.robots[0].uid, q)        # {'gripper_right_pad': (128, 3), ...}
approach = grasps.pre_contact_poses(gs['poses'][0], [0.02, 0.04, 0.06])
picks = grasps.from_trajectory(p, traj_uid, mode='closures')  # one grasp per closing of the gripper
rec = grasps.from_dataset(p, 'path/to/lerobot_dataset', p.robots[0].uid, episodes=[0, 1], unit='rad')
