Statistics: a worked example

Three questions a research team asks, answered on the SO-100 sample and on success counts. The model is in docs/models/stats.md. Figures below are from runs on 2026-11-07 at the default settings (alpha 0.05, power 0.80, Holm's correction, seed 0).

1. Is Diffusion Policy better than ACT on our task?

Twenty evaluation episodes each: ACT succeeded 14 times, Diffusion Policy 19 times. Open Analysis ▸ Statistics, keep Success counts, and enter the two rows (condition, successes, trials). The page runs as you type:

  • the headline: +25.0 pp, status no clear difference:

    Diffusion against ACT: no clear difference (Diffusion − ACT = +25.0 pp [+0.8, +47.3]; adjusted p = 0.0915). This does not show they are equal. With these episodes, only a true rate below 22 % (against 70 %) would be detected 80 % of the time.

  • the table: 14/20 (70 %) against 19/20 (95 %), the odds ratio 8.14 [0.878, 75.5] (Woolf), Fisher's exact test, p = 0.091; Wilson intervals 48–85 % and 76–99 % in the tooltip;
  • the note that the difference's interval (Newcombe) excludes zero while Fisher's test does not reject: the evidence is borderline, which twenty episodes cannot settle.

The assumptions are spelled out under the table: each episode an independent trial, one success probability per condition, the conditions' episodes independent of each other.

2. How many episodes would settle it?

The suggestion under the headline says: about 43 episodes per condition would tell 70 % from 95 % apart 80 % of the time (normal approximation). Apply writes this plan into the power calculator (one undo step); Preview shows it there without storing it. The calculator, method Exact (Fisher, every outcome), then answers 39 episodes per condition (78 in all) give 80.7 % power, and draws power against n with the target line, the n needed and the n planned. The other methods give 43 (normal approximation with the continuity correction) and 32 (arcsine): near a rate of 1 the arcsine formula is optimistic, which is why the exact enumeration is the default here.

From the command line, with a spec file:

{"schema": "dexvantage.stats/1", "source": "rates",
 "rates": [{"condition": "ACT", "successes": 14, "trials": 20},
           {"condition": "Diffusion", "successes": 19, "trials": 20}],
 "power": {"kind": "rates", "p1": 0.7, "p2": 0.95}}

dexvantage --stats spec.json            # → spec.results.json with provenance, exit 0

or in a notebook:

import dexvantage as dv
dv.stats.compare_rates([('ACT', 14, 20), ('Diffusion', 19, 20)]).headline['word']   # 'no clear difference'
dv.stats.episodes_needed(0.7, 0.95)['n_per_group']                                    # 39

Demonstrations instead of evaluation episodes: choose Demonstrations, a pass rate of 70 % against 90 %, 25 demonstrations per operator with an intra-class correlation of 0.05 and 10 % discarded by the audit: 176 demonstrations per condition (72 before the clustering and the discards). The calculator says what it does not model: how many demonstrations a policy needs to learn the task.

3. Does raising the third-person camera by 6 cm change coverage?

On the SO-100 sample, add a variant (Variants ▸ Add variant, a copy) and raise its third-person camera by 6 cm. On the Statistics page choose Variants, A the default (the active variant), B the copy, and figures coverage.covered, coverage.seen_twice; set 16 draws and press Run (about 4 s). Each variant's mounts are drawn within their tolerances with the same seed, so the draws are paired by seed:

Figure A B B − A [95 % BCa] p (permutation, 9 999 sign flips) Holm
Task volume seen 94.56 % 94.38 % −0.179 pp [−0.187, −0.172] 0.0002 0.0004
Seen by two or more cameras 47.21 % 46.32 % −0.890 pp [−0.896, −0.883] 0.0002 0.0004

Both differences are clear (A is better), because the paired draws cancel the mount noise: the raised camera loses 0.18 points of the task volume and 0.89 points of the two-camera share. Whether a 0.2-point loss matters is the team's call; the statistics say only that it is not chance. The same comparison with --stats and "source": "variants" in the spec, or dv.stats.compare_variants(rig, b='Rig B', metrics=[...], n=16).

Experiment and tracker runs

Experiment runs compares the last designed experiment's runs at a factor's two levels: a full factorial pairs the runs that share the other factors (with three pairs the smallest two-sided p is 2/8 = 0.25, so even a constant difference is not clear: the page says so through the p-value). Tracker runs compares two logged tolerance runs draw by draw (paired when they used the same seed) or two groups of runs (by ids or tag:NAME), one value per run.

What it will not do

It does not stop early: decide the episodes first, test once (sequential designs are out of scope). It does not call two conditions equal when the difference is not clear; it gives the smallest difference the data could have detected.
