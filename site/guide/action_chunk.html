Action-chunk latency: a worked example

The Action-chunk latency page (Analysis ▸ Action-chunk latency) says how late or early each of your action-chunked policies closes the gripper on the contact point, and what delay offset and ensemble weight to write into the training config. The model is in docs/models/action_chunk.md.

1. Start from the UR5e sample

  1. File ▸ Open sample ▸ UR5e. It carries the π0 policy (sample choice) and a scripted pick.
  2. Tasks ▸ Policies: add ACT and Diffusion Policy (CNN, real-world setting). Their horizons, executed steps, ensemble weights, inference times and control rates come from the library entries (each figure cited; the ACT entry's m = 0.01 from the official code).
  3. Analysis ▸ Bandwidth: add a host with one USB 3 controller and plug both cameras into it. The transfer time of each camera's frame then comes from that budget (9.5 ms for the D405's 1280 × 720 YUY2 frame, 15.8 ms for the D435's 1920 × 1080).
  4. For this example, remove the scripted pick (Tasks), so the study uses the default approach speed of 0.10 m/s (labelled a default on the page; with the pick in place the hand is still when it closes, and the error at contact is 0 mm).

2. Run it

Open Analysis ▸ Action-chunk latency and press Run (the study is quick and also re-runs after edits).

  • Headline: 28.3 mm, critical, "closes late": Diffusion Policy closes 50 to 283 ms after contact (nominal 167 ms): 100 ms inference, a 10 Hz control step to land on (±50 ms) and the tick wait. At 0.10 m/s that is 28.3 mm past the contact point.
  • ACT: 0 to 73 ms late, 7.3 mm. π0: 63 to 136 ms, 13.6 mm.
  • The loop bars show each camera's terms. Exposure and readout are listed as not stated (no maker in the library states them); the loop says "at least". Enter measured figures in the study settings (figures) to complete it; with the default host timestamps they do not change the timing error, only the reaction.
  • For ACT the chart shows the reaction distance against m. With m = 0.01 (ACT's code; i = 0 is the oldest prediction, so a positive m weights old predictions more) the executed action rests on predictions 57.7 steps old on average: the hand travels 122.6 mm before a change at the contact is acted on.

3. Apply

The recommendations:

  • Diffusion Policy: delay offset 200 ms (2 control steps): worst error 28.3 → 15.0 mm (the 10 Hz step limits it).
  • ACT: delay offset 40 ms (2 steps): 7.3 → 4.0 mm; ensemble weight m = −0.55: reaction 122.6 → 10.0 mm, the ensemble then averages 3.7 predictions in effect instead of 92.4.
  • π0: delay offset 100 ms (5 steps): 13.6 → 3.7 mm.

Preview draws the overshoot map as it would be after the offset. Apply stores the offsets and m on the three policies (one undo step; Edit ▸ Undo takes it back). The study runs again and reads them as the policies' configured values: the headline drops to 15.0 mm (Diffusion Policy, now "closes early" by up to 15 mm, "serious"). Export… writes the result with provenance.

In batch:

dexvantage --sample ur5e --action-chunk chunk.json

A policy whose inference time or control rate no source states is left out with a sentence (the SO-100 sample's SmolVLA: "its inference time is not stated by its source"); with no usable policy the run exits 2 with that sentence.
