Depth validity: a worked example

Where in the task volume does each depth camera give a depth reading, and why not elsewhere? This walk-through uses the UR5e + Robotiq 2F-85 sample, whose "Wrist D405" rides on the flange and whose "Overhead D435" hangs from the cage. The model and its checks are in docs/models/depth.md. The figures below are what this build computes on the sample at the home pose; they move with the robot's pose and your edits.

1. Open the sample and run the study

dexvantage --sample ur5e

Analysis ▸ Depth validity opens the page. Run passes the 3D view's joint state to the study (the sample opens at its home pose). In batch mode the same study runs at the home pose:

dexvantage --sample ur5e --depth-validity depth.json

The headline reads 84.6 %, good: that much of the 0.50 × 0.40 × 0.30 m tabletop volume has valid depth from at least one of the two cameras (good from 80 %, partial from 50 %: defaults, shown in the headline's tooltip). Per sensor: Wrist D405 45.8 %, Overhead D435 61.3 %.

2. Read the breakdown

With Sensor set to Wrist D405, the chart gives the first condition each voxel fails:

% of the task volume
Depth-valid 45.8
Outside a field of view 36.6
Occluded for an imager 17.6
Closer than min-Z, beyond the range, outside the projector cone, material 0.0

The D405 looks down at the gripper from 70 mm off the tool axis, so a third of the tabletop is simply outside its 84° × 58° depth field of view (both of its imagers must see a point). Of the occluded 17.6 %, the line under the chart splits off 7.2 % hidden from one imager only: the fingers' shadows and the stereo parallax, which a single camera model would miss. The D405 is passive (no projector), so condition 4 does not apply; the D435's projector cone does (Sensors table: "needed").

Tick the boxes under Invalid voxels in the 3D view to see the failing voxels coloured by reason (the field of view box is off by default, since those are most of the invalid voxels and the least surprising).

3. Read the min-Z blind zone

In the 3D view a translucent pyramid in each camera's colour sits in front of it: the part of its field of view nearer than min-Z, where the stereo matcher cannot reach the disparity. At the default resolution, 1280 × 720 (the D405's largest depth stream, labelled "(default)" in Resolution), its depth is 100 mm (Intel's Table 4-12). Move the arm (Robot page sliders, or IK drag) and the D405's zone moves with the wrist; the D435's stays put. View ▸ Depth min-Z zones hides them.

The status line under the chart answers the question a wrist camera raises: are the fingertips beyond min-Z? Here they are: "Fingertips beyond Wrist D405's min-Z (100 mm): gripper_right_pad 114 mm, gripper_left_pad 114 mm." A 14 mm margin.

4. Move the camera and watch it change

Select Wrist D405 (Outliner) and change its Position in the Inspector. The mount pose is in the wrist_3_link frame and the camera looks along (0, 0.873, −0.489) there, so moving along that axis keeps its aim.

30 mm closer, Position (0, 0.157, 0.055): the zone now swallows the finger pads. Run again:

  • "Inside Wrist D405's min-Z (100 mm): gripper_right_pad at 84 mm, gripper_left_pad at 84 mm. The fingertips have no depth there", a warning.
  • Depth-valid 41.4 % (more of the table is out of view from closer in).
  • The headline offers: "Set Wrist D405 to 848 × 480: its min-Z is 70 mm instead of 100 mm, which clears the fingertips at 84 mm (inside min-Z now); σ_z rises 1.51× (focal length about 471 px instead of 711 px)." Preview draws that zone dashed; Apply sets the resolution (one undo step) and runs again: the fingertips clear, the zone shrinks to 70 mm, and the median σ_z goes from 0.67 mm to 1.02 mm.

30 mm back instead (Edit ▸ Undo, then Position (0, 0.105, 0.085)): the fingertips sit at 144 mm, the zone is well clear of them, and depth-valid rises to 51.4 % (rig 86.2 %) because the wider view takes in more of the table. The cost is noise: σ_z median 0.86 mm instead of 0.77 mm, since σ_z grows with z².

5. Read the noise against the maker

The table Expected depth error against the maker's figure gives, for the D405 at 1280 × 720: f 710.8 px, baseline 18.0 mm, σ_d 0.08 px (default: Intel states no subpixel accuracy), σ_z median 0.77 mm [0.25–1.39] (5th–95th percentile over the depth-valid voxels), the model at Intel's distance, 1.6 mm at 0.5 m, against Intel's "≤ 5.0 mm (1 % at 0.5 m)", and the σ_d that would match Intel's figure, 0.256 px. Intel's figure is an upper bound on a plane-fit RMS over 80 % of the view under its own test conditions, so the two are not the same quantity; if you have measured your unit, put its σ_d in the settings (sigma_d_px) and the table follows it.

What to try next

  • Add a task object (Workspace ▸ Add box object) and tag it transparent or glossy (its materials list in the project; the Inspector shows the tags but has no field to change them yet): its surface shows as a material warning with the reason, and the IR-absorbing tag warns for the active D435 but not the passive D405.
  • Change the D435's resolution and compare its min-Z (280 mm at 1280 × 720, 105 mm at 424 × 240) with how far it is from the table.
  • dexvantage --validate out/validation --models depth runs the four depth checks.
