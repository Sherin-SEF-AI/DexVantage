Policies: a worked example on the UR5e sample

Name the policy your data is for, choose the cameras it takes, see what part of each image it uses, and replace a figure the source does not state with one you measured. The model, the library's sources and the validation are in Policies: what a robot-learning policy takes from each camera.

1. Add a policy

  1. File ▸ Open sample ▸ UR5e and Robotiq 2F-85 with a wrist D405 and an overhead D435.
  2. Open the Policies page (Tasks ▸ Policies, or Ctrl+K: "Policies"). The sample comes with one policy, "π0 (sample)"; to follow these steps from the start select it and press Remove. The headline then reads — · no policy.
  3. Pick π0 (pi0) · Physical Intelligence in the combo and press Add policy (one undo step). The table shows π0 (pi0) · all (2) · 224 × 224 px · 0: it takes every camera and depth sensor of the rig, its input is 224 × 224, and no figure the studies need is missing.
  4. Select it. The fields list each figure and its source: action chunk H 50 steps, "cited: K. Black et al., π0 …, arXiv:2410.24164, read 2026-11-06: "we use H=50 for our tasks"". Hover over a value for the full quote.

The headline now reads 0 not stated · camera count: π0 takes 3 images (a base camera and two wrist cameras) and the rig has 2. That is information, not an error: π0 masks a missing slot. Tell DexVantage so (step 3).

2. See what the policy sees

Select Overhead D435 and look at the Camera view while the Policies page is shown: the policy's input region is drawn on the image, labelled π0 (pi0) input 224 × 224 · resized with padding · 44 % padding. For π0 the whole 1920 × 1080 colour image is used and shrunk to 224 × 126 (0.117 input pixels per camera pixel), with black bars above and below. Add GR00T N1 2B instead and the rectangle shrinks to the centred 95 % crop (48, 27)–(1872, 1053), stretched to 224 × 224; Diffusion Policy draws its centred 81 % crop.

3. Enter your own figure

  1. Select the π0 row. In Override pick Cameras, enter 2 and the source this rig has two cameras; the third slot is masked. Press Set (one undo step). The field now reads 2 with "yours: measured by the user: this rig has two cameras; the third slot is masked", and its tooltip keeps the library's figure. The headline turns 0 not stated · all cited.
  2. Add π0-FAST: the headline reads 1 not stated · measure them, "control rate is not stated by its source" (the paper lists 5–50 Hz across its robots). Measure your controller's rate, enter it as an override with how you measured it, and the headline turns good again.
  3. A value that cannot be a figure (fast, 224) or a figure with no source is refused with the reason under the buttons; nothing changes. Clear brings back the library's figure. Ctrl+Z undoes each step.

4. Choose the cameras

Untick Every camera and depth sensor of the rig and tick the cameras the policy takes. The token and latency studies use these cameras; the Camera view draws the input region only on them.

5. Export

Export… writes every policy, its cameras and each resolved field with its source and origin (library, yours or not stated), with the project's provenance block, as JSON.

From code, the same figures:

from dexvantage.core import policies
policies.resolve(project, uid)['inference_ms']      # (73, 'K. Black et al., … read 2026-11-06: 73 ms on-board …')
policies.input_geometry(project, uid, (1920, 1080)).dst_rect   # (0.0, 49.0, 224.0, 175.0)
