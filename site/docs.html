DexVantage: models and methods

Every model DexVantage uses, written out with its equations, references and limits. Each phase adds its models here.

Frames and units

Units are SI. The world frame is the primary robot's base frame (REP-103: x forward, y left, z up). A camera's optical frame is REP-103's _optical_frame: z along the optical axis, x right, y down. For a camera link whose x axis is the optical axis, a point's optical coordinates are

p_opt = B · p_body,   B = [[0, −1, 0], [0, 0, −1], [1, 0, 0]].

Rotations are unit quaternions q = (x, y, z, w) with w ≥ 0 (one of the two signs, so equal rotations compare equal). The matrix is the standard one [Shoemake 1985]; the inverse uses Shepperd's method [Shepperd 1978], taking the largest of the four diagonal combinations so half turns stay accurate. URDF's rpy is R = R_z(yaw) · R_y(pitch) · R_x(roll) (fixed axes x, y, z). MuJoCo writes (w, x, y, z); the conversion happens at the boundary. The angle between two rotations is 2·atan2(|v|, |w|) of q₁⁻¹ q₂, which keeps full precision for small angles.

Validation: six checks against scipy's Rotation and MuJoCo's mju_quat2Mat (worst error 3 × 10⁻¹⁴). Known limits: rpy is singular at pitch ±90° (the yaw takes the whole turn there).

References (checks): mujoco_xml, rep103, scipy_rotation, shepperd1978, shoemake1985, urdf (full entries).

The project summary and the dense-voxel limit

A dense axis-aligned grid over a box of edges (a, b, c) at voxel size s has N = ⌈a/s⌉·⌈b/s⌉·⌈c/s⌉ voxels. At 1 mm, 1.5 × 1.5 × 1.2 m needs 2.7 × 10⁹ voxels, so the coverage studies (Phase 2) use 5 mm by default and refine to 1–2 mm only inside refinement regions. The summary reports N at the chosen size and at 1 mm, and marks a dense grid above 5 × 10⁷ voxels as not feasible (the limit is a documented default of this build, not a standard).

Robots

A URDF or MJCF robot is read with every file it names into the project and compiled by MuJoCo from memory; joint limits and the home pose are the file's own. The UR5e + Robotiq 2F-85, SO-100, UR5e + Shadow Hand, ALOHA 2, bimanual Panda and G1 samples are bundled MuJoCo Menagerie models, after their licences were read (the new samples). Robot kinematics

MuJoCo poses the links: T_link(q) from its tree of body transforms and joint motions, and the world Jacobian of a point from mj_jac. An independent product-of-exponentials chain checks it,

T(q) = e^[S₁]θ₁ · e^[S₂]θ₂ ⋯ e^[Sₙ]θₙ · M        [Lynch and Park 2017]

with its own XML reader and twist exponentials (core/robot/kinematics.py), and both engines are checked against the closed-form DH tables of the UR5e and the Panda: see Robot kinematics: forward kinematics and Jacobians.

Validation: 11 checks (checks_kinematics.py, checks_ik.py, checks_obstacles.py). Measured in the validation run of 2026-11-08 (239 of 239 checks pass): MuJoCo's forward kinematics against the product-of-exponentials chain 8.7 × 10⁻¹⁵ m (10⁻⁹ m); the UR5e and Panda DH tables 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ and 6.3 × 10⁻¹⁶ m (10⁻⁹ m); the analytic Jacobian against finite differences 4.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/rad (10⁻⁷) and against MuJoCo's mj_jac 8.8 × 10⁻¹⁵ m/rad (10⁻⁹); IK round trips 99.7 µm and, position only, 93.0 µm (100 µm); the 2R manipulability closed form 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶ m² (10⁻¹²); sphere and capsule distances 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁶ m (10⁻⁹ m); a moved and rotated obstacle 2.6 × 10⁻⁷ m (10⁻⁶ m). Known limits: free and ball joints are held at home and are not part of q; URDF mimic joints move independently; URDF planar joints and MJCF <replicate>, <attach>, <composite> and <flexcomp> are refused in a plain sentence; the DH checks validate the engines' conventions, not a vendor file's geometry (the bundled Menagerie UR5e's flange is up to 1.3 mm from the DH flange).

The hardware library

One JSON file per device (dexvantage/resources/library/<kind>/<id>.json, schema library.schema.json): depth cameras, cameras, tactile sensors and hosts, every figure copied from a cited source (URL or document, title, the date read, what it covers) and null where the source is silent, shown as "—" with the tooltip "not stated by the maker". An offset the maker does not give (an RGB imager's position in a D435, a projector's) is placed as a labelled assumption (pose_stated: false). library.sensor_imagers(sensor) turns a sensor's device into imagers: each a pose in the device body frame and a lens model fitted to the stated FOV at the chosen stream (a centred crop for other aspect ratios, a square-pixel pinhole from a diagonal-only FOV; both assumptions the imager's label says), or the unit's own calibration. A sensor with no device gets a labelled default camera (pinhole 1280 × 720 px, 90° × 60°). 40 entries in Phase 2; 138 hardware entries since Phases 9 and 10 (33 cameras, 33 depth cameras, 10 tactile sensors, 18 hosts and capture cards, 19 policies, 6 lights, 6 cables, 13 teleop devices in the new teleop kind) and 53 robots in the robot kind (the robot catalogue). Equations: the sensor geometry check uses w = p·W, h = p·H, d = p·√(W² + H²) (pixel pitch p, native resolution W × H) and the pinhole FOV 2·atan(p·N / 2f) of a stated focal length f; a ViT policy input of w × h px with patch p and token merge s gives (w/p)(h/p)/s² tokens. Validation: 8 checks (checks_library.py, checks_library_systems.py, checks_policies.py, checks_prices.py). Measured in the validation run of 2026-11-08 (239 of 239 checks pass): pixel pitch × resolution against the stated active area 1.2 % (2 %: sizes are rounded to 0.1 mm); stated FOVs within the bounds of a radially symmetric lens, 2.92° at most (3°), and against the pinhole FOV of the stated focal length 0.48° (3°); stated totals against count × the per-port figure 0.09 % (0.5 %); token grids exact; teleop blocks, prices and the rig cost 0 problems. Known limits: figures are as stated on the date read (a maker can revise them); a gap stays null, so a study cannot use that figure until the user measures it; an FOV for a stream the maker does not state is a centred crop, an assumption the imager says. See The hardware library: devices from datasheets (worked examples: The hardware library: a worked example, Systems from the library: hosts, policies, lights, cables and teleop devices).

Lens models, pixel density and defocus

CameraModel (pixels, OpenCV conventions: pixel centres at integers): pinhole; Brown-Conrady with 5 or 8 terms [Brown 1966]; Kannala-Brandt, OpenCV's fisheye θd = θ(1 + k₁θ² + k₂θ⁴ + k₃θ⁶ + k₄θ⁸) [Kannala and Brandt 2006]; equidistant, equisolid and stereographic. Every model has a valid angle beyond which it is not one-to-one, and points past it are never projected. Pixel density at a point is √|det J| of the image Jacobian over a patch facing the camera (f/Z on a pinhole's axis; × √cos α for a tilted surface), and defocus is the thin-lens blur disc b = A f |s − z| / (z (s − f)) [Ray 2002]. Ported from Vantage core/lens.py. See Optics: lens models, pixel density and defocus.

Validation: 10 checks (checks_optics.py, checks_optics_mujoco.py). Measured in the validation run of 2026-11-08 (239 of 239 checks pass): against OpenCV's projectPoints 1.8 × 10⁻¹² px and fisheye.projectPoints 5.7 × 10⁻¹³ px (10⁻⁶ px); project(unproject(pixel)) 4.7 × 10⁻¹³ px; FOV fits 2.3 × 10⁻¹² °; pixel density against √|det J| 8.2 × 10⁻⁹ relative (10⁻⁶) and the coverage study's figure against cv2.projectPoints differences 0.0065 (0.01); depth of field and the blur disc 2.9 × 10⁻¹³ and 1.1 × 10⁻¹³ relative; MuJoCo-rendered fiducials 0.075 px for mask centroids (0.1 px) and 0.17 px for centres (0.3 px). Known limits: thin lens only (no pupil magnification, diffraction, aberrations or focus breathing); no skew, thin-prism or tilt terms; a model fitted from a stated FOV reproduces the stated angles, and its distortion between them is a model until a calibration replaces it; the MuJoCo check covers pinhole cameras only (MuJoCo renders no distortion).

References (checks): brown1966, hartley2004, hecht2017, kannala_brandt2006, miyamoto1964, mujoco_camera, mujoco_renderer, opencv_calib3d, opencv_fisheye, ray2002 (full entries).

Occlusion by the posed robot

Around each imager's optical centre, a cube map of distances to the nearest occluder (the robots' visual meshes posed at q, the task objects, the tables): six 90° faces of n × n texels (default 512), built by a z-buffer on the GPU (EGL) or by an exact numpy rasteriser (the ray through each texel centre against each triangle; the depth is the exact plane distance along that ray). A point is visible when its distance is at most its texel's depth plus 1 mm (a default); near-plane clipping at 5 mm keeps a sensor from being blinded by its own seat. Exact ray casting (Möller-Trumbore over a uniform grid) is the independent reference. Ported from Vantage core/occlusion.py (the cube-map idea and face basis). See Occlusion: articulated cube maps and exact ray casting.

Equations: with the cube map centred at the optical centre c, a point p is visible when |p − c| ≤ D(texel(p)) + ε (ε = 1 mm, a default), D the texel's stored distance; a texel ray d meets the triangle (a₀, a₁, a₂) (c at the origin) at t = (a₀ · n) / (n · d), n = (a₁ − a₀) × (a₂ − a₀), kept when t ≥ the 5 mm near plane [Möller and Trumbore 1997]. Validation: 7 checks (checks_occlusion.py, checks_occlusion_mujoco.py, checks_camview.py, checks_coverage.py). Measured in the validation run of 2026-11-08 (239 of 239 checks pass): texels whose distance differs from the exact first hit by more than 1 mm 4.2 × 10⁻⁶ (10⁻³); against exact ray casting 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ of random points (10⁻³) and 0.18 % on the SO-100 and UR5e samples (1.45 %, the measured share of points within half a texel diagonal of a silhouette); MuJoCo segmentation of a box scene 0 and of the UR5e 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ of the target pixels away from silhouettes (10⁻³); the Camera view 1.4 × 10⁻⁵ (10⁻³); the coverage shares 8.8 × 10⁻⁴ of the volume (5 × 10⁻³). Known limits: one centre per map (a stereo pair needs one per imager); visibility is decided per texel (0.22° at n = 512), so a point within about a texel of a silhouette may go either way; MuJoCo's equality constraints (the 2F-85 linkage) are not applied; cables, cloth and sensor housings do not occlude.

References (checks): amanatides_woo1987, greene1986, moller1997, mujoco_camera, mujoco_renderer, mujoco_todorov2012 (full entries).

Sparse voxels

Each task volume is covered by cubic voxels of 5 mm (a default), split into k³ voxels of at most 2 mm (a default) inside refinement regions and around the task objects. A dense 1 mm grid over 1.5 × 1.5 × 1.2 m is 2.7 × 10⁹ voxels; a grid over 5 × 10⁷ voxels is refused with a message saying so. See Sparse voxels with adaptive refinement.

Coverage and resolution

For every voxel and camera: in the image (projected by the imager's lens model), visible (the cube map), pixels per millimetre, the viewing angle, the defocus blur where every lens figure is stated or entered, and redundancy (how many cameras see it). Shares are volume-weighted; the headline is the share seen by two or more cameras, with status thresholds 90 / 60 / 30 % (defaults). See Coverage and resolution in the task volume.

Depth validity

A voxel is depth-valid for a depth sensor when it is (1) inside every needed imager's FOV (both of a stereo pair), (2) beyond min-Z for the chosen resolution (the maker's table) and within the stated range, (3) seen by every needed imager (finger shadows and stereo parallax), (4) inside the projector cone where texture is needed, and (5) not on a surface tagged transparent, IR-absorbing or specular (a warning). The expected error is σ_z = z² σ_d / (f B) [Hartley and Zisserman 2004], σ_d = 0.08 px by default (labelled), shown beside the maker's RMS statement. See Depth validity.

Validation: 4 checks (checks_depth.py). Measured in the validation run of 2026-11-08 (239 of 239 checks pass): σ_z against a Monte Carlo of disparity noise 0.70 % relative (1.83 %, the Monte Carlo's band); the library's min-Z and FOV figures against Intel's D400 tables, 0 differ; the stereo-overlap formula against brute force 9.4 × 10⁻⁴ ° (0.01°); finger shadows against ray casting from both imagers 0 (10⁻³). Known limits: the voxel centre stands for the voxel; the projector's own shadow is not modelled (its position in a D4xx is a labelled assumption); Intel states the depth FOV at one distance, so the modelled overlap is narrower by 0.7–3.6°; min-Z is measured from the optical centre, not the glass; materials are tags, not optics; σ_z is first order and leaves out calibration error and matcher bias.

References (checks): amanatides_woo1987, gallup2008, greene1986, hartley2004, intel_d400, intel_d457, moller1997 (full entries).

Grasp sets

A grasp set (core/grasps.py, project.tasks.grasp_sets) is a list of gripper poses at contact, each with the direction the gripper moves in. It is sampled over a task volume (top-down pinch, side, angled, bimanual handoff), imported from CSV or JSON, or taken from a trajectory. The grasp centre and the gripper's approach and closing axes are measured from the contact pads at home, not typed in: the robot's tagged pads, else the inner faces of each fingertip's collision geometry (faces within 40 mm of the tip facing the other fingertips within 45°, not covered by the same finger). On the UR5e sample this gives the inner faces of the 2F-85's pads, 130.7 mm out along the gripper base's z axis. Validation: the pads against MuJoCo's compiled pad boxes 3.6 × 10⁻¹¹ m (10⁻⁶ m), the grasp centre against Menagerie's pinch site 0.0° (0.5°), the samplers against their definitions written out again 4.4 × 10⁻¹⁶, object surface points against a box's closed form 9.7 × 10⁻¹⁷ m. Known limits: samplers do not check reach or collisions (the study's IK does); object grasps use the object's axis-aligned box, so the width is an upper bound; dataset grasp sets wait for the Phase 8 readers. See Grasp sets.

Grasp self-occlusion

For every grasp of a set: batched damped-least-squares IK for the grasp centre (several seeds, joint-limit avoidance, self-collision and the project's obstacles; the rule nearest home or most manipulable; infeasible grasps counted by reason; pinocchio and cuRobo as optional backends, every solution re-checked by DexVantage's own FK). At each solution the contact patches ride with their links; each patch point, lifted 2 mm along its normal (a default: the inner faces of a parallel gripper face each other, so the faces themselves are blind from every side view), is visible from a camera when it projects inside the image through the lens model and nothing in the posed scene's cube map is nearer. Cube maps are cached by q-bucket (0.1°, a default). Per grasp, the requirement (default: one camera sees every fingertip at 50 % or more) holds or not; the Contact Visibility Score is CVS = k / n over the reachable grasps, with a percentile bootstrap 95 % interval [Efron 1979] (drawn as Binomial(n, p̂)/n, the exact distribution of the resampled mean) and Wilson's score interval [Wilson 1927] beside it. Pre-contact visibility over the last 5 cm, the both-fingertips rate, a worst-case gallery and "what to move" (each camera moved ±2 cm and turned ±5°, one at a time) complete the result. Performance (§16): measured 2026-11-06, 2 000 grasps on the UR5e sample in 111.6 s on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5080 (EGL device 0) and 131.3 s on the Ryzen 9 9900X's integrated Radeon, 17.9 and 15.2 grasps/s, 10 808 cube maps; 85.5 s with 256-texel maps, 83.6 s without the sensitivity sweep. Measured again at the Phase 11 gate (2026-11-08, RTX 5080, a loaded machine) on every sample: 114.2 s on the UR5e and 182.1 s on ALOHA 2, which misses this project's 180 s default (performance.md). Validation: visibility against exact ray casting of the full scene, 0 of 7 892 points away from an edge (10⁻³); the bootstrap's coverage against the known p of binomial samples, within 0.017 of 95 % (0.031); the q-bucket against evaluation at each grasp's own q, mean 0.0024 of a patch (0.02). Known limits: the fingers stay at their home opening; obstacles are not checked along the approach; the 2F-85's linkage joints are independent entries of q. See Grasp self-occlusion.

References (checks): amanatides_woo1987, efron1979, greene1986, lerobot_dataset_format, moller1997, mujoco_todorov2012, mujoco_xml, rep103, scipy_rotation, wilson1927 (full entries).

Policies

A project names the policies its data is for (core/policies.py, project.policies): a policy library entry (8: OpenVLA 7B, π0, π0-FAST, SmolVLA base, ACT, Diffusion Policy, Octo-Base, GR00T N1 2B), the cameras it takes and the user's own figures over the entry's. Every figure (input resolution, crop rule, patch, tokens per image, control rate, action horizon, inference time) is cited from the model card, the paper or the official code at a pinned commit, with a quote; a figure no source states is null and shown as not stated. The crop geometry maps a camera pixel (u, v) to the input, x_in = dst_x0 + (u + ½ − x0)·s_x, for six rules (resize with padding, centre crop then resize, plain resize, centre crop, top-left padding, and GR00T's fractional crop). Validation: each rule's pipeline written out again from its source code and run through OpenCV's resize, 2.5 × 10⁻¹³ px over 1.4 million input pixels (10⁻⁶ px). Known limits: which camera fills which slot (base or wrist) is not modelled; Diffusion Policy's step from a 16:9 stream to 320 × 240 is not stated and is modelled as a stretch, labelled. See Policies: what a robot-learning policy takes from each camera.

VLA token resolution and Plücker ray maps

Per policy and camera: the camera at its stream through its lens model, the policy's crop geometry, and a token grid of the stated patch (cols = ⌊w/p⌋, rows = ⌊h/p⌋), or a cell derived from tokens per image when that tiles the input on a square grid (ACT 32 px, GR00T 28 px, labelled derived). Each task object's and fingertip's triangles are projected (subdivided on distorting lenses) and rasterised at input resolution; the study counts the tokens they cover at the pre-grasp and contact poses of a grasp set and warns below 2 × 2 tokens (a default) or when more than half is cropped away. Plücker ray maps (o × d, d) in the robot base frame are exported per input pixel as .npy or .safetensors (ported from Vantage core/plucker.py; DexVantage's own safetensors writer). Validation: token counts against ray casting every input pixel's centre, 0 tokens (0); Plücker moments against OpenCV's undistortion, 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ (10⁻⁶); the safetensors round trip bit-exact. Known limits: silhouettes are not occlusion-tested (the grasp study does visibility); Octo's 128 × 128 wrist input is not applied per camera role. See VLA token resolution and Plücker ray maps.

Multi-finger occlusion

For a dexterous hand (the dexhand sample: the Shadow Hand on a UR5e, Apache-2.0 and BSD-3-Clause), over grasp postures swept between the hand file's own keyframes or over a grasp set: per fingertip patch point P and camera, the first hit along the ray to P and its owner (each finger, the palm, the arm, objects, the rest), d(P) = min_g d_g(P), seen ⇔ in view ∧ |P − o| ≤ d(P) + 1 mm. The finger × camera matrix gives the share of each patch not seen and what hides most; the mount comparison ranks a palm micro camera, a dorsal wrist camera and a head camera; "what to move" turns each camera ±10° about its optical centre (no ray casting needed). Validation: the matrix against a central projection written out in closed form, 0 (10⁻³); owners against all-pairs Möller–Trumbore, 0 (10⁻³); the union of group cube maps against one cube map, 2.0 × 10⁻⁸ m (10⁻⁶ m). Known limits: the postures are sample sweeps, not measured synergies; the Shadow Hand's tendon coupling is not enforced between keyframes. See Multi-finger occlusion.

Visual-to-tactile handoff (§7.15, part 2 of the same study, result key handoff). Along a straight approach of each contact case (the hand in its contact posture, moved back along the approach), the share of each fingertip's contact patch some camera sees is computed at coarse distances and refined inside every seen-to-hidden transition; at contact, the share a tactile pad's footprint covers. The sensory blind gap is the distance before contact over which a contact is neither seen nor touched (flagged above 10 mm, a default). On the dexhand sample every fingertip is seen up to contact (gap 0 mm), and no tactile sensor is mounted, so nothing is touched. Validation: the gap against a brute-force scan of every distance at the refine spacing, 0 m (2 mm). Known limits: the fingers do not close during the approach; touch is judged by geometry, not force. See Visual-to-tactile handoff and the sensory blind gap (spec §7.15).

Bandwidth, hosts and power

Each stream's payload is width × height × fps × bits per pixel / 10⁶ Mbit/s from the library's stream modes, on the wire × (1 + overhead), the overhead a labelled default of 5 % per interface; a stream whose maker states no bits per pixel has no rate until a measured one is entered. The host topology (project.rig: hosts, controllers, hubs, which sensor plugs where) gives each controller and link its load against its capacity (USB 2.0: 53.248 MB/s from Table 5-10; USB 3.2: 450 MB/s and 1.1 GB/s from §4.4.11, doubled for x2), the margin and flags (a controller over budget, a hub on a shared controller); disk per episode and campaign; bus power against USB 3.2 Table 3-1 (900 mA) and USB 2.0 §7.2.1 (500 mA). Ported from Vantage core/recording.py and core/power.py. Equations: payload = W × H × fps × bits per pixel / 10⁶ Mbit/s; on wire = payload × (1 + overhead); load = Σ on wire / capacity per controller and link; margin = capacity − Σ on wire; recorded = Σ payload × record ratio × (1 + container overhead). Validation: the NanEyeC's stated serial rates bound its payload (79 %); the USB figures against their derivations, 0; the budget re-computed independently over 40 random topologies, 3.9 × 10⁻¹⁶. Known limits: GMSL link rates and deserialiser power are not stated in the library; USB Type-C current levels and Power Delivery are not modelled. See Bandwidth, hosts and power.

References (checks): naneyec, usb20_spec, usb32_spec (full entries).

Cable flex across joints

A cable runs between attachments on links. Across one revolute joint it is a belt over a pulley: tangent, arc round a wrap cylinder of radius ρ (default the nearer attachment's distance from the axis), tangent, with the height difference unrolled, L = √(L_plane² + Δz²), L_plane = t_a + t_b + ρ(|θ| − α_a − α_b) when it wraps; the angle is not folded to half a turn, so a joint turned further winds the cable. Each crossed joint is swept over its limits and trajectories are played at 2°: slack, bend radius ρ/cos²φ (a helix [do Carmo 1976]) against the cable's stated minimum × a factor, pull, and snags (the slack loop through a link). A harness sheet lists cable, length, slack per joint, connectors and strain relief (ported from Vantage core/harness.py). Validation: the arc against the circle, helix and belt closed forms, 1.7 × 10⁻¹⁶ m (10⁻⁹ m); helix bend 4.9 × 10⁻⁵ (5 × 10⁻³); loop arc 4.4 × 10⁻⁴ (5 × 10⁻³); snags against brute force, 0 poses. Known limits: runs across several joints are straight chords; snags are tested at home and trajectory poses only, against the cable's own robot. See Cable flex across joints.

References (checks): amanatides_woo1987, docarmo1976, moller1997, scipy_brentq (full entries).

Brackets and mounts

Watertight, printable STL from signed-distance solids meshed by marching tetrahedra (ported from Vantage core/meshing.py and core/brackets.py): wrist-flange adapters (ISO 9409-1-50-4-M6 from UR's drawing; other sizes from the designation), 2020 and 4040 T-slot clamps, a pan-tilt neck plate, palm and dorsal sleeves for micro cameras; the camera's stated mounting holes where its datasheet gives them (RealSense D405, D435, D435i). Clearance: the bracket and camera body against every link but the mount link at home, on screen and 48 seeded joint states, and against cable paths when there are cables.
